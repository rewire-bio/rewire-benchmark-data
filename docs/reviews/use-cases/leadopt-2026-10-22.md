## Rulings on the nine questions in `coverage.json`

`coverage.json`'s `reviewer_must_judge` holds nine items, not the six in the review brief. All nine
are ruled on below: items 1 to 7 under their own headings, items 8 and 9 under "The two further

### 1. "target avg." headers against appendix D.2.2

**The source.** D.2.2 'Evaluation metrics' states: "All regression metrics are calculated per assay
and averaged across assays with weight proportional to the number of compounds in each assay,
ensuring that larger assays contribute proportionally to the overall performance summary." The
caption of Figure 10 says the same thing independently: it aggregates "by computing the per-assay
mean of the maximum similarity scores" and its dashed line "indicates the weighted average of the
per-assay Pearson correlations". So the source states its computation twice, and both times the unit
is the assay and the mean is compound-weighted.

Reading the rendered tables settles a second point the extractor did not separate: **"target avg."
is printed over three columns only.** In Tables 11, 12 and 14 the sub-label row reads `target avg.`
under Pearson R, Kendall tau and PMAE (Pairwise MAE in Table 14), and `non-cent. | cent.` under MAE,
Perc. within 1 and Perc. within 2. The six centring sub-columns carry no "target avg." label at all.

**Ruling.** The aggregation is what D.2.2 states. "Mean over targets" is not what the source says
anywhere: it is a reading of a column label, and it gets both the unit wrong (assay, not target) and
the weighting wrong (compound-weighted, not a plain mean). Two corrections follow, both applied.

- `b9c7eed` had set `comparison.aggregation` on 26 evaluations and `aggregation` on 3 protocols to
  "Mean over targets, as the columns are labelled ('target avg.'). Appendix D.2.2 states the
  computation: ...". That leads with an assertion the source contradicts, in the field that gates
  automatic comparison. **Corrected on all 29 records** to lead with D.2.2's computation and keep
  the printed label as a label: "Calculated per assay and averaged across assays with weight
  proportional to the number of compounds in each assay (appendix D.2.2, 'Evaluation metrics'). The
  Pearson R, Kendall tau and PMAE columns are printed under the label 'target avg.'; assays and
  targets are not the same unit, and the source does not reconcile the printed label with the stated
  computation."
- `b9c7eed` had also set every Boltz-2 result's `metric_qualifier` to begin "target average as
  printed ...", including the 156 results in the six columns where "target avg." is not printed.
  **Corrected on those 156 results** to "computed per assay and averaged across assays weighted by
  compound count". The 78 results in the three columns that do print "target avg." keep the wording,
  which is accurate for them.

**Effect on the relevance judgements.** D.2.2 means per assay, so the two Boltz-2 FEP-subset
judgements could not keep a rationale that called a printed value "the mean of per-series Pearson
values" without saying that reading an assay as a series is an inference. It is a defensible
inference: D.2.1 describes these subsets as "protein-ligand complexes from hit-to-lead and lead
optimization campaigns", and Ross et al. 2023, the benchmark's source, calls its contents "congeneric
series of small molecules". But the supplement never says one assay is one congeneric series.

**The `direct` calls stand, on corrected wording.** Per the brief, the wording changed and no value
did.

- `uc-leadopt-20261022-judgement-ross-fep-plus` (`direct`) needed nothing on this point. Ross's
  Table 3 legend states the unit of aggregation outright: "For the pairwise, R2, and Kendall tau
  metrics, the weighting is equal to the number of compounds in the assay (in the experimental
  survey) or FEP graph." An FEP graph is one congeneric series, and the paper describes its data set
  as congeneric series. The endpoint is the use case's own.
- `uc-leadopt-20261022-judgement-boltz2-fep-openfe-subset` and
  `uc-leadopt-20261022-judgement-boltz2-fep-4target` (both `direct`): **rationale corrected** to name
  the per-assay unit and to mark the assay-to-series step as an inference rather than the source's
  statement.

The extractor's own consistency is worth recording as sound: it kept the CASP16 judgement at `proxy`
because no source establishes that the chymase and autotaxin sets are congeneric series. The same
scepticism, applied to the FEP+ subsets, is answered by D.2.1 and by Ross; applied to CASP16 it is
not. The two calls are consistent with each other once the inference is stated, which it now is.

### 2. Unprinted units on PMAE and MAE

**Confirmed, no change.** D.2.2 states "All predictions are converted to kcal/mol prior to computing
the metrics", in the same paragraph that defines PMAE and MAE. The within-threshold metrics are

Recording those columns as `kilocalorie-per-mole` with the basis stated in `unit_detail` is right,

### 3. "Perc. within" recorded as `proportion` in `fraction`

**Confirmed, no change.** D.2.2 defines the columns as "the fraction of predictions with absolute
error below 1 and 2 kcal/mol". The printed values bear this out: across all three tables every value
in those four columns lies between 0.11 and 1.00, with 1.00 printed twice in Table 12. Nothing is on
a percentage scale. The `unit_detail` note that the header reads "Perc." is the right way to keep the
source's wording without adopting its scale.

### 4. Origin of the OpenFE, FEP+ and ABFE rows, and the CASP16 participant rows

**Confirmed, no change.** D.2.3 'Baselines' introduces these arms by citation: "we use our
recently-published ABFE protocol [Wu et al., 2025] as well as the relative FEP protocols of OpenFE
[Gowers et al., 2023] and FEP+ [Wang et al., 2015] for benchmarking", with FEP+ attributed to [Ross

The distinction the extractor drew within the physics arms is right and worth keeping: FMO, MM/PBSA
and Chemgauss4 are `author_reported` because D.2.3 describes running them in the first person, with

The six `LG*` CASP16 rows are `paper_compilation`, which is right: they are competition entries the
supplement tabulates, and it states nothing about what methods they used.

### 5. The experimental noise floor modelled as a `method`

**It stays. One correction applied.**

The modelling choice is the extractor's, not Ross's, and the brief is right that the risk is a
website presenting an assay repeat as a competing method that can be ranked. Against that:

- Ross et al. 2023 puts the two columns side by side in one table for exactly this purpose and
  states that they are commensurable: "The pairwise errors and correlation metrics of the
  experimental survey and FEP+ benchmark are directly comparable." Modelling the noise floor on its
  own protocol so it carries the same metrics is faithful to how the source presents it.
- The alternative, recording it only as prose, would leave the single most important number for this
  decision outside the evidence graph, where nothing links it to the metrics it bounds.
- The judgement is `proxy`, its rationale says the column "scores assay reproducibility, not any
  method's ability to rank analogues", and its first constraint is "Read these values as a ceiling on
  achievable accuracy, not as a method's score." The protocol and configuration records each carry
  the same caveat in their own `limitations`.

What was missing is the one the brief named: the `method` record's own description did not say it.
`limitations` is not an allowed attribute on a `method` in `shared/omics/attribute-registry.ts`, so
the caveat has to live in the description, which is the first text a reader sees on the record page.
**`uc-leadopt-20261022-method-experimental-reassay` description corrected** to end: "This is a
measurement of assay reproducibility, not a prediction method: it bounds the accuracy any predictor
could reach on this kind of data and cannot be ranked against predictors as though it were one." Its
`reference_kind` of `experimental_reference` and its `provenance_note` already carried the point and
are unchanged.

### 6. Gilson's three maxima

**They can coexist as recorded. No `evidence_concerns` entry. One limitation added.**

What the source says:

- Abstract: "The CASP affinity predictions showed modest correlation with experimental data (maximum
  Kendall's tau = 0.42), well below the theoretical maximum possible given experimental uncertainty
  (~0.73)."
- Section 3.3.1, body text: "This is well above the maximum achieved value of 0.39 (Figure 9A)."
- Section 3.3.1, two sentences later: "the maximum value of Kendall's tau observed here, about 0.4".

The body's 0.39 is traceable: Section 3.3.1 reports "Haiping (group 16) stands out (kappa_N = 0.39)"
as the best participant on the N-weighted Kendall tau. "About 0.4" is that same number rounded, in a
sentence comparing CASP16 with the D3R challenges. The abstract's 0.42 is reconciled nowhere, and
this review could not establish what it is: the paper reports no participant value of 0.42 in its
text. Figure 8A and Figure 9 are image-only in the pinned XML, so the figures cannot settle it.

A further inconsistency this review found and the batch did not record: Figure 8A is captioned as
the participants' N-weighted Kendall tau, while Figure 9 is captioned "N-weighted Kendall's tau for
each **baseline** affinity predictor", yet the body cites Figure 9A for "the maximum achieved value
of 0.39", which is the participant maximum discussed under Figure 8A. **Added to the Gilson source's
`limitations`**, stated as what could not be settled rather than as a conclusion, because the figures
are not readable in the pinned artifact.

Why no `evidence_concerns` entry, which `docs/collection.md` names for "abstract and body disagree":

- The mechanism exists to stop a source supporting comparison. Gilson supports no comparison here.
  It contributes no `result` record and no evaluation; its affinity results are figure-only and the
  batch correctly extracted none of them. Only six descriptive claims cite it.
- The disagreement is already disclosed at every point of use. The claim's own `value` names all
  three numbers and says the body gives 0.39 and "about 0.4"; the source's `limitations` state that
  the source does not reconcile them; and the use-case `summary` repeats the point and adds the
  sharper one, that Gilson's 0.42 and the 0.42 printed for group LG016 in Boltz-2 Table 14 "are not
  established to be the same quantity".
- Adding the entry would not qualify the number, it would delete the page. `cleanSource` in
  `shared/omics/use-cases.ts:231` treats any source carrying `evidence_concerns` as unusable, and
  line 649 blocks every positive judgement when "a source the use case cites is unchecked or has
  evidence concerns". The use-case record lists `gilson2025` among its `source_ids`, so the entry
  would withhold all five judgements and the summary, suppressing the fully verified Ross and
  Boltz-2 evidence because a third source rounds one number three ways. That trades a correct,
  disclosed caveat for a blank page.

The extraction agent reached the same conclusion for the same reason; this review confirms it
against the code rather than on the strength of the assertion.

## Questions raised after the first pass

### Should the Boltz-2 records also cite `expansion-p3-boltz2`?

**No. The supplement alone is right, no change.** `docs/record-contract.md` defines `source_ids` as
"IDs of source records supporting this entity". Every Boltz-2 record in this batch has a
`source_locator` pointing into the supplement, and nothing else: the results and evaluations into
Tables 11, 12 and 14; `config-boltz2-affinity` into "Tables 11, 12 and 14, row 'Boltz-2'; appendix
D.2.2"; `config-boltz2-iptm` into "Tables 11, 12 and 14, row 'Boltz-2 iptm'". The existing
`expansion-p3-boltz2` snapshot holds the main text, figure captions and Table 1 only, so it supports

opposite of what the field is for. It belongs on records whose values come from the main text, and
this batch has none.

### Figure 10's bin sizes summing to 867 against the OpenFE subset's 876

**Checked against the pinned text layer. Not an established discrepancy, and no `evidence_concerns`
entry.** The bin labels are printed text at lines 1503 to 1504 and read `N=289`, `N=288`, `N=108`,
`N=182`, which does sum to 867 against the 876 complexes appendix D.2.1 gives for the OpenFE subset.
Three things stop that being a finding.

1. The caption never says what `N` counts. It says the values are aggregated "by computing the
   per-assay mean of the maximum similarity scores", that the figure groups "the resulting **assays**
   into similarity bins", and that each violin shows "the distribution of per-assay Pearson
   correlation coefficients". The binned unit is the assay, so an `N` of 289 is not obviously a
   complex count; and it cannot be an assay count either, since 876 complexes cannot yield 867
   assays with more than one compound each. The label is undefined in the source.
2. The populations are not stated to be the same. D.2.1 introduces Figure 10 as an analysis "for the
   FEP+ benchmark", while 876 is the size of "the OpenFE subset of the FEP+ benchmark". The
   supplement never says the figure covers that subset rather than the benchmark as a whole.
3. Nothing in this batch depends on it. No result is extracted from Figure 10, and the figure is
   image-only apart from these labels.

Asserting a 9-complex shortfall would require establishing that two numbers denominate the same
thing, which the source does not support. Recording it as an evidence concern would also be a
disproportionate response: it would withhold every Boltz-2 record in the batch through `cleanSource`,
on the strength of an arithmetic near-miss between an undefined label and a differently scoped count.
Written up here so a later pass does not spend the work re-finding it.

### Where the "mean over targets" wording came from

the printed header first was not the extraction agent's reading of the source: it came from an

### Two schema defects found after the batch was added

`npm run records -- add` accepted the batch and the SHACL shapes passed, but `npm run test:fast`
then failed 16 tests on two defects that `add` does not check. Both would have withheld the entire
use case, so they are recorded here rather than left for the build.

- **Every relevance judgement lacked `reason`.** `mappingSchema` in `shared/omics/use-cases.ts`
  requires a non-empty string and the batch set none, so all five mappings failed to parse. A
  `reason` naming what each judgement adds was written for each.
- **`planned_work` held plain strings.** The schema requires `{title, url, status, reason}` objects

  its `reason` and the URL pointed at issue #178.

A third, smaller defect was this review's own: the review blocks carried both `date` and
`reviewed_at`, and `normalizeAttributes` drops `date` when `reviewed_at` already carries it, so the
stored records were not in canonical form and `tests/attributes.test.ts` failed. `date` was removed
from all 369 review blocks.

All three were applied to the store and to `batch.jsonl`, recorded with
`npm run records -- change` against this document, and the receipt's `batch.jsonl` hash updated to
match the corrected bytes. The receipt was not regenerated to clear a failure: each change is listed
in its `corrections` block with what was wrong.

One ordering trap, recorded for whoever runs the next review. `npm run use-cases:repin` must be the
last step, after every record edit. Judgement pins hash over the protocol, evaluation and source
records they cite, so editing a protocol after pinning leaves the pins stale, and
`tests/use-case-records.test.ts` fails on "stores pins that match the store for every reviewed
judgement" rather than on anything about the edit. That happened here when the protocols gained the
target-versus-assay limitation after the first repin; re-running repin cleared it.

The extraction script was deliberately not re-run. Re-running it would have checked the batch against
the extractor's own parser; the parser written for this review is independent of it, which is the
stronger check, and not running it also kept a `__pycache__` out of the folder the receipt hashes.

## Conflicts found

1. The batch changed under review (`b9c7eed` landing mid-pass). Resolved by freezing the folder and
   reviewing `b9c7eed`.
2. `extract/extract_leadopt.py` no longer rebuilds `batch.jsonl`: it reproduces the batch as
   extracted, not as reviewed. The script was deliberately not edited, so that what the extractor
   wrote and what the review changed stay separable. The receipt and the provenance hashes are the
   record of the reviewed bytes.
3. `coverage.json` keeps the extractor's `reviewer_must_judge` block, including its position that the
   columns are a target average and that the FEP-subset judgements are `direct` on per-series
   grounds. It is left as the extractor's record of what it asked; this document is the answer.
4. Boltz-2 Table 14 prints Kendall tau 0.42 for group LG016, and Gilson's abstract gives 0.42 as the
   maximum participant Kendall tau. The two are not established to be the same quantity: the
   assessors weight and skip-penalise on their own conventions. The summary claim already says so and
   no record treats them as equal.
5. The Gilson source record's ID says `gilson2025` while the paper carries a January 2026 cover date
   and the batch's prose calls it Gilson et al. 2026. Cosmetic, and record IDs are never edited. Noted
   so a later reader is not misled.

## Remaining gaps

The gaps the extraction pass recorded all stand and are not repeated here. The ones this review adds
or sharpens:

- No per-series value exists in this batch for any arm, so nothing here answers "would this method
  have ranked my series correctly". This is the use case's central gap and it is recorded as such.
- Whether one assay in the Boltz-2 FEP+ subsets is one congeneric series is an inference from D.2.1
  and from Ross, not a statement of the supplement. Confirming it needs Hahn et al. 2022 or the
  benchmark repository, neither of which was retrieved.
- The abstract's maximum Kendall tau of 0.42 in Gilson could not be reconciled with the body's 0.39,
  and the figures that would settle it are not readable in the pinned XML. The official
  predictioncenter.org per-target results file was not downloaded in this pass and is the route.
- Boltz-2 Tables 13, 15 and 16 remain unextracted, by scope. Table 16's blinded Recursion assays are
  the only place the supplement reports across-target spread (Pearson R 0.165 to 0.634, centred MAE
  0.855 to 1.734 kcal/mol), which bears directly on the spread this use case says is missing, even
  though the assays are hit-to-lead. Worth revisiting.
- This review checked transcription. It did not re-derive any metric from underlying data, and no
  result in this batch carries a `derivation`.
