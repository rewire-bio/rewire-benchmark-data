## Decisions

**GEL blood RNA-seq judgement: `proxy`.** The use case's inputs are "Patient skin-fibroblast (or matched tissue) RNA-seq aligned reads" and a FRASER-style cohort size; its output is a known-event recovery curve, with sequence classification kept as "a separate sequence-classification proxy". SpliceAI, Pangolin, AlphaGenome and SpliceAI2 take DNA variants, not patient RNA-seq. The Drost et al. 2025 judgements are proxy for this reason ("the predictors take DNA variants rather than patient RNA-seq, the use case's output keeps sequence classification as a separate proxy"). The concrete differences here, truth from RNA-seq in the same participants rather than RT-PCR, and no ascertainment through another predictor, improve the truth set but do not change what the methods take as input or produce. Silverstein 2026 is `direct` because its callers take patient RNA-seq. Consistent with both, this stays `proxy`. The prioritised-variant judgement (17 of 23) is also `proxy`: one tool, no comparator, and confirmation of a splicing change rather than recovery of known pathogenic events.

**Training overlap: limitation, not an evidence concern.** `evidence_concerns` is for problems that should block comparison, such as conflicting tables or unclear units. The source is clear about its split (chromosomes 21 and 22 held out for validation; benchmarks genome-wide), and the overlap is stated on every protocol and judgement. It does not affect the GEL judgement's truth labels: Genomics England RNA-seq is not a training source. For the GTEx benchmarks the risk is larger, because GTEx is in the recount3 training data and the paper does not say whether the benchmark junctions were removed; those protocols are not mapped to any use case. Blocking would withhold all three judgements, including the two whose truth data are not in training, for a caveat already shown with them.

**OpenSplice comparator scores.** The OpenSplice Methods paragraph says "SpliceAI, Pangolin, and AlphaGenome scores released with the OpenSplice dataset". The AlphaGenome paragraph says the composite score was best "across the eight evaluations" and was used "for all evaluations". The paper has exactly eight other analyses with Oxford-scored AlphaGenome (four GTEx panels in Fig. 1d, Figs. 3c, 3f, 4b and 4g), so the count is consistent with OpenSplice being excluded, and I read the OpenSplice comparator scores as the released ones. The released scores' settings are not described in this paper. This is recorded as a limitation and constraint on the protocol and judgement, not an evidence concern: the paper is explicit about using released scores, and the remaining question is about settings, which the judgement already flags.

**Missing motif-scoring baseline and access terms.** Recorded. The GEL GT/AG protocol and judgement say no rule-based motif scorer (for example MaxEntScan) is evaluated, citing #121; the OpenSplice judgement says MaxEntScan is not evaluated; `coverage.json` lists it as the first remaining gap. Access terms are on the dataset records (`reuse_restrictions`): GTEx dbGaP phs000424.v9.p2, Genomics England NGRL project 754 inside the Research Environment, UK Biobank application 33751. The licence claim records the SpliceAI2 Model Terms of Use.

**Summaries: updated now.** Both summaries describe every linked comparison, so leaving SpliceAI2 out would make them incomplete once the judgements are active. I wrote the added sentences from the extractor's suggestions and checked them per review-evidence step 4:

- Patient RNA: 5,435, 3,065, 1,775,800, auPRC 0.59, 0.48, 0.40, 0.36 and 0.45, and 23 and 17 each match a stored result or the protocol and dataset records. The text names the authors as the SpliceAI2 developers (author-reported), states the truth-set bias (only splicing visible in blood can be confirmed, so nulls may splice in other tissues), the training overlap, that no other tool went through prioritisation, and that no rule-based scorer was compared. No recommendation.
- Splicing follow-up: the opening now says a fourth comparison is reported by a tool's developers. 283,121, 599, and 0.59, 0.55, 0.53 and 0.49 match the "All" OpenSplice results. The text says the comparator scores were released with OpenSplice and that a rank correlation over all substitutions is not precision among selected variants. No recommendation.

The earlier text of both summaries is unchanged. Both are repinned.

`use-case-links.json` is applied: `use-case-patient-rna-splicing-validation` gains `assessed_by` links to the GEL GT/AG and GEL prioritised protocols, `use-case-splicing-follow-up` to the OpenSplice protocol.

## Remaining gaps

- No baseline (MaxEntScan, legacy splice-site vote, FRASER or OUTRIDER) is compared with a machine-learning method in this paper (#121).
- Fig. 4f validation rates and the Fig. 4b comparator curves have no printed values.
- Extended Data Fig. 2 stratum sizes and all uncertainties are unprinted.
- Benchmark variant lists are not released and all data are controlled access, so no comparison can be rerun outside the data holders' environments.
- The OpenSplice preprint's own comparator settings were not screened.
- No independent evaluation of SpliceAI2 exists yet.
- `use-case-patient-rna-splicing-validation` `evidence_checked` was not changed: this was a targeted source pass, not a use-case literature pass.
