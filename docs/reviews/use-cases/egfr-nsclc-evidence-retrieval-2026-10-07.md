## Endpoint boundary

The endpoint is retrieval of correctly scoped sensitivity/resistance evidence for advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC: source-linked assertions with regimen, direction, treatment setting, native evidence tier, contradictions and unresolved information, conditioned on biomarker, prior therapy, line/stage, sampling source, jurisdiction and evidence cutoff. This is distinguished from treatment recommendation, response/survival, trial eligibility, somatic oncogenicity classification, and longitudinal resistance prediction.

## 1. FLAURA first-line osimertinib resistance analysis

Chmielecki J, Gray JE, Cheng Y, et al. *Nat Commun* 2023;14:1070. doi:10.1038/s41467-023-35961-y (PMC9971254, open access). Ramalingam SS is a co-author (last-listed), not the first/lead author.

**Population chain, as reported in Results:** 556 randomised (279 osimertinib / 277 comparator EGFR-TKI). Paired baseline+progression/discontinuation plasma NGS samples: 137/279 (49%) osimertinib, 179/277 (65%) comparator — 316 patients with paired plasma. Of these 316, 254 (80%) had baseline detectable plasma EGFRm (Ex19del/L858R) and form the acquired-resistance analysis subset: 109/137 (80%) osimertinib arm, 145/179 (81%) comparator arm.

**Resistance-mechanism frequencies reported for this subset** (query-source context for a future benchmark corpus; not a retrieval-quality measurement in themselves): osimertinib arm (n=109) — 38 (35%) with a detected mechanism, 71 (65%) with none detected, no EGFR T790M, MET amplification n=17 (16%), EGFR mutations n=11 (10%, including C797S n=7/6%), multiple mechanisms in 15 (14%). Comparator arm (n=145) — 71 (49%) with a detected mechanism, EGFR T790M n=64 (44%), MET amplification n=9 (6%), CDK6 amplification n=6 (4%).

**Author Correction** — *Nat Commun* 2023;14:3179, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-38999-0 (PMC10235017), published 2023-06-01. It makes three edits: (1) the Figure 3 legend, originally mislabelled as the comparator-arm swimmer plot, is corrected to the osimertinib-arm plot (n=109 total, n=38 with a detected mechanism), with a companion addition to the Supplementary Figure 1 legend (the comparator-arm plot) giving its previously-missing mutation count (n=145 total, n=71 with detected resistance mutation); (2) a Discussion cross-study comparison to AURA3's MET-amplification rate is changed from "19%" to "18%"; (3) a Discussion sentence attributing a 14% C797S rate to "Phase I AURA (n=6) and Phase III AURA3" is changed to attribute it to Phase III AURA3 only, removing the Phase I AURA reference. A data point for one patient with an acquired BRAF D594N mutation, already shown in Fig. 2B, is added to the corrected Supplementary Fig. 1. The corrected Results and Discussion, as they now stand, report the population chain and resistance-mechanism frequencies above; the correction text itself does not enumerate which other reported values it leaves unchanged, so no inference is drawn beyond the three edits and one added data point actually specified.

This primary source is open-access via PMC. Extraction into a benchmark query/answer pair and independent molecular-oncology relevance adjudication are not performed in this pass.

**Task-schema change, v2→v3:** v2 used a SUPPORTS/REFUTES/NEI claim-verification label space. v3's current benchmark uses **SUPPORTS/REVISIONS/NEI** (REFUTES removed; a REVISIONS label is added, with an optional candidate corrected value for curator review). v3's own text states that its historical inter-annotator-agreement analysis (Fleiss' κ 0.65, 77.9% agreement) was conducted under the predecessor SUPPORTS/REFUTES/NEI formulation and "does not directly validate the current SUPPORTS/REVISIONS/NEI task." v2's claim-verification accuracy figures (89% gold-evidence, 85% retrieved-evidence, both under SUPPORTS/REFUTES/NEI) are v2-specific, historical artifacts of a label space the current (v3) benchmark no longer uses, and are not carried forward as current figures.

**Static dataset-scale counts, disposition only (not ingested):** v3's Methods prose states passage-retrieval evaluation-subset figures (n_train=3456, n_dev=1194, n_test=1120) that are not the same counts as v3's own Table 2, which separately reports an "Evidence Retrieval" row of CIViC-entry counts (train=907, dev=99, test=88) alongside a differently-scoped claim-verification row. These are different counts/units from different parts of the same source and were not reconciled for this bounded intake. Neither set of numbers is the denominator for the post-cutoff 37/40 result reported below — that result has its own, separately stated 150→66→42→40 selection chain. None of these static Methods/Table 2 counts is ingested into the catalogue.

**Passage retrieval remains scoped within the already-linked source paper**, confirmed again in v3 Methods: the retrieval task is to rank passages drawn from the one paper CIViC already links to each claim, not to search an open corpus for a matching study.

**Post-cutoff temporal-evaluation cohort — selection chain (Methods, "Temporal evaluation"; Supplementary Figure 2, printed p.33 / PDF p.34) and retrieval result (Results, "Passage Retrieval Models Perform Well without Fine-Tuning", printed p.14 / PDF p.15):** "Temporal evaluation" is the Methods heading that defines the selection chain only; the 37/40 retrieval figure itself is reported under the Results subsection named above, not under a "Temporal evaluation" Results heading. CIViC entries submitted or modified 2026-03-03 to 2026-06-09 were selected to probe curation drift after the dataset's last build. 150 such entries were identified; 68 were excluded for missing full-text access, 14 were excluded as derived from documents already present in the CIViC-Fact dataset, and 2 were excluded for non-content data errors (e.g. a malformed disease record), leaving **66** entries curator-evaluated for reference labels. Of these 66, **24** were labelled NEI because verification required material outside the main text (images or supplementary data), leaving **42** entries for which full-text access was available and the claim did not require supplementary/image material. Of these 42, **2** were labelled NEI because the submitted claim had been substantially revised from the original (a re-used entry rather than a newly entered one), leaving **40** scored entries. Both the fine-tuned MedCPT retriever and the 8B Qwen3-Reranker retrieved appropriate content, on manual review, for **37 of these 40 (92.5%)**; the fine-tuned MedCPT model was used downstream for its lower resource requirement (single-GPU, non-generative). The source's Methods separately describe a curator, with group-consensus discussion for ambiguous cases, assigning the cohort's reference labels — that process description does not by itself specify the reviewer count, blinding, or replicate structure for the retrieval-success judgment, and none of those is assumed here; they are recorded as unreported. These 37/40 results are retrieval of passages within each entry's already-linked publication, not retrieval of an open EGFR-NSCLC study from an unconstrained corpus, and this cohort carries no EGFR or NSCLC-specific tag or subgroup in the text read. The exact Huggingface model identifiers (`ncbi/MedCPT-Cross-Encoder`; `Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-8B`) are confirmed in Table 4; these are namespace identifiers, not pinned immutable checkpoint revisions, and the source does not report one.
