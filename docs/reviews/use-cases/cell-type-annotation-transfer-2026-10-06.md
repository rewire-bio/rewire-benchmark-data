## Revision note, 2026-10-06 (this pass)

This revises the first-pass version of this dossier after an independent source check by Codex, which fetched the same primary XML files into `workbench/source-review-20261006/` (gitignored, not committed) and found five classes of problem. I re-verified every one directly against the XML myself before correcting; the corrections and their verification are below, not taken on trust from the check alone.

1. **scTab "single-study" mischaracterization.** The scTab corpus is explicitly stated in the paper (Fig. 1a legend and Methods) to span **249 datasets**, not one study. The held-out evaluation is a **donor-level** split within that pooled multi-dataset corpus, which the Methods text explicitly contrasts with (and chose over) a study-level holdout split. "Single-study" was wrong; corrected throughout to describe the corpus and the split precisely, and to state plainly what is and is not established about dataset/study overlap between train and test (see below). I also softened an overclaim that the Introduction's conceptual contrast with "annotation transfer" approaches "confirms... rather than infers from silence" that cross-study evaluation is absent from the paper — a paper's framing of its own contribution cannot itself demonstrate an absence; the actual basis for that absence claim is a targeted keyword search of the Results/Methods text, which is now stated as such.
2. **Hao et al. 2021 (Azimuth) STAR Methods wrongly marked "not retrieved."** The same full-text XML I already had contains the complete STAR★Methods section; I had not searched far enough into the 378 KB file to find it. Reading it now: it states **35,619 concordant cells (67.1%) and 17,480 discordant cells (32.9%)**, so the total query-cell denominator (**53,099**) is an exact sum of two source-printed counts, not an inversion of a rounded percentage as I had done before. It also surfaces a genuine **within-source numeric conflict**: the Figure S7D legend states "In 73.8% of cases, we observe stronger support for the Seurat annotation," while the STAR Methods "Quantification and statistical analysis" text, describing what reads as the identical comparison, states "In 79.4% of cases, we observe stronger support for the Seurat annotation (Figure S7E)" — but Figure S7E's own legend (quoted in the matrix below) is about computing time, not protein support. This is reported below as an unresolved conflict in the published source; I am not choosing one number over the other.
3. **scArches "calibrated" threshold.** The word "calibrat[ion/ed]" does not appear anywhere in the retrieved scArches full text. The source reports a **fixed cutoff (uncertainty > 50% → "unknown")** with no reliability diagram, binning, or calibration-error metric offered to support that this cutoff corresponds to a validated error rate. "Calibrated abstention" was my own unsupported framing, not the source's; corrected to "fixed-threshold abstention" throughout, including in the duplicate-check discussion of rewire-benchmarks B349.

**License, verified directly against the XML (not assumed from journal policy).** Quoted verbatim: *"Open Access This article is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License, which permits use, sharing, adaptation, distribution and reproduction in any medium or format..."* © The Author(s) 2024. **Correction/addition to the existing evidence_gaps note.** The relevant numeric values are not confined to a supplementary figure; the main article's Methods section states them in running text. Methods, subsection "Uncertainty quantification for scTab model": the paper splits held-out cells into Group 1 (correct, in-distribution), Group 2 (incorrect, in-distribution) and Group 3 ("Absent in Training Data" — cell types excluded from training for having too few observations, used to simulate unknown-type detection), then reports deep-ensemble uncertainty-score separability by ROC-AUC: *"Group 1 and Group 3 can be separated with an ROC-AUC score of 0.782 and Group 1 and Group 2 can even be separated with an ROC-AUC score of 0.891"* (quoted verbatim, Methods, "Uncertainty quantification for scTab model," last paragraph before "Statistics and reproducibility"; visualized in Supp. Fig. 4d). This is source-reported, not a derivation. **These are two distinct comparisons, and only one of them is unknown-type detection: AUC 0.782 is Group 1 (correct, known-type) vs. Group 3 (absent-type, never seen in training) — this is the unknown/absent-type detection figure. AUC 0.891 is Group 1 (correct, known-type) vs. Group 2 (incorrect, known-type) — this is a separate known-type error-detection figure: whether the same uncertainty score flags the model's own mistakes among types it was trained on, not whether it detects types absent from training.** An earlier version of this dossier described both numbers together as "unknown/absent-type rejection values"; that conflation is corrected throughout below.

**Corpus and split, precisely (corrected).** Fig. 1a legend and surrounding text, quoted verbatim: *"The dataset spans 22.2 million cells, 5,052 donors, 249 datasets, 164 cell types, and 56 different tissues."* This is a **pooled, multi-dataset (249-dataset) corpus**, not one study. The Methods subsection on data splitting states, quoted verbatim: *"the data is split into train, validation, and test sets based on donors and not based on random subsampling. Meaning, each donor is exclusively found either in the training, validation, or test set... In the end, the data is split such that 70% of the donors are assigned to the training set and 15% of the donors are assigned to the validation and test set respectively."* Earlier in the Methods, the paper explicitly justifies this choice over a dataset/study-level holdout: *"We defined test holdouts based on donor annotation, which we see as a sensible compromise between an entirely random split and a split based on holdout studies. The donor-wise split improves the coverage of labels in both training and test sets compared to a split based on studies, and reduces leakage of similar observations between training and test data compared to a random split of cells."*

**What this does and does not establish about study overlap (precise, not inferred from silence).** The quoted rationale — that a study/dataset-level holdout was considered and rejected specifically because it would unevenly distribute cells and reduce per-split label coverage — is consistent with donors from the same underlying dataset appearing in more than one split. But I did not find, in the retrieved main-text XML, any directly stated count, table, or cross-tabulation confirming how many of the 249 datasets contribute donors to more than one split, nor any explicit sentence stating that this occurs. **This is therefore reported as strongly implied by the stated splitting rationale, not as a directly confirmed fact.** Resolving it exactly would require the dataset-ID-to-split manifest, which is part of the "checkpoint and split hashes unextracted" item already in the existing `evidence_gaps`; I did not locate or re-derive that manifest in this pass. Accordingly: scTab's evaluation is **donor-held-out within a pooled 249-dataset corpus, with likely but unconfirmed dataset/study overlap between splits** — this is a materially different (weaker, for the cross-study-transfer question) claim than either "single-study" (which I wrote before and was wrong) or "whole-study holdout" (which the paper explicitly did not do).

I also checked the scTab paper's own Discussion and Introduction for cross-study claims: it conceptually contrasts itself with the "annotation transfer" paradigm (projecting a query onto a reference atlas) rather than evaluating under that paradigm. **Corrected framing:** this conceptual contrast does not itself demonstrate that scTab's paper omits a cross-study/cross-platform validation; the basis for that claim is a targeted keyword search of the Results and Methods text (`generaliz`, `transfer`, `independent data`, `another dataset`, `new study`, `other studies`, `batch effect`, `different study`) that returned no cross-study or external-validation result. That search is necessarily bounded by the keywords chosen and by what Europe PMC's XML mirror includes; it is reported as a search finding, not a logical proof of absence.

## Decision-endpoint assessment

The use case's own `collection_plan.comparison_question` asks whether a method improves known-type annotation **and** unknown-type recognition **at matched coverage**, **across independent studies**, relative to conventional methods. None of the seven sources in this matrix, taken alone, answers that compound question for any one method:

- scTab answers the unknown-type half within one donor-holdout split of its pooled 249-dataset corpus (ROC-AUC 0.782), and the known-type half within the same split; cross-study/cross-platform transfer of either is not established, and dataset-overlap between splits is unconfirmed either way.
- Abdelaal et al. 2019 runs genuine cross-platform (pancreatic, Fig. 5) and cross-study (brain, Fig. 4) inter-dataset experiments, but the only precisely quotable rejection-option numbers (median F1, % unlabeled) are from its separate **intra-dataset** setup (same-dataset stratified 5-fold CV, Fig. 1); the inter-dataset experiments are reported in the retrieved text only as qualitative classifier rankings, with no numeric rejection percentage located for them. So the cross-platform half is answered qualitatively, not with a quotable rejection-option number, and only for pre-2019 conventional classifiers, not for scTab, CellTypist, Azimuth, scANVI, or scParadise.
- Hao et al. 2021 (Azimuth) gives a genuine cross-study result, but as a relative concordance-support figure (73.8% or 79.4%, unresolved which) in a disagreement subset, not an absolute accuracy or calibrated-rejection figure; its unknown-type evidence is qualitative.
- Xu et al. 2021 (scANVI) gives a genuine absent-type cross-dataset design, but scores it with integration-geometry metrics this use case's own exclusions rule out as establishing cell identity; its one classification-accuracy figure (23–37%) is single-dataset.
- Lotfollahi et al. 2022 (scArches) comes closest: a genuine cross-study/cross-technology transfer with a whole-tissue holdout and an explicit **fixed-threshold** (not demonstrated-calibrated) abstention rule, headline ~84% accuracy — but its own method-comparator figure (Supp. Fig. 17) excludes the unseen-tissue cells, so it does not compare rejection behaviour across methods.
- Domínguez Conde et al. 2022 (CellTypist) gives only an internal ~0.9 F1 figure in the accessible main text; the material needed to assess its cross-study validation and rejection mechanism is inaccessible in this pass.
- scParadise (2026) runs the closest thing to a matched, same-protocol, 7-method comparator panel found in this search (exactly the shape B349 asks for) — but that panel holds out non-overlapping samples/donors within the same published atlas, not a cross-study or cross-platform test; its genuinely cross-study demonstrations are qualitative; and its unknown-type detector uses an automatic (Otsu) but still non-calibrated threshold with no quantified detector accuracy located.

Consistent with the existing `evidence_gaps` on file and the 2026-09-30 audit's own stated remaining gap for #349 ("independent-study and coverage-aware unknown-type validation remain gaps"), this bounded search — now including an expanded 2023–2026 round — did not find a single source that scores known-type accuracy and unknown-type rejection together, at matched coverage, across independent studies, under one common protocol, for any of the methods named in the use case. **This is reported as a gap in the sources checked in this pass, not a claim that no such study exists in the broader literature**, and the use case itself remains open and incomplete.

### Draft (not posted; superseded by rewire-benchmarks #27, kept only as a historical record): proposed focused issue for rewire-benchmarks

> **Title:** B349: Measure cross-study/platform annotation transfer with known-type accuracy and unknown-type rejection at matched coverage, under one common protocol
>
> **Scope.** Freeze one ontology level and one reference/development/test study split (whole studies and, where available, whole platforms held out — not donor-level only). Score CellTypist, a nearest-centroid/kNN reference-mapping baseline, and a marker-rule baseline on identical held-out cells. Include at least one reference-absent (unknown) cell type in the held-out set. Report a **risk-coverage (accuracy-vs-abstention) curve** for each method — not a single accuracy number at one unstated operating point — so that methods can be compared at matched coverage levels rather than only at whatever cutoff each happens to use by default. Each method's own native abstention mechanism may be used (e.g., a fixed numeric cutoff, an Otsu-style automatic threshold, or another rule); a single shared threshold rule applied identically to every method is **one possible design choice for comparability, not a requirement** — the first milestone should specify, for each method, how its threshold is set and on what data (development-only, to avoid tuning on the held-out test set), and report the resulting curve rather than one point on it.
>
> **What existing evidence already covers, so this item should not re-measure it:** scTab's donor-held-out unknown/absent-type-detection ROC-AUC of 0.782 — correct known-type vs. absent-type, measured within its pooled 249-dataset corpus (not a whole-study or whole-platform holdout), an AUC, not a risk-coverage curve, and distinct from scTab's separate 0.891 figure, which is a known-type error-detection AUC (correct vs. incorrect among types the model was trained on) rather than an unknown-type result, and is not claimed as existing coverage for this item; Abdelaal et al. 2019's pre-2019 conventional-classifier cross-platform/rejection comparison; scArches's TMS→TM whole-tissue-holdout demonstration using its own fixed >50%-uncertainty cutoff (~84% accuracy at that one operating point, known-type only in its own comparator figure); scParadise's matched, non-overlapping-sample/donor 7-method comparator panel (same published atlases, not cross-study), whose UnknownCellDetector uses an Otsu-automatic (not fixed-constant) threshold.
>
> **What this item would add that none of the above does:** a *single* common protocol, with a *whole-study-or-platform* (not donor-level or within-atlas) holdout, scoring more than one method on *identical* held-out cells, with a *reference-absent type included in that same held-out set*, and **risk-coverage reporting** across each method's own abstention mechanism rather than a single accuracy figure at one unstated threshold — i.e., the compound comparison the use case's `collection_plan.comparison_question` actually asks for.
>
> **Candidate data/baselines to reuse, not re-invent:** scArches's TMS/TM pancreas and whole-body setup (already demonstrates the holdout design, just needs a multi-method common-protocol scoring layer added); scParadise's scNoah benchmarking module and its named comparator list (already implements the multi-method scoring layer, just needs a cross-study/whole-platform holdout substituted for its current within-atlas, non-overlapping-sample/donor test splits). **Licensing note for implementation:** the scParadise article text is CC BY-NC 4.0, but its installable software (scparadise, MIT-licensed per its own GitHub `LICENSE` file, verified directly) and its Figshare-deposited analysis scripts (CC BY 4.0, per Figshare's own API metadata) are each separately and more permissively licensed than the article itself; do not assume the article's non-commercial term restricts code reuse without checking the specific artifact being reused, as the two are independent here.
>
> **Not claimed:** that this is the only possible design, or that the two reused components above can simply be concatenated without engineering and validation work.
