# De novo binder design: independent review of the 2026-10-22 batch

Batch: `data/omics/use-case-coverage-binder-20261022/` (420 records). Use case:
`use-case-de-novo-binder-design`. Issue [#176](https://github.com/rewire-bio/rewire-benchmark-data/issues/176).

Reviewer: a Claude review agent that did not extract this batch. No human review is claimed. Nothing
was executed, reproduced or experimentally replicated. This review checks each stored value against
the pinned source at its stated locator, checks that the nulls are genuinely unstated, and judges the
relevance claims, the exclusions and the draft summary.

The task named commit `2ec78cd`. That commit was amended to `8a2a61c` while this review was reading
the batch, dropping an obsolete note about issue #177 from `build_coverage.py`, `coverage.json` and
`research.md`. Everything below is of the bytes at `8a2a61c` plus this review's own corrections.

## Outcome

- Five of the seven pinned source hashes recompute exactly. Two do not, and neither is reconciled by
  changing a hash: the BindCraft supplementary archive is generated per request by Europe PMC, and the
  Adaptyv page is a mutable bioRxiv page. Both are recorded as findings and checked another way.
- All 159 results match their source. No stored number is wrong, no denominator is wrong, and every
  footnote marker and parenthesis in a printed value is kept as printed.
- Two results had a wrong locator: an Adaptyv sentence attributed to Section 4.2 that is in Section
  4.1, quoted as "the most exciting improvement" where the source reads "Another exciting
  improvement". The values, 3% and 13%, are correct. Both locators are corrected.
- Twelve evaluations are corrected from `author_reported` to `independent_paper`. They are the
  published RFdiffusion designs re-tested by the AlphaProteo authors, and `data/vocab/origin.ttl`
  defines `author_reported` as reported by the authors of the evaluated method.
- Seven Table 1 cells now carry a `source_warnings` entry: Table 1's AlphaProteo column pools two or
  three different design systems for SC2RBD, PD-L1, TrkA and TNF-alpha.
- The same-assay disagreement with the RFdiffusion publication is now stated in the limitations of the
  two AlphaProteo protocols that carry the re-test. It was not stated anywhere before.
- The draft summary opened with "Nine sources carry measured outcomes". Nine is the literature pass's
  candidate count, not this use case's evidence, and five of the nine were never retrieved. Corrected
  to four studies and seven artifacts, with the five unretrieved sources named as gaps.
- Both refusals to compute hold, and nothing was computed anyway: no record in the batch carries a
  derivation.
- All three external vocabulary mappings dereference to what the batch claims. None is removed.
- All 14 relevance judgements hold with the grades the extraction pass gave them: 10 direct, 2 proxy,
  2 outside scope.
- PXDesign's baseline column appears nowhere as a result or evaluation, as required.

## How the check was done

1. Re-downloaded all seven artifacts into an empty directory and recomputed every SHA-256.
2. Built my own text layers with `pdftotext -layout` (poppler 26.08.0), reproducing the extraction
   pass's line counts exactly (AlphaProteo 2,811 lines, Bennett SI 3,582). Line counts are therefore
   stable across poppler versions for these two files, but no locator in this batch is a line number:
   every locator names a table, row and column or quotes a sentence, so the batch does not depend on
   the poppler version.
3. Read AlphaProteo Table 1, Table S2, Table S3 and Sections S1.2.1, S2, S4.1 and S4.2 cell by cell,
   and Bennett Supplementary Methods Tables 5 and 6 cell by cell: 117 of the 159 results checked by
   eye against the printed table, not sampled. For AlphaProteo's wide tables I re-derived the column
   assignment arithmetically rather than trusting `pdftotext` ordering.
4. Wrote my own parser for this review to check that every quoted sentence in a `source_locator`
   appears verbatim in its source, and that every `printed_value` appears verbatim in its own source's
   bytes. 198 of 198 printed values were found. Of the 71 locators carrying a prose quotation longer
   than 40 characters, one failed, and that is the Adaptyv correction above. The remaining misses were
   multi-line PDF column headers that no contiguous-string test can match; each was confirmed by
   reading the table.
5. Reparsed BindCraft Supplementary Data 1 and the Adaptyv round 2 CSV with my own scripts to test the
   two refusals to compute.
6. Dereferenced the STATO and QUDT IRIs.
7. The extraction pass's own modules (`extract/alphaproteo.py` and the rest) were **not** rerun. The
   batch's `extract/validate_batch.ts` was run as a schema harness only, after the corrections; it
   validates records and does not produce values.

## The Section S4.1 quotation and what it implies

The quotation is verbatim. The only difference from the source is that the source prints
"the published RFdiffusion designs" with a footnote marker after "designs" pointing at the figshare
URL the sequences came from, and the quotation elides the marker.

> Published experimental success rates of RFdiffusion were based on 96-well biolayer interferometry
> (BLI) measurements [S28], which are impractical for us to perform on our own designs. Therefore, to
> quantitatively compare success rates, we downloaded sequences of the published RFdiffusion designs
> and synthesized and tested them by yeast display (Section S1.2) alongside our own designs. Our
> results matched the published success rates for PD-L1 but were 2-fold lower for IL-7RA (16% versus
> 33%) and 0% for TrkA (versus 6%) (Table S2).

**1. The assay is shared and the selection funnel is not. The extraction pass is right, and the null
fields do block comparison.** Section S4.1 states one shared assay and says nothing about a shared
selection funnel. Table 1 confirms the asymmetry: AlphaProteo "used an automated filtering procedure
to choose between 47 and 172 binder candidates to test", while the RFdiffusion arm is a flat 95
designs per target in every Table S2 and Table 1 cell. The two arms are two evaluations on one
protocol. On each RFdiffusion-arm evaluation `comparison.population`, `comparison.inputs`,
`comparison.adaptation` and `comparison.budget` are null, each with a `missing_metadata` entry giving
a reason (`conflicting` for population, adaptation and budget; `unreported` for inputs), while
`protocol_id`, `dataset_version`, `aggregation` and `metric_implementation` are stated. That is the
documented mechanism: `docs/collection.md` says comparison fields the source does not state are null
and "that blocks automatic comparison, which is intended". They were not promoted to a comparison,
and they should not be. The judgement on the protocol also carries the constraint in words: "Do not
read the AlphaProteo and RFdiffusion evaluations on a target as a controlled comparison."

**2. The same-assay disagreement was not recorded, and now is, on the protocols rather than the
source.** In AlphaProteo's own yeast display the re-tested RFdiffusion designs give 16.8%, 12.6% and
0.0% over 95 designs, against published rates of 33.7%, 12.6% and 6.3%. So the re-test reproduces
PD-L1 and contradicts IL-7RA and TrkA. The batch recorded the re-tested values as results and the
published values only inside the compilation claim, and it recorded a concern about Table 1 rounding
the re-tested figures, which is a different and much smaller matter. The disagreement itself was
stated nowhere.

Ruling on where it belongs: **the protocol's limitations**, not the source's `evidence_concerns`. Two
reasons. First, an `evidence_concerns` entry makes `cleanSource` false in
`shared/omics/use-cases.ts`, which withholds every judgement that cites the source; the house
precedent for a problem confined to particular cells is a result-level or protocol-level mark, stated
plainly in `docs/reviews/2026-10-12-rna-other-omics-batches.md`: "The reviewer chose result-level
marks over source-level `evidence_concerns`, which would block comparison for every result of the
source." Second, and more important, the disagreement is not a defect in AlphaProteo. AlphaProteo
disclosed it, printed both numbers side by side in Table S2, and offered a mechanism: the yeast
surface environment, and target concentrations "10- to 20-fold lower" than the publication's, "which
may exclude lower-affinity hits". A source that surfaces a conflict with another paper is behaving
exactly as the store wants.

On the brief's requirement that the re-tested and published values "both exist as results with their
own sources": that cannot be satisfied from this evidence, and the extraction pass was right not to
fake it. The published values' own source is Watson et al., which neither pass retrieved.
`docs/collection.md` holds that only primary sources support a record, so the published rates stay a
verbatim transcription in
`uc-binder-20261022-claim-alphaproteo-other-design-methods-compilation` with Watson et al. named as an
unretrieved gap. Retrieving Watson et al. is what would close this, and it is the single highest-value
next step for this use case after the missing comparator.

**3. Table 1's pooled AlphaProteo column: confirmed, and marked at result level rather than
withheld.** Section S2 states the pooling verbatim, and the arithmetic confirms it: the Table S2
per-system denominators sum to Table 1's for every multi-system target (SC2RBD 94+47+31 = 172, PD-L1
94+31+34 = 159, TrkA 94+37 = 131, TNF-alpha 30+24 = 54). The filter effect the extraction pass cites
is real: changing only the filter moved PD-L1 from 9.6% over 94 designs to 16.1% over 31, and Section
S2 says "Both improving the filter and the model resulted in increased experimental success rates".
Table S2 is indeed the better table and both are extracted.

A pooled column across three design systems is not one method's success rate, so it should not stand
in a cross-method comparison. It should not be withheld entirely either: the cells are honestly
labelled, arithmetically consistent with Table S2, and Table S2 supplies the per-system
decomposition, so withholding would lose a correctly transcribed value for no gain. Each pooled cell
now carries a `source_warnings` entry quoting Section S2, giving the denominator sum and pointing at
the Table S2 evaluations. Four of the eight Table 1 targets are single-system (BHRF1, IL-7RA, IL-17A,
VEGF-A) and their Table 1 cells equal their Table S2 cells, so they are not marked. That is seven
cells in total: the hit rates for SC2RBD, PD-L1, TrkA and TNF-alpha and the affinities for SC2RBD,
PD-L1 and TrkA.

Both hold, and nothing was computed anyway: `grep -c '"derivation"'` over the batch returns 0.

**BindCraft Supplementary Data 1.** I reparsed the pinned CSV (212 rows, 13 target labels) with my own
script, counting non-empty `Binding` cells per target. The reconciliation the extraction pass reports

The per-target values in the batch come from the Results prose and Supplementary Table 1: each

designs", "did not bind"), each locator quotes the sentence, and PD-L1 is recorded as 7 of 9 from the

One incidental finding, not a defect: the file carries a thirteenth target label, HER2, with 10 rows
and one non-empty `Binding` cell. HER2 appears in BindCraft only in the AAV retargeting experiment,
not among the 12 benchmark targets, so it does not belong in the 46.3% mean and is correctly absent
from the batch.

**Adaptyv per-method rates.** Also correct. The pinned CSV has 402 data rows against the 400 designs
the paper says were tested, `design_models` is the entrant's own free text, the organisers "manually
curated and categorized" those declarations by hand, no section of the paper or the README states an
aggregation over the rows, and the three binding thresholds behind the Figure 8 counts are never

**AlphaProteo's compilation rows.** The refusal is upheld. `paper_compilation` is a permitted origin
and the store uses it 293 times, so the question is fair, but two things stop these particular cells.
The primary sources were not retrieved, and Section S4.2 states that the asterisked values were
re-measured by the AlphaProteo authors and that for PD-L1 they substituted a different design for the
published best one: "we found a design with 1000-fold better KD (RFD_PDL1_76, KD=1.6 nM) than the
published 'best' design (RFD_PDL1_77, KD =1.4 uM). Therefore, we compared our results to the
higher-affinity PD-L1 binder". That is the same defect the brief identifies in PXDesign's baseline
column, and it gets the same treatment.

## Metric and unit concepts

`hit-rate` is not a respelling of `success-rate` and the `skos:broader` relation is right.
`success-rate` is defined as the "proportion of cases that meet a task-specific success criterion
stated by the source"; `hit-rate` narrows that to a wet-lab criterion over the tested set. The batch
relies on the distinction: Bennett's RF2 and AlphaFold3 filter pass rates are `success-rate` (24
results) and the wet-lab rates are `hit-rate` (39 results). Collapsing them would put a computational
filter pass rate and a yeast-display threshold in one concept. The `#177` sibling batch adds
`enrichment-factor`, `normalized-enrichment-factor`, `bedroc` and three CACHE challenge scores and
does not add `hit-rate`, so there is no collision and the obsolete reconciliation note was already
dropped from `research.md` and `coverage.json` before this review began.

All three external mappings were dereferenced, and all three are correct, so none is removed:

| IRI | Resolves to | Used as | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `STATO_0000607` | "proportion": "a ratio which corresponds to the fraction of the total presenting a particular feature" | `broadMatch` on `hit-rate` | Correct; consistent with the same file's `exactMatch` on `:proportion` and `broadMatch` on `:success-rate` |
| `qudt:NanoMOL-PER-L` | "Nanomole per Litre", symbol `nmol/L`, multiplier 1.0E-6 | `exactMatch` on `nanomolar` | Correct |
| `qudt:MicroMOL-PER-L` | "Micromole per Litre", symbol `μmol/L`, multiplier 1.0E-3 | `exactMatch` on `micromolar` | Correct |

`research.md` records the QUDT IRIs as written from a naming pattern and not dereferenced, and the
STATO IRI as not dereferenced. That was true of the extraction pass. They have now been dereferenced
and verified, which is recorded here and in `review.json` rather than by rewriting the extraction
pass's own notes. `dissociation-constant` carries no external match; leaving it unmapped is allowed,
since `docs/collection.md` asks for an external match only where one exists.

Keeping `nanomolar` and `micromolar` as separate concepts rather than one molar concept with the scale
in `unit_detail` is right for the reason the extraction pass gives: a comparison checks metric,
qualifier, unit and direction but not `unit_detail`, so one shared concept would let a nanomolar and a
micromolar value compare as equals. Two results use `micromolar` (BindCraft's CrSAS-6 affinities, 5.7
and 4.2 uM) and 38 use `nanomolar`.

Descriptive, no workflow recommendation, and it states in its first sentence that no paper runs a
controlled cross-method comparison. Every number traces to a result, with one exception that is now
corrected.

The claim opened "Nine sources carry measured outcomes for de novo binder design and none of them
answers which workflow to use". Nine is the literature pass's candidate count, from
`data/omics/lit-search-20261022-uc-binder/report.md` ("What exists is nine sources with real measured
outcomes"). This batch extracted four of those nine, as seven source records, and the other five were
never retrieved. So the number matches no record in the store, and asserting what all nine show
reaches past what was read, against the rule that a failed retrieval "is never evidence that nothing
exists". The opening sentence now says four studies and seven artifacts, and names the five
unretrieved sources as gaps rather than as evidence.

Everything else in the summary was traced: 0% over 54 on TNF-alpha and 88% over 94 on BHRF1 at the
0.2 threshold (1.3 on IL-17A); 46.3% over twelve targets with a 10% to 100% range; 2 of 7 on Bet v1
and 6 of 6 on SpCas9; 2% over 96 on TcdB, 0% over 96 on IL-7Ra, 0.01% to 0.2% over 9,000; 1% over 96

## Conflicts in the sources

Five, all internal to a source rather than extraction errors, and all already recorded by the
extraction pass on the relevant source's `evidence_concerns`. I checked each and found them accurate
and correctly worded.

1. **AlphaProteo:** Table S2 prints the re-tested RFdiffusion IL-7RA rate as 16.8% and Table 1 as 17,
   while Section S4.1 states it as "16% versus 33%" and TrkA as "0% (versus 6%)" where the table
   prints 0.0 and 6.3. The text rounds down rather than to nearest. The table values are what is
   recorded.
2. **BindCraft:** Supplementary Data 1's row filter reconciles for nine of twelve targets and fails
   for PD-L1, CrSAS-6 and CbAgo. Independently reproduced above.
3. **BindCraft:** Fig. 1b's caption defines success as binding on SPR, but PD-1 was screened by BLI in
   a bivalent Fc-fusion format and CLDN1 against soluble analogues, so the 46.3% average pools
   different endpoints.
4. **Bennett:** 95 designs per target in the main text against 96 in Supplementary Methods Table 6.
5. **Adaptyv:** round 1 printed as both 2.5% (5/201) and 3%, and round 2 expression as both 95% (378
   of 400) and 94%; and the three binding thresholds behind Figure 8 are never defined.

One observation on where these sit rather than on their accuracy. All six entries are on source
records, which makes `cleanSource` false for AlphaProteo, BindCraft, Bennett and the Adaptyv preprint,
and that withholds every judgement citing them. By the house precedent quoted above, each of these is
confined to particular cells or to one protocol and would ordinarily be marked at result or protocol
level instead. I have not reversed the extraction pass's choice: downgrading an honest caveat is not a
reviewer's call to make quietly, and "withheld" is a safe and honest state. It is flagged here as a
decision for the maintainer, with the derived outcome recorded below so the effect is visible rather
than discovered at release.

Every judgement is withheld, and the reason on each is the same: "a cited source is unchecked or has
evidence concerns; a source the use case cites is unchecked or has evidence concerns; protocol
evidence is disputed or unchecked". The two Bennett in-silico judgements are withheld on the protocol
clause alone. The cause is `cleanSource` in `shared/omics/use-cases.ts`, which treats any non-empty

**This is a decision for the maintainer, and I have deliberately not made it.** I did not remove or
weaken any concern the extraction pass recorded. Downgrading an honest caveat to make evidence
publishable is not something a reviewer should do quietly, and "withheld" is a safe state. But the
