## How the values were checked

The extractor's `extract/build_batch.py` was not run. Three parsers were written for this review and
are kept in `data/omics/use-case-coverage-hitdisc-20261022/review/`:

- `myparse_shen.py` reads the Shen et al. JATS XML with `xml.etree`, expanding header `colspan` and
  `rowspan` into leaf column names and keeping footnote markers in row labels, so a locator such as
  `row 'Boltz-2 (Affinity)a'` has to match the printed label exactly.
- `myparse_xlsx.py` reads the CACHE workbook straight from `xl/worksheets/sheet9.xml` and
  `xl/sharedStrings.xml`, keeping both the stored double string and the shortest decimal that
  round-trips to it.
- `compare.py` parses each result's `source_locator` back into a table, row and column and compares
  `printed_value` with the reference cell; for sheet S8 it also compares `raw_xml_value` with the
  stored string and the locator's quoted column header with the sheet's own header.
- `coverage_check.py` builds the complete set of printed cells and compares it with the set of cells
  the batch claims, so an omitted, duplicated or invented cell shows up.

### Nulls and zeros

`missing_metadata` carries `uncertainty: unreported` on all 1,091 results. Correct: the Shen tables
print a mean and a median with no spread, sheet S8 prints single values, and Kim et al. print point
AUCs. The other reasons (44 configuration versions, 121 evaluation budgets, two method versions, two
source DOIs, one benchmark version, one dataset positives count, one split seed) are genuinely
unstated in the pinned sources.

Two results have `numeric_value` null: the dopamine D4 AUC for DOCK3.7 and for Boltz-2 pIC50. The
source prints `both with AUC ~ 71` for the two scores together. Leaving `numeric_value` null and
keeping `AUC ~ 71` in `printed_value`, with a source warning, is the right call; writing 71 would turn
an approximation shared by two arms into two exact figures.

No missing value is recorded as zero. The four CACHE workflows with blank merged-library cells (1694,
1695, 1716, 1717) have no result at all, which the coverage check confirms: 19 rows times 9 columns,
not 23. Column K (carboxylic acid penalty) holds the shared empty string for 22 workflows and `yes`
for 1700, and is recorded as a claim rather than as 23 results. The unheaded first column, a
permutation of 1 to 23, is likewise a claim and not 23 results under an invented metric.

## Rulings on the three corrections the extraction pass made to the search pass

### 1. CACHE #3 and the per-workflow hit rate: partly upheld, partly overturned

Upheld. The figure is **Figure 6**, not Figure 5: Figure 5 is "Sample CACHE #3 chemical series".
Panel a is the score of the best Round 1 hit, panel b is the aggregated score of all Round 1 hits, and
panel c is "the normalized number of experimentally confirmed Tier 1 hits (chemically novel) and Tier
2 hits (not novel) predicted active compounds when screening all 1739 Round 1 compounds (calculated
as the aggregated score of all compounds predicted active divided by the number of compounds
predicted to be actives)". So the "divided by the number of compounds predicted to be actives"
wording does belong to panel c, as the extractor says. The search report's "Figure 5b (per-workflow
hit rate)" is wrong for the published version.

Also upheld: recording `Compounds tested` (column D) and `Hits` (column E) as separate counts without
dividing them. A derived rate is a computation, not a printed value, and this arm has no
predicted-active denominator at all.

Overturned: **"CACHE #3 publishes no per-workflow hit rate" is wrong, twice over.**

- Columns R, S and T of sheet S8 are not transcribed numbers. The workbook stores them as the
  formulas `1000*N/L`, `1000*O/L` and `R+S`, which is 1000 times the confirmed Tier 1 or Tier 2 hit
  count divided by the count of compounds predicted active. Divided by 1000 that is the article's own
  hit-rate form, restricted to one tier. The batch stores all 57 of these values, so it does carry a
  per-workflow rate; the previous `metric_qualifier` ("Normalised Tier 1 value") hid it, which is also
  why the check for a hit rate smuggled in under another concept came back clean.
- The article's Results section, "Trends and Strategies from the Best Performing Computational
  Workflows", calls Figure 6 panels a, b and c "top scoring compounds, top hit rates, and top
  selections from the merged Round 1 library". The body text therefore calls panel b a hit rate while
  the caption calls it an aggregated score. The article is inconsistent with itself.

The extractor also attributed to the article a hit-rate definition the article does not print:
"confirmed actives divided by compounds predicted active". That paraphrase happens to describe the
stored formula, but the caption says "aggregated score ... divided by", so the paraphrase and the
claim that no rate is published cannot both stand.

Changes made: the `cache-challenge-normalized-hit-score` definition and scope note in
`data/vocab/metric.ttl` now state the stored formula and say plainly that the Figure 6c caption does
not describe it; the 57 results' `metric_qualifier` names the formula and the per-1000 scaling and
their `source_warnings` entry explains it; the merged-library protocol's `metric_definition` and two
limitations are corrected; the own-selection protocol's hit-rate limitation is corrected; and the
Figure 6 inconsistency is recorded as a limitation on both CACHE protocols. Four
`metadata-correction-*` claims keep the previous wording.

No `evidence_concerns` entry was added for this. See [Why no evidence concern on the CACHE
sources](#why-no-evidence-concern-on-the-cache-sources).

### 2. The LIT-PCBA totals conflict: upheld, and extended

Both figures verified in the pinned bytes. The official page's data content table reads "15 target
sets, 7761 actives and 382674 unique inactives selected from high-confidence PubChem Bioassay data".
Shen et al., Methods "Datasets", read "The full set contains a total of 15 targets, 10 030 confirmed
actives and 2 798 737 confirmed inactives". Neither reconciles the other, and leaving the conflict
unresolved is right.

Two gaps in how it was recorded, both fixed.

- The `evidence_concerns` entry on the project-page source named both figures and both locators but
  only the page's artifact hash. It now carries Shen's hash as well, in both the message and the
  locator.
- The conflict was not constraining the results it bears on. EF and NEF both depend on the
  actives-to-inactives ratio, and NEF is defined precisely as a correction for it, so a dispute of
  this size about that ratio belongs on the numbers. All 84 Shen Table 4 results now carry a
  `source_warnings` entry naming both totals, both locators and both hashes.

The per-target counts used for the five `dataset_subset` records were checked against the page and
match (ESR_ago 13/4378, ESR_antago 88/3820, PPARG 24/4071, TP53 64/3345, MAPK1 308/61567, and ADRB2
17/311748 for the unscreened target quoted as about 1:18,000).

### 3. DUD-E's 50 decoys per clustered ligand: upheld, nothing left to fix

The DUD-E abstract reads "102 proteins with 22886 clustered ligands drawn from ChEMBL, each with 50
property-matched decoys drawn from ZINC". The benchmark record says exactly that. The search pass's

## The finding that mattered most: the leakage audit was withholding the whole use case

The extraction pass recorded the LIT-PCBA leakage audit as an `attributes.evidence_concerns` entry on
`hitdisc-20261022-source-litpcba-audit`, the audit's own source record. That is the wrong record for
it, and the consequence is not cosmetic.

`cleanSource` in `shared/omics/use-cases.ts` treats any source with a non-empty `evidence_concerns`
as unclean, and a judgement is withheld when "a cited source is unchecked or has evidence concerns"
or when "a source the use case cites is unchecked or has evidence concerns". The use case lists
`hitdisc-20261022-source-litpcba-audit` in its `source_ids`, and so does the summary claim. So the
moment the batch was reviewed, **all twelve judgements and the summary would have been withheld**,
including the DEKOIS 2.0, GPCRrecent, Kim et al. and CACHE #3 arms, which the audit says nothing
about.

The audit is not an unreliable source. It is the source that reports the problem, it was read only as
an abstract, and the batch already records that. The entry is removed, and the audit's finding is kept
where it bites:

- the claim `hitdisc-20261022-claim-litpcba-leakage-audit` on the LIT-PCBA benchmark;
- the `limitations` on the LIT-PCBA benchmark record and on the five-target protocol;
- a new `source_warnings` entry on each of the 84 LIT-PCBA results, with the audit's hash and locator.

A `metadata-correction-*` claim keeps the removed entry verbatim.

On the brief's instruction to record the audit as an `evidence_concerns` entry on the LIT-PCBA dataset
or benchmark records: that is not possible under the current schema. `shared/omics/attribute-registry.ts`
declares `evidence_concerns` for `source` records only, and `source_warnings` for `result` records
only. Benchmarks and datasets carry `limitations` and `scope_note`. The placement above is the closest
the registry allows, and the per-result warnings do reach every LIT-PCBA number in this batch. Making
the field available on benchmarks and datasets would be a schema change and is out of scope for a
review.

The remaining concern on `hitdisc-20261022-source-litpcba-page` is kept, because that one is a genuine
source problem: its totals contradict the other pinned source. Its effect is targeted. That is the
right outcome: the LIT-PCBA arm is the one arm resting on an unresolved conflict about the denominator
its headline metric normalises by.

Both claims above were checked against the loaded store rather than read off the code. Running
`deriveUseCaseInputs` over the store after the batch was added gives eleven active judgements and a
published summary, with `shen-litpcba5` withheld for "a cited source is unchecked or has evidence
concerns" and for having no eligible evaluation. The concern reaches the arm by two routes, both
pointing only at it: the judgement cites the page directly, and the five-target dataset
`hitdisc-20261022-dataset-litpcba-5` cites it too, which makes each of its seven evaluations
ineligible under `evaluationBlocker`. The use case does not cite the page, so the other eleven arms
are untouched.

### Why no evidence concern on the CACHE sources

The CACHE #3 problem is real but it is a problem of description, not of the numbers: the counts are
unambiguous, and the formulas resolve what the caption muddles. An `evidence_concerns` entry on
`hitdisc-20261022-source-cache3-si002` or `-cache3` would withhold every judgement on the use case,
because the use case cites both, exactly as the audit entry did. Blanking the page to mark a caption
inconsistency would be out of proportion. The corrections went to the records that carry the meaning:
the vocabulary definition, the 57 results' qualifiers and warnings, and both protocols' limitations.
The inconsistency is stated here and in those limitations so a later reader, or a human reviewer, can
decide otherwise.

## The other corrections this review made

### The WF1696 wording was the wrong sentence

The summary claim and the merged-library judgement both said WF1696 captured its hits, "which the
authors report as significantly more hits than any other workflow". That sentence belongs to a
different workflow and a different arm. The article says of WF1715: "Workflow WF1715 not only produced
the top scoring compound (CACHE3HI_1715_89) but also identified significantly more hits than others,
as reflected by the aggregated score of Round 1 molecules (Figure 6b)". Of WF1696 it says: "It also
did better than any other method in retrieving hits from the collection of all molecules selected in
Round 1". Both records now use the source's own words. Two `metadata-correction-*` claims keep the
previous text.

### A zero score is not a score of zero

Twelve of the 23 workflows returned no experimentally confirmed hit, and the workbook stores `0` for
their score of best hit, aggregated score and top novelty score. Those zeros are explicit cells and
the transcription is right, but a 0 there means there was no hit to score, not a hit the committee
scored at zero, and anyone averaging or ranking the column across workflows would be misled. 54
results now carry that warning (columns G, H and I for the twelve workflows with no hit, and column J
for every workflow with no novel hit), and the own-selection protocol records it as a limitation.

### A link the store refused

The five LIT-PCBA `dataset_subset` records linked `used_in` to the five-target `dataset`.
`shared/omics/relations.ts` defines `used_in` as data to an assessment, "Dataset subset to the
protocol that uses it", so a dataset is not a legal target and `npm run records -- add` refused the
batch outright. The links now point at `hitdisc-20261022-protocol-shen-litpcba5`; each subset's parent
dataset was already recorded in `attributes.source_dataset`, so nothing is lost.

Worth noting for the extraction skill: the retrieval log says an independent checker confirmed that
"every link relation exists in `shared/omics/relations.ts` and resolves to a record of an allowed kind
in this batch or the store". It checked that the target exists and is of an allowed kind for *some*
relation, not that the relation permits that from-kind and to-kind pair. One `metadata-correction-*`
claim keeps the previous links.

### `evidence_checked` now names this review

`docs/use-cases.md` requires the pass's dated review in `evidence_checked.records`. This document was
added to the list.

## The rest of `reviewer_must_judge`

**ipTM and Min-iPAE reprinted in Table 2.** Keep them. They are printed cells of a table extracted in
full, each carries a source warning saying it is the co-folding confidence score rather than a
rescoring arm and that Table 1 prints the same value, and dropping them would make a table that was
extracted in full look partial. Verified that the three pairs are identical: Protenix ipTM and AF3
ipTM and Min-iPAE match their Table 1 rows cell for cell.

**Per-target columns as their own evaluations.** Keep one evaluation per configuration and protocol,
with `metric_qualifier` and `source_locator` distinguishing the per-target and averaged cells. The
alternative would create 84 evaluations for Table 4 and 36 for Table 5 and still need the qualifier to
tell EF1% from NEF1%. The five `dataset_subset` records hold the per-target counts for anyone who
wants them, and the evaluation's `comparison.aggregation` says "per target, and the average over the
five targets". Table 5's column structure was checked: the method cell spans two sample-count rows,
and the batch's continuation rows ("row continued, sample count 1") attach to the right method, which
the comparator confirms by resolving each one through its configuration.

**CACHE workflows as configurations with no parent entity.** Right. Their computational steps are in
Figures 2, 7 and 8, which this batch does not read, and no primary source describes each workflow, so
there is nothing to describe an entity from. The eleven Shen comparators without a parent entity are
the same case. The ten workflows whose participants sheet S8 names are no better off: a participant
name is not a description of a method.

**Granularity of the three CACHE metric concepts.** Right as they stand. The activity score covers
columns G, H and J, which are the same committee score at three aggregations, and the contract puts
aggregation in `metric_qualifier`. Novelty is column I. The normalised score covers R, S and T, which
differ by tier and by summation, again a qualifier matter. Splitting further would multiply concepts
without separating measures. Two of the three are flagged as committee judgements rather than
measurements, correctly: the activity score as "a committee judgement, not a measurement" and the
novelty score as "a panel vote, not a measurement". The normalised score is not flagged that way and
should not be: the formulas show it is arithmetic on counts of confirmed hits.

**`printed_value` as the shortest round-tripping decimal.** Accept, with one qualification stated in
the batch itself. It is the right default: the cell number formats were not read, every result says so
in `workbook_number_format`, and `raw_xml_value` keeps the stored string so nothing is lost. All 332
`raw_xml_value` strings were compared with the sheet and match.

**The CACHE own-selection arm as `direct`.** Agree, and the confound is correctly its first
limitation: every compound was synthesised and assayed and the labels are measurements, but each
workflow chose its own compounds, so the arm does not compare workflows on a common library. The
merged-library arm is the comparison on one library, and both are recorded.

**Whether the GPCRrecent six-target protocol earns a judgement.** Yes, keep it. It is the only arm in
the batch whose targets postdate the training cutoff of the model scored on it: Shen et al. say "since
Boltz-2 employed a temporal cutoff of 2023-6 in its training, we have to extract a corresponding
subset containing only six targets", and footnote a names the six UniProt accessions. Dropping it
would remove the tightest leakage control in the batch. It stays `proxy`, and its rationale and
limitations already say plainly that only two Boltz-2 arms ran on it, that no docking or rescoring arm
was run on the same six targets, and that it therefore constrains Boltz-2's own numbers rather than
comparing it with docking. Its stratum label says "(2 arms)".

### For whoever merges this with the #176 batch

The batch for issue #176 adds `hit-rate` to the same file. This branch adds no key that collides with
it: the six concepts in `data/vocab/metric.ttl` came from the extraction commit `161dc5b`, and this
review changed only the definition and scope note of `cache-challenge-normalized-hit-score`, adding no
key to `metric.ttl` and nothing at all to `unit.ttl`.

`cache-challenge-normalized-hit-score` does not duplicate `hit-rate`, but it is a hit rate in kind:
same sort of numerator (experimentally confirmed actives) over the same denominator (compounds the
workflow predicted active). It differs in two ways that keep the numbers from being interchangeable,
and both are now in its definition: the numerator counts one hit tier only (Tier 1 chemically novel,
or Tier 2 not novel), and the value is scaled by 1000.

So once `hit-rate` exists in the file, the right triple on `cache-challenge-normalized-hit-score` is
`skos:broader :hit-rate`, exactly as `normalized-enrichment-factor` already carries
`skos:broader :enrichment-factor`. This review did not add it, deliberately: `:hit-rate` does not
exist on this branch, and a `skos:broader` pointing at a missing key is not caught by anything. That
was checked rather than assumed, by adding the triple temporarily and running `tests/linked-data.test.ts`
and `tests/attributes.test.ts`, which both passed with the reference dangling. A triple that no
validator would ever flag is worse left to rot on one branch than added once the key is there, so it
is left for the merge.

The vocabulary file parses and `npm run records -- check` accepts it; see
[Validation](#validation).

active-to-decoy ratio" over 16 GPCRs with first structures after 2022; the five LIT-PCBA targets run
from about 1:43 to about 1:200; DUD-E is 50 decoys per clustered ligand. The three Kim et al.
protocols have no decoys at all, which their limitations say: the negatives are compounds a docking
screen already ranked highly, from a 490-million (σ2), 1.7-billion (AmpC) and 138-million (D4)
molecule screen, and the limitation that these AUCs are not comparable with decoy enrichment is
present on each. The CACHE protocols likewise have no decoys and now say so explicitly.

No judgement has `excluded_evaluations`, and none needs one: every evaluation on each protocol is on
the protocol's own endpoint.

The use case names no `baseline` claim. The extractor's reason stands as far as it goes: issue #177
asks for docking **and** a ligand-similarity or pharmacophore comparator, and no ligand-similarity arm
is scored anywhere in this batch, so a claim naming both would overstate. But `docs/use-cases.md`
asks each use case to name "the best established algorithmic, pre-machine-learning method", and
docking alone meets that for virtual screening: Glide SP, Gnina with the Vina and AD4 scoring
functions, GOLD CHEMPLP, LeDock, Surflex-Dock and DOCK3.7 are all scored here. A baseline claim naming
the docking methods, with the missing ligand-similarity comparator as a stated gap, would be accurate
and is recommended for a later pass. A review should not author a judgement it would then be reviewing,
so none was added; the gap stays recorded in the use case's `evidence_gaps` and in all twelve
judgements' limitations.

## Conflicts and gaps left open

- **The LIT-PCBA actives-to-inactives ratio** is unresolved between the two pinned sources and is now
  marked on the concern and on all 84 affected results. It withholds the `shen-litpcba5` judgement,
  by design.
- **The CACHE #3 article contradicts itself about Figure 6b**, calling it a hit rate in the Results
  text and an aggregated score in the caption. Recorded, not resolved. No value in this batch comes
  from Figure 6.
- **Eight of Shen Table 1's 24 arms are quoted from references 34, 35, 36, 38 and 39**, none
  retrieved. The footnotes were verified one by one: b to reference 38 (Glide_SP, Surflex-Dock), c to
  39 (GOLD_CHEMPLP, LeDock), d to 34 (TankBind, KarmaDock), e to 35 (CarsiDock), f to 36 (SurfDock,
  computed on all 81 targets because per-target labels were unavailable). The Gnina rows carry no
  footnote, so recording them as the authors' own runs is right. Those eight evaluations' origin is
  `paper_compilation` and no evaluation of those tools in this batch is independent of this
  compilation.
- **`evidence_concerns` is a source-only field.** Recording a benchmark-level or dataset-level concern
  is not possible without a schema change, which this review did not make.
- **Kim et al. quote their own normalised logAUC differently in two places**: the Results text says
  Boltz-2 Ligand ipTM was "27.1% above random" while the author response in the same XML says "20.5%
  above random". Both are in the Mac1 pose arm, which this use case excludes on endpoint grounds, so

- **Scale of `auroc`.** The store now carries `auroc` on a 0-to-1 scale (Shen) and a 0-to-100 scale
  (Kim et al.) under one concept. The Kim results record it in `unit_detail` ("printed as a percentage
  on a 0-100 scale"), which is the right place, but anyone comparing across the two sources must read
  it.
- Everything in `coverage.json` under `gaps` stands, with one exception: the entry saying "per-workflow
  hit rates are not printed anywhere in the article or table S8" is superseded by correction 1 above.
  The extraction pass's documents are left as its own record of what it did; this review supersedes
  that statement.
- Not reached by the extraction pass and still open: no ligand-similarity or pharmacophore baseline

## What this review is not

Automated review by a Claude agent. Every value was checked by transcription against the pinned bytes
by a parser written for this review and by a 48-value manual sample; nothing was recomputed from
primary data, no model was run, no experiment was reproduced, and no human scientist read the sources.
Figures were not read: Shen et al. Figures 1 to 5 and S1 to S17, including the ligand-similarity
leakage analysis, Table 6 and the per-target composition tables S1 and S2; Kim et al. Figures 3 and 5
and their supplements; CACHE #3 Figures 2 to 9, sheets S1 to S7 of the workbook, the second workbook
and the Supporting Information PDF. The LIT-PCBA audit was read as an abstract only. Five closed-access
primaries (DEKOIS 2.0 and 1.0, LIT-PCBA, TocoDecoy, CACHE #1 and #2) were not retrieved by either
pass.
