## Rulings on the extractor's questions

### 1. One protocol per metric, and the 485 evaluations

Upheld. `docs/collection.md`: "A protocol fixes the split, inputs and metric; two tables with
different splits are two protocols." Per-metric protocols are the shape that sentence describes, and
the `lit-structure-20261011-scaffoldlab-*` precedent the extractor cites holds 160 evaluations for
160 results with one metric each.

The leaner precedent is contract-valid too, but not because the contract allows several metrics on one
protocol. `rna-splicing-20261011-alavi2026-protocol-multigene`, which carries the six metrics of
alavi-v2's bundled evaluations, has no `metric` attribute at all. `metric` is optional on a protocol
(848 of the store's 1,350 protocols set it, 502 do not), so a protocol may decline to fix a metric and
both shapes pass validation. Only one of them matches the sentence.

For a reader comparing methods the per-metric shape is better here, for two reasons that are not
about record counts. Comparability is enforced at result level, on metric, qualifier, unit and
direction, so bundling would add no comparison the split shape lacks. And use-case relevance is
judged per protocol, one endpoint at a time, through an `assessed_by` link and a judgement claim; in
this batch the metrics differ in kind, not only in name. `sequence-diversity` has direction `unknown`
and Appendix B says it "is matched rather than ranked"; `scrmsd` is lower-is-better; Appendix B says
pLDDT "is the predictor's confidence in itself, not a measurement of the design" and is not compared
across predictors; and recovery is "descriptive and not an objective", since "methods with high
recovery tend to have low diversity and lower scTM". One bundled protocol would force a single
relevance verdict across six endpoints the report itself says mean different things.

The cost, measured: the 485 evaluations are 1,034 KB of the batch's 2,219 KB, because each repeats the
full `comparison` object and its `metric_implementation` prose. Bundling to about 85 would save
roughly 860 KB. The coordinator closed this on the second round: the owner's lean-release priority is
about release artifacts and duplicated export formats, which ran to 161 MB, not about record counts,
and 860 KB of JSONL is not what it refers to. No reshape.

### 2. The printed numeric matrices in Figures 14 and 15

They are results. `docs/collection.md`: "Figures without printed numbers are not read by eye, by
colour or from pixels; record them as a gap, unless the source publishes the figure's underlying
rows." Figures 14 and 15 print a number in every cell and those numbers are in the PDF text layer, so
the prohibition does not reach them: nothing was read by eye, colour or pixel. The section's own
locator example is a figure locator, `Figure S10, Panel B (page 15 of 18), row "SVMrejection", test
set ALM`, so a figure-located result is an example in the contract, not a concession.

That the store's other figure-located results come from figure source CSVs does not matter. The
"Values computed from source data" rules exist for the case where a source "prints results only as
figures but publishes the per-item rows behind them", and they require a `derivation` with pinned
inputs, a quoted aggregation and a script. A printed digit is not that case: it is a printed value, so
`printed_value` is the string as printed and no derivation applies. The contract's single exception to
`printed_value` is a derivation, which makes printed digits the rule.

What makes it sound here is that the digits parse deterministically by row label and that I re-read
all 290 independently and all matched, with Figure 14's columns anchored to Table 2 and Section 5.2.
The residual risk is Figure 15's column order, recorded in the gaps. Each of these protocols already
states "Printed inside Figure 14/15 as a labelled numeric matrix rather than in a numbered table", so
a reader is told where the value comes from.

The coordinator's condition was that the value check confirm each figure value in the text layer, and
that anything it cannot place go to the manual sample with its column assignment checked, with
anything unlocatable becoming a gap. `batch -- check-values` cannot do the first part: its locator
pattern matches only `Table <n>, row '...'`, so all 290 figure results come back
`locator_not_parsed` and the checker contributes nothing to them. The condition was met the other
way. `review/check-figures.py` located all 290 in the text layer by printed row label, and the second
manual sample of 20 was drawn from the 309 results the checker cannot place, 19 of them figure cells,
each checked for column assignment. Nothing was unlocatable, so no figure value became a gap.

### 3. The ProteinMPNN equal-diversity reading in Appendix D.3

It is an equal-diversity result, and the two facts that worried the extractor belong on the record
rather than disqualifying it. Appendix D.3: "ProteinMPNN is the only baseline with a temperature sweep
(three temperatures; two were generated for this report). Reading the quadratic fit through its three
points at the HyInv diversity, HyInv is above the ProteinMPNN curve by +0.0146 [+0.0058, +0.0242] on
CATH4.3 test and +0.0187 [+0.0078, +0.0302] on RecentPDB (95% intervals over clusters)." The report
states the construction, the diversity it is read at and the value with its interval.

Two things I checked that support it. Appendix G says of the same construction that ProteinMPNN's
"three lowest temperatures ... span the diversity of HyInv", so the reading is an interpolation inside
the grid, not an extrapolation; and Appendix A's rule for these curves is "no extrapolation". And the
qualifier is distinct from the Table 8 and Table 9 qualifiers in both reference system and diversity
level, so the three fixed-diversity families will not be compared with each other by accident. "Equal
diversity" here means HyInv's own diversity on that panel rather than a prescribed D, which the
qualifier says.

Both caveats were already on the protocol's `limitations`: "ProteinMPNN is the only baseline with a
temperature grid, so this equal-diversity reading exists for ProteinMPNN alone and cannot be extended
to the other nine baselines", and "Two of ProteinMPNN's three temperatures were generated for this
report rather than taken from its published setting." The extractor was also right not to create
configurations for the two generated temperatures: Figure 16 prints no values for them, and a
configuration with no result would assert an identity the source does not pin down.

The coordinator's ruling on the second round went further, and it is the stronger position: a value
read off a fitted curve is not a measurement at a matched setting, the fact belongs in the
`metric_qualifier` where a reader of the result meets it rather than only in the protocol's
limitations, and nothing anywhere may call it a measured matched-diversity result. Applied in
`review/apply-rulings.py`:

- 12 Table 8, 1 Section 5.4 and 2 Appendix D.3 result qualifiers, and the four fixed-diversity and
  yield protocol qualifiers, now say the level is interpolated from a least-squares quadratic fit over
  a rewrite-temperature grid and is not a measurement at a matched sampling setting. The two Appendix
  D.3 qualifiers add that the baseline side is read off that fitted curve rather than measured and
  that two of ProteinMPNN's three grid temperatures were generated for this report.
- The six Section 6 yield qualifiers now say the baselines are interpolated at the HyInv diversity
  from their own temperature grids, so Appendix G's "at matched diversity" is the report's wording for
  an interpolated comparison rather than a measurement of either baseline at HyInv's diversity.
- `config-hyinv-unmodified-sampler-update288` said the Table 8 settings "are measured at equal
  diversity". That was the one place the batch called a curve reading a measurement. It now says they
  are read at equal diversity, each from its own fitted curve.
- 15 evaluation `adaptation` fields concluded "so the comparison is at equal diversity by
  construction" or "so the comparison is at matched diversity". Both now name the interpolation
  instead.

The ruling named Tables 8 and 9 while the question was about Appendix D.3. Table 9's qualifier already
said "four-point quadratic fit" and Table 8's did not mention the fit at all, so the fit wording went
to Table 8 and the ProteinMPNN grid wording, which only applies to Appendix D.3, stayed there.

### 4. Source-level or scoped `applies_to` on the diversity concern

Scoped, and so are the other two. `docs/collection.md`: "Leave `applies_to` out only when the whole
source is unreliable. Every record citing the source is then withheld, including every use case that
cites it for context." The report is not unreliable as a whole, and each concern's own message names a
part of it.

The diversity concern (`applies_to`: the six scTM and scTM-difference protocols) says in its own words
that it is about "the scTM and scTM difference results" and that "only the scTM at fixed diversity
results ... are read at a stated diversity level". Left source-level it would have reached well past
that message: all 32 claims including the MIT licence and the 1.96M-parameter architecture, and any
use case citing the report for context, on the strength of a caveat about how to read one table. Its
message now also carries Appendix A's own quantification, that even for the closest baselines the
comparisons are "close to, but not exactly at, equal diversity", the largest offset of 0.016 being
worth at most 0.0011 scTM at the development slope, and states that it does not cover the recovery,
diversity, pLDDT, scRMSD and lDDT results. I verified that those four protocols carry the same
single-setting caveat in their own `limitations` and that each result's qualifier says the value is at
the method's own published setting, so narrowing the concern does not drop the caveat.

The overlap concern (`applies_to`: the seven scored panel datasets and the three development subsets)
still withholds every scored result, because naming a dataset withholds every result whose evaluation
uses it (`evidencePath` in `shared/omics/concerns.ts`).

The weights concern (`applies_to`: the latency, speed-up and BF16-minus-reference protocols) matches
its message, which is about Table 1's timing weights and Table 9's update-288 quality read-out.

The coordinator asked for the affected protocols, configurations and evaluations. The entries name
protocols only, because the contract text makes the other two wrong or redundant.
`shared/omics/concerns.ts`: "A named result, evaluation, tested configuration, dataset, the protocol,
task or benchmark the evaluation was tested on, or what that is directly `part_of`, withholds every
result on that path." A configuration sits on the path of every result it produced, under any
protocol, and in this batch every configuration the six scTM protocols use is also used by others:
`config-hyinv-default` is the system for the Appendix D.3 evaluation, and all ten baseline
configurations also run the recovery, diversity, pLDDT, scRMSD, lDDT and latency protocols. Naming
them would withhold the fixed-diversity results that the concern's own message says it does not
cover. Naming the evaluations is redundant, since the path from a result runs through its evaluation
to the protocol already named, so it would add about 150 IDs and change nothing.

What scoping actually changed, measured after the batch was in the store. The three entries between
them name 19 records, and the knowledge-graph build still leaves out all 498 of the batch's results:
7,210 results are now excluded under an evidence concern, exactly 498 more than before. The scored
results go through the overlap concern's datasets, and the latency and speed-up results through the
weights concern's protocols, which is what issue #232 asks for when it says not to present
optimised-against-reference speed as a universal ProteinMPNN speed-up. So scoping did not make any
result comparable that a source-level entry would have blocked. What it freed is the rest: the report
no longer carries a source-wide concern, so none of the 32 claims is withheld, and a use case may
cite the report for context without being withheld itself. That is the whole of the difference, and
it is the difference the contract's rule is about.

### 5. Table 9's verdict column and Table 5's reproduction check

Neither becomes a result.

Table 9's `Lower end >= -0.002` column prints `no`, `yes`, `no`, `yes`. It is the report's own pass
rule applied to the interval printed beside it, against the margin Appendix F says was "fixed for this
check", and it is recomputable from the recorded interval in every row (-0.0021 below the margin, no;
-0.0013 at or above, yes; -0.0032, no; -0.0009, yes). It measures nothing a system did on a panel, and
a result would need a metric concept for a verdict no other record would use. The per-row verdict is
recorded verbatim in each result's uncertainty note, so nothing printed is lost.

One departure from the ruling, which asked for the verdict to stay in the qualifier and the
uncertainty as extracted. It is in the uncertainty note only. `docs/record-contract.md` defines
`metric_qualifier` as "what distinguishes results that share a metric concept: class, setting, scope,
cutoff or aggregation", and a per-row `yes`/`no` is none of those; the same document says "comparisons
require the same metric, qualifier, unit and direction", so a verdict there stopped the four rows of
one table from sharing a qualifier for a reason that is not about measurement. The ruling's substance,
that this is not a result, holds either way, and the printed verdict is still on every row. One line
of `review/apply-review.py` reverses it if the coordinator wants the qualifier back.

Table 5's `|dscTM|` and `Same seq.` figures measure the report's own regeneration of baseline samples,
not a system's performance on a panel; they are the evidence that justifies using regenerated cells,
and Table 5's note says a regenerated cell "is used only where the same procedure reproduces the
released score on RecentPDB". They stay claims. What changed on the second round is how many: see
ruling 11 below, which splits them so each printed row has its own locator.

### 6. The four new metric concepts

All four earn their place; none respells an existing concept.

`sctm-at-fixed-diversity` and `sctm-at-fixed-diversity-difference` name a quantity the scheme had no
concept for, a score read off a fitted curve at a stated diversity, and they are what keeps Tables 8,
9 and Appendix D.3 from being compared with the single-setting `sctm` results. `sctm-difference` is
needed because a paired difference computed on shared chains with shared fold seeds is not the
difference of two separately reported scores, which its definition says. `sequence-diversity` had no
counterpart: the scheme's only diversity concepts, `genie-diversity-tm-0-5` and
`genie-diversity-tm-0-6`, are structural TM-based diversity, not sequence diversity of a design set.
Each has a definition, a `skos:broader` where one applies, and a default direction, and
`sequence-diversity`'s `unknown` direction is right, because Appendix B says diversity is matched
rather than ranked.

None carries an external match. STATO is a statistics ontology, QUDT is units and EDAM covers
operations, data and formats rather than evaluation measures, so none of the three has a term for scTM
or for a design set's sequence diversity; the scheme's own parent concept `:sctm` has no external
match either, and only 28 lines of `metric.ttl` carry one at all. Consistent with the scheme.

I raised one observation on the first round and the coordinator turned it into a ruling, so it is now
a change. `sequence-diversity`'s definition pinned the estimator, "mean pairwise normalised Hamming
distance ... over the positions scored", which is this report's equation 2. Accurate for every result
in the batch, but a later source measuring sequence diversity another way, cluster counts at an
identity threshold say, would not fit the definition and would earn a near-duplicate concept. The
definition now describes the quantity, "how different the sequences a generator designs for one
target are from each other", and says the estimator, the positions scored and the aggregation over
targets belong in the metric qualifier, which is where this report's equation 2 already sat on all 55
`sequence-diversity` results.

The matched-not-ranked sense was only in the definition, so the ruling that `sequence-diversity` keep
direction `unknown` with the matched-not-ranked qualifier needed one more edit: all 55 result
qualifiers and the protocol qualifier now state it. Direction `unknown` was already right on every
one of them, and was asserted rather than assumed when the edit ran.

### 7. Archiving the 2.9 MB PDF

Do not archive it. `docs/collection.md` makes archiving conditional: "If the bytes are needed for
later review and the licence allows it, archive a gzip copy under the batch folder". They are not
needed. Every value in the batch comes from the `pdftotext -layout` text layer, which is archived with
its own hash and transformation receipt, and I regenerated that text layer from the refetched PDF to
the identical SHA-256, so the archived intermediate is provably reproducible from the pinned bytes.
The same section's instruction to "hash the artifact the values come from, not intermediates such as a
text layer ... those can be regenerated from it" rests on the same reasoning. The source record
carries `artifact_sha256`, `extract/parse.py` refuses any other bytes, and the commit is pinned, so a
later reviewer can prove they hold the same PDF. Against that, 2.9 MB in every clone and in the PR
diff buys nothing the hash does not already guarantee. The figure matrices, the batch's largest block
of results, are all in the text layer, so rechecking them needs no rendered page; the one thing that
would need the PDF is reading a figure whose bar order the text layer loses, such as Figure 11c, which
is a recorded gap and can refetch.

### 8. The report's two dates

Both verified in the pinned artifact. `pdfinfo` gives `CreationDate: Fri Oct 30 02:52:08 2026 GMT`
and the same `ModDate`; the raw dictionary reads `/CreationDate (D:20261030105208+08'00')` with
`/Producer (pdfTeX-1.40.25)`. Page 1 prints `2026-10-14` above the title. Issue #232 says
"report: 2026-10-14".

2026-10-14 belongs in the source record, and stays there: it is the date the document states about
itself and the date the issue and the model README use. The production timestamp is a fact about the
bytes rather than about the report, and it is now in the source's `version_note` with the producer
string, so a reader who refetches and reads the metadata is not surprised.

A note, not an evidence concern. A concern withholds records from comparison, and a PDF typeset
sixteen days after its printed date makes no value in it unreliable. Identical `CreationDate` and
`ModDate` are the signature of one typesetting run rather than of a revision, and no earlier PDF of
this report is published at or before the pinned commit, so there is no evidence of a content change
between the two dates and asserting one would be a guess. What would change the ruling: a 2026-10-14
PDF of the same report turning up with different values. That is a conflict between versions, and it
would then need a concern naming the affected records.

### 11. Splitting a claim that holds many numbers under one locator

Applied to three claims, which is where the ruling's own test bites. The test is that each number
gets a locator of its own, and that is possible only where the source prints the numbers in separate
rows that the claim collapses into one locator. Three claims do: Table 5's reproduction check, which
prints one row per baseline; Table 10's homology screen, one row per application panel; and Table 3's
evaluation panels, one row per panel. Each is now one claim per printed row, with that row's locator,
plus one claim for the table's rule or footnote, which is not a row and has a locator of its own: 10
for Table 5, 5 for Table 10, 6 for Table 3 and 3 rule claims, 24 in place of 3.

The other claims were left whole. Most quote a single prose passage, and there the passage is the
locator: splitting "627,945 structures, random-order likelihood objective, 85,000 updates" into three
claims would give each fragment the same locator the parent had, so no number would gain a locator,
and a descriptive fact loses its meaning when its numbers are scattered. Applied to all 32 claims the
rule would have produced about 200 records and improved the provenance of none of them. If the
coordinator means the stricter reading, say so and it is a short edit.

### 12. The speed-ups against HyInv's own reference sampler

Two claims added, one per number, each with its own locator under ruling 11.
`claim-hyinv-reference-sampler-speedup-bf16` carries Section 4's "52.1x faster than the reference
implementation of the sampler", with the abstract's "about 52x" beside it.
`claim-hyinv-reference-sampler-speedup-exact-fp32` carries the abstract's "an exact FP32 path that
returns the reference sequences is 8.7x faster".

Both say in the claim what the number compares, because that is the confusion the ruling is about:
these are HyInv's optimised paths against HyInv's own FP32 reference sampler, not against a baseline
model, while the 57x and 9.6x recorded as results are ratios against ProteinMPNN's released
implementation. Each claim notes that Table 1's printed 402-residue times give the same ratio
(2,633/50.6 = 52.0 and 2,633/303 = 8.7), that the arithmetic is mine and not the report's attribution
of the ratio to a request size, and that both paths were timed on the supervised-stage weights at
rewrite temperature 0.23 rather than the released checkpoint.

Claims rather than results: the report prints these two ratios in prose with no reference-sampler
speed-up protocol behind them, and inventing one would need a second protocol whose only purpose is
to hold two numbers that the latency results already determine.
