**Ruling on the link: `supersedes` is wrong; `companion_of` is right.** The extractor proposed
`supersedes` from the new record to the stored one, on the precedent of
`lit-protein-20261011-prime-source-prime2026`. That precedent does not fit. Checked at review:
`prime-2026` has the same URL *and the same `artifact_sha256`* as the record that supersedes it. It
is a thin duplicate record of the same bytes, with status `discovered`, replaced by a fuller one.

and the older one stays `source_checked`.

`shared/omics/relations.ts` defines `companion_of` as "Source to the paper it belongs with: its
repository, leaderboard, supplement **or another version**. One study; never merges results", and
`supersedes` as "A newer record replacing an older one". The store already uses the former for this
exact case: `bacformer-20261115-source-preprint-v2 companion_of bacformer-20261115-source-preprint-v3`.
This batch itself links the bioRxiv preprint to the version of record with `companion_of`, so
`supersedes` for the preview made the batch inconsistent with its own treatment of the preprint.

Nothing in the code keys off either edge, so this is a semantics question, not a behaviour one. I
checked: `supersedes` appears in `shared/omics/relations.ts` only as a declaration, and every rule
that hides, demotes or blocks a record keys off `status` (`publicRecords` in
`scripts/omics/schema.ts`, `shared/omics/concerns.ts`, `published-comparisons.ts`,
`catalogue-query.ts`). `use-cases.ts`'s `supersedes_id` is a field on use-case mappings and is
unrelated.

## How the values were compared

Three passes, each able to fail independently, plus two negative controls.

1. `npm run -s batch -- check-values` on the regenerated text layers. It reproduced the extractor's
   figures exactly: 12 `in_cell`, 44 `in_row`, 16 `locator_not_parsed`, with no `not_in_row`, no
   `wrong_column` and no `overclaimed_in_row`. Report in `value-check.tsv`. The 44 `in_row` rows are
   Supplementary Table 3 cells whose column the repository checker cannot name, because the two
   "Finemapped" headers are printed over two lines; the 16 unparsed locators are Fig. 4b's 12 bar
   labels and the 4 prose counts.
2. `review/check_values_independent.py`, written for this pass, importing nothing from
   `extract/build.py`. Supplementary Table 3 is printed rotated, so this script reads the page's
   word bounding boxes (`pdftotext -bbox-layout`) rather than a text dump. It recovers the seven
   column bands from the header words' y extents, pairing each "Finemapped" header with whichever
   of "coding" and "non-coding" overlaps it in y; clusters the body words into eight row bands by
   x centre; and places every numeric token in the cell whose column centre is nearest, rejecting
   any token more than 45 per cent of a column pitch from its nearest column or less than 25 per
   cent of a pitch clear of the runner-up. Column order is therefore measured from the page, which
   is the check `build.py` could not make: it asserted the order positionally. Results are matched
   to cells through each `source_locator`'s text, not through record IDs. Fig. 4b labels must be
   the first number printed after their series label on the same line, and a line naming a series
   without a number (panel d's legend) does not count. The four rare variant counts are matched
   against the two sentences that print them, with the capture position deciding which count is
   DeepRVAT with GPN-Star annotations and which the original method. Result: 68 in cell, 4 in
   sentence, 0 failures. Report in `review/value-check-independent.tsv`.
3. A hand transcription of all 56 Supplementary Table 3 cells and all 12 Fig. 4b bar labels, read
   from the two pages rendered at 190 dpi with `pdftoppm` and typed into
   `review/manual-transcription.tsv` before any comparison, then compared by `review/cmp_manual.py`:
   68 values, 0 problems. This is the pass that settles the column assignment, and it covers every
   one of the 44 cells the repository checker could only place at row level. The remaining 4 results
   are the prose counts, read directly from the sentence on page 5.

Two negative controls show pass 2 is a cell check and not a row or page check:

| Control | Mutation | Result |

| swap | `GPN-Star default` and `GPN-MSA (no species curation)` ClinVar values exchanged; Fig. 4b `PhastCons (V)` set to `5.3`, which is `PhyloP (M)`'s printed label | all three fail, `review/control-swap.tsv` |

## Rulings on the extractor's questions

1. **`supersedes` for the preview record?** No. `companion_of`, on the stored record, pointing at
   the version of record. See above.
2. **One result per `mean (max)` cell, or two?** One, as built. The cell prints one measurement with
   a parenthetical second statistic over the same three runs, not two columns as in the GPN-MSA
   table, so one `printed_value` with the maximum in `source_statistic` and the whole cell in
   `printed_source_cell` is the faithful shape. The 59 false `check-values` failures that two
   results produced are a consequence of that, not the reason.
3. **Supplementary Tables 5-8 as evaluations?** No. They give the count, proportion and odds ratio
   of each Ensembl consequence class among one model's top 0.1% most constrained variants, and two
   of the four compare GPN-Star with itself. There is no ground truth and, in two cases, no
   comparator, so they are not evaluations of a capability. The numbers the text quotes from them
   (33% and 170-fold, 26% and 2.4-fold, 8% and 0.35-fold, 1.14-fold, 1.52-fold, 3.89-fold) are in
   `gpn-star-20261116-claim-prioritized-consequences` and were each located in the Supplementary
   Notes at review.
4. **Fig. 4b's bar labels as results?** Yes. They are text tokens in the PDF, each the first number
   after its series label on the same line, confirmed both by parsing the text layer and by reading
   the page rendered at 190 dpi. Nothing is read from a pixel, a bar length or a colour. The pair
   PhastCons (M) and PhastCons (V) both print 1.9, and only the same-line label separates them,
   which is exactly what the checker requires.
5. **Three Fig. 4b GPN-Star configurations, or attach to the 200M setups?** Keep them separate. The
   paper says "we focus here on the largest models (200 million parameters)" of the three human
   models, and the collection releases exactly three human repositories, all 200M, so the inference
   is a strong one. It is still an inference, and `docs/collection.md` is explicit: "An unidentified
   model version stays at family level and is not assigned to a specific checkpoint." The
   configurations already carry `missing_metadata.checkpoint` with the reason and a
   `provenance_note` naming that sentence, which is the right record of a strong but unstated link.
6. **Is the GPN-Star model the right parent for the "MSA Transformer-like architecture" row?** Yes.
   The table's own caption defines the row as an architecture change inside this framework: "The MSA

7. **Reuse the two TraitGym dataset records, which pin a revision GPN-Star does not name?** Reuse,

8. **Extract Fig. 5a's 15 labels?** No, as above.
9. **Apply the recommended changes to `vep-uc-20261014-model-gpn-star`?** Not in this pass. They are

## Protocol scoping: the ablation table is correctly its own protocols

The seven Supplementary Table 3 columns are separate protocols from anything the store already
holds, and the source says why in one sentence at the end of its Evaluation Benchmarks section:

> For each benchmark presented in this paper, we only include variants where all the compared models
> have available predictions when computing the performance metrics.

That restriction defines a different scored set from any third-party protocol, and each new protocol
record quotes it in its `procedure`. Checked against the closest candidates:

- **TraitGym** (`lit-genomics-20261011-traitgym-protocol-*`) computes "AUPRC per chromosome and
  averaged with weights by sample size" with a bootstrap standard error. GPN-Star computes a plain
  AUPRC over the restricted set. Different metric implementation and different split, so separate
  protocols, even though the OMIM and fine-mapped non-coding columns reuse TraitGym's datasets.
  `ag-atlas-20261019-protocol-traitgym-*` adds AlphaGenome Atlas positional filtering on top, so it
  is further away still.
- **GPN-MSA's ablation table** (`gfm-guides-20261014-gpnmsa-protocol-ablation-*`) is the same shape
  from the sibling paper. Its ClinVar column is AUROC against gnomAD common missense controls;
  GPN-Star's is AUPRC against ClinVar benign controls on release 20220924. Different metric and
  different controls, so a new protocol and a new ClinVar dataset are right.
- **COSMIC** is the one column where the two papers share a dataset, and the batch reuses
  `gfm-guides-20261014-gpnmsa-data-cosmic-frequent-vs-gnomad-common-missense` rather than making a
  new one. The Supplementary Information says the COSMIC data were "downloaded data processed by
  ref. [22]", the GPN-MSA paper, with the same MutantCensus v98 0.1% threshold and gnomAD common
  missense controls. The reuse is right and the protocol still differs, by the all-models-scored
  restriction.
- **HGMD** was "processed similarly to the Mendelian traits dataset in TraitGym", not downloaded
  from it, so a new dataset record is right. The store's HGMD protocols
  (`gfm-guides-20261014-esm1b-protocol-hgmd-*`) are missense AUROC, not regulatory AUPRC.
- **Fine-mapped coding** comes "from the same source" as the non-coding set "but only matched MAF,
  similar to GeneticsGym", so a new dataset record is right there too.

None of the seven duplicates a stored protocol. A near relative worth naming, outside the ablation
table: `gpn-star-20261116-protocol-gnomad-rare-vs-common-odds-ratio` (Fig. 4b) is close to
`gfm-guides-20261014-gpnmsa-protocol-ablation-gnomad-odds-ratio`, and the source says so explicitly:
"We follow the evaluation protocol of GPN-MSA paper [22]". They stay separate records because
GPN-Star names gnomAD v3.1.2, groups small consequence categories and subsamples rare variants
within each category, where the GPN-MSA record subsamples over all variant types on an unnamed
release. Their comparison fields differ, so the graph will not compare them automatically, which is
the intended outcome. This is a lead for anyone later reconciling the two papers' gnomAD numbers,
not a defect in either record.
