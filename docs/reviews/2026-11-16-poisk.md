## How the values were compared

Three passes, each able to fail independently.

1. `npm run -s batch -- check-values` rerun against the regenerated text layer. It reproduced the
   extractor's figures exactly, 36 `in_cell`, 36 `in_row`, 6 `locator_not_parsed`, with no
   `not_in_row`, no `wrong_column` and no `overclaimed_in_row`, and wrote a `value-check.tsv`
   byte-identical to the extractor's.
2. `review/check_values_independent.py`, written for this pass, importing nothing from
   `extract/build_batch.py`. It asserts the text layer's hash, then for each of Tables 1, 2, 7 and 8
   locates the table's own header lines, takes each column's span from the character offsets of the
   label text in them, and assigns every numeric token on a body row to the column whose centre is
   nearest. A row is rejected unless the token count equals the column count, each token's nearest
   column is the one in its own left-to-right position, the distance is within five characters and
   the runner-up column loses by at least three. It also asserts that Table 1's four sub-columns sit
   under their printed group labels, that Table 7's `Coverage` group spans the five coverage columns
   and does not reach the `Ties` column, and that Table 8's body holds exactly 27 construct rows.
   Results are matched to cells through each `source_locator`'s text, not through record IDs. Result:
   72 of 78 checked in cell, 6 prose values checked against the sentence their locator names, 0
   failures. Report in `review/value-check-independent.tsv`.
3. A hand transcription of all 42 printed cells of Tables 2 and 7 into
   `review/manual-transcription.tsv`, typed from the page before comparison, then compared by
   `review/cmp_manual.py`. Every cell matched on `printed_value`, and every `numeric_value` agrees
   with its printed value under its unit.

Two negative controls show pass 2 is a column check and not a row check:

| Control | Mutation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| swap | Protenix-v2 and Boltz-2 Table 7 `50%` values exchanged | both fail, `review/control-swap.tsv` |
| shift | AlphaFold-Multimer Table 7 `50%` set to `0.643`, its own `75%` cell; Boltz-2 Table 2 set to `56.5`, the row below | both fail, `review/control-shift.tsv` |

The shift control is the one that matters. `0.643` is present in the AlphaFold-Multimer row and
`56.5` is a printed Table 2 value, so a row-level check passes both. The geometric check rejects
them, naming the value the column actually holds.

`review/audit_fields.py` checked every result's `metric`, `metric_direction`, `unit` and
`denominator` against its protocol, and every evaluation's nine `comparison` fields for
completeness. Metric, direction and denominator agree on all 78. None of the 66 evaluations has a
null comparison field. The only unit divergence is the deliberate one discussed under question 4.

### The 36 unverified cells

The extractor's priority question was that 36 results sat at `in_row`, so their column assignment
rested on the extraction script's own header assertions. Those 36 are the 30 Table 7 coverage cells
(six engines by `100%`, `75%`, `50%`, `25%`, `10%`) and the 6 Table 2 cells. Every one of them was
checked twice over, by geometry in pass 2 and by hand in pass 3, and all 36 are correct. Two details
that a weaker check would have missed:

- Table 2's printed row order is `AlphaFold-Multimer, OpenFold3, Boltz-2, IntelliFold v2,
  Protenix-v1, Protenix-v2`, ascending by recovery rate. It is not the row order of Tables 1 and 7.
  The records follow the printed labels, not the position.
- Table 7's `Ties` column sits outside the `Coverage` group. The group label is centred over the five
  coverage columns and its span does not reach `Ties`, which the checker asserts, so the tie
  fractions cannot be read as a coverage level.

Reading across Table 7 also reproduces the paper's own statement that "accuracy increases
monotonically as coverage tightens for every engine": all six rows rise left to right, which an
off-by-one column assignment would be unlikely to preserve.

## Rulings on the questions raised

### 1. Version 2 of the preprint: stays on version 1

Version 2 was posted 2026-11-14, one day inside the cutoff. It is retrievable: the full PDF came
back over plain `curl` at the versioned URL, SHA-256
`aeaec593d7aa2d42382a49bd6fadbbccabaa6c084843ea1c7f0c91097dedb0c2`, 5,262,433 bytes, text layer
`82a99bc2e061b7247cf65f7bc9450924b7af5be7ab3ef283d2284ba787ea629c`. Only the JATS feed is
front-matter-only for it, which is what the extractor hit.

Comparing every numeric token of the two text layers, after dropping the per-page bioRxiv banner,
leaves exactly three differences:

- the affiliation marker on one author, `Andrea Roncoli2` becoming `Andrea Roncoli2,3`, with a new
  footnote `3 Utrecht University, Faculty of Science - Chemistry, Bijvoet Centre, the Netherlands`;
- in Section 3.1, `publicly available with full field descriptions at
  https://zenodo.org/records/19883049` becoming `https://zenodo.org/records/21775623`;
- in Section 4, `We release the POISK dataset at https://zenodo.org/records/19883049` becoming
  `https://zenodo.org/records/21775623`.

Every other difference is pagination and reflow. **No table value and no prose value differs**, so
there is no finding of changed numbers and nothing to re-pin for. The batch stays on version 1,
which is the artifact the values were read from and whose text layer is reproducible. The source
record's limitation, which said version 2's full text was not available and its PDF not retrieved,
is now false and has been corrected, with the previous text in
`metadata-correction-57dfd6c270a545104287`. No version 2 source record was created: nothing is
extracted from it, so it would carry no records, and the hashes above plus this review are the
durable statement that it was checked.

The Zenodo link change is worth noting for its own sake. From version 2 onward the paper releases
the dataset at version 1.1, while the folding predictions it benchmarks were run against version
1.0.1, which is what every evaluation here pins. The two statements are not in conflict, but a later
reader following the paper's link will land on a release whose table the published predictions were
not computed from. Version 1.1's own description says so.

### 2. `publication_status: preprint` stands

The primary source does not support a published-paper record:

- The bioRxiv API reports `"published": "NA"` for both version 1 and version 2.
- Every page of the pinned PDF carries the banner "(which was not certified by peer review)".
- The line "40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026). Track on
  Evaluations and Datasets." on page 1 is printed by the conference's own style file and sits on the
  preprint itself. It is not a publisher record of acceptance, and the paper also carries the full
  NeurIPS Paper Checklist.

The explorer site's statement is a project-site statement. Its hero section renders "Read the paper
↗ · Accepted at NeurIPS 2026", so the phrase quoted in `claim-publication-status` is verbatim and
only the separator is transcribed. The split the extractor chose is right: `publication_status`
stays `preprint` on the source and the acceptance stays a claim attributed to the explorer page. If
a proceedings version appears, it is a new source record, not an edit to this one.

### 3. `paired-accuracy`: definition widened, no new concept

The concept fits structurally. POISK's "pairwise accuracy" is the proportion of matched pairs in
which the model scores the positive member above its matched control, which is what the concept
describes. What did not fit was the wording: the definition read "matched element/control sequence
pairs ... scores the regulatory element above its matched control", so a stored antibody result
pointed at a definition about regulatory elements.

A new concept was the wrong fix. `docs/collection.md` says never to add a concept that only respells
an existing one, and a `paired-discrimination-accuracy` beside `paired-accuracy` would split one
statistic in two, so a future antibody result and a regulatory-element result could never be
compared even where they should be. Widening is monotone: a regulatory element scored above its
matched control remains an instance of the general definition, so none of the 13 existing DART-Eval
results changes meaning.

`data/vocab/metric.ttl` now reads:

> Proportion of matched positive/control pairs in which the model scores the positive member above
> its matched control. The pair population, how each control was matched to its positive, and how
> exact ties are handled go in metric_qualifier.

with `pairwise accuracy` added as an alt label and a scope note recording DART-Eval as the
definition's origin, this review as the widening, and that results are comparable only when their
`metric_qualifier` agrees. That last point is the guard, and it already holds in the data: the 13
DART-Eval results carry no qualifier, while all POISK results carry one naming the ipSAE score, the
retained population and the half-credit tie rule, so the two families cannot pool. No STATO, QUDT or
EDAM term for a matched-pair discrimination accuracy was found; the concept keeps `skos:broader
:accuracy` as its anchor.

### 4. A standalone standard deviation gets no metric concept

Table 8's across-target standard deviation stays as the six `claim-screen-target-spread-*` records.
A `standard-deviation` metric concept would be contentless: it would say that a number is a
dispersion without saying of what, and because comparison keys on metric, qualifier, unit and
direction, a dispersion of top-1 accuracy over antigen constructs would become formally comparable
with a dispersion of anything else reported under the same concept. Its direction is also undefined;
a small across-target spread means a model is consistent across targets, which is not better or
worse in the way `higher` and `lower` mean. And the across-seed spread already has a proper home in
`uncertainty`, so adopting a metric concept for the other axis would put one table's two spreads into
two different mechanisms.

The six claims each name their printed cell, and each screening result's `uncertainty.note`
cross-references its engine's across-target value, so neither number is lost and neither can be read
as the other. Checked against the page: the across-seed row prints 1.5, 0.8, 1.7, 1.6, 1.9, 1.4 and
the across-target row 13.4, 3.3, 19.0, 11.4, 19.5, 20.6, each in the printed engine order, and that
is what the `uncertainty` objects and the claims hold.

### 5. Two printings of one value: keep both

Accepted as extracted. The 50% coverage accuracy appears as Table 1's `0.87` and Table 7's `0.868`,
and the 1-in-50 top-1 figure as Table 1's `0.29` and Table 8's `28.9`. Both printings sit on the same
evaluation, each with its own locator and a rounding note on the Table 1 record, so nothing is
double-counted and the coarser printing stays findable at the place a reader of Table 1 would look
for it.

The unit divergence the extractor flagged is a feature. The six Table 1 top-1 results carry `unit:
fraction` while their protocol declares `percent`, because Table 1 prints `0.29` where Table 8 prints
`28.9`. Recording the printed value with its printed unit is what the contract asks for, and the
differing units are exactly what stops a fraction and a percentage of the same quantity being pooled.
This is the only place in the batch where a result's unit differs from its protocol's, and
`review/audit_fields.py` confirms it is those six and nothing else.

Not defensible, and the primary source settles it from the two Zenodo records' own file lists:

| `poisk_dataset_column_description.csv` | 4,707 bytes, md5 `9aca92a984c931bdec9e86fa62d41a06` | 5,415 bytes, md5 `24fffaa865572d6b7723d5ee7c153bdd` |

The release tables are different files. Version 1.1's description says it "corrects errors found in
version 1.0.1" and gives 93,193 rows and 47 columns, against the manuscript's 89,074 records for the

2026-10-09 plasma ctDNA review and the 2026-10-22 phenotype-perturbation review, and again today in
`docs/reviews/2026-11-15-perturbpfn.md`, is that it requires identical data. The link is removed.

`supersedes` is not recorded in its place. Version 1.0.1 is not replaced: it is still the release the
published folding predictions were run against, in version 1.1's own words "These predictions were
run against dataset version 1.0.1, not against this release", and it is what all 66 evaluations pin.

What is left is a dataset record with no outgoing edge and the relationship stated in prose. Version
1.1's `scope_note` now carries the file-level evidence above, the reason neither relation is
recorded, and the shared folding-metrics archive; `claim-v1-1-folding-version` and
`claim-v1-1-folding-metrics-files` already carried the release's own statements, and both check out
against the re-retrieved record, including the md5 identity of the shared archive. `reuse_note` would
have been the natural field but it is declared for `dataset_subset` only. Previous values in
`metadata-correction-a0b1abee1791f618f866` and `metadata-correction-0b86aff1c266a6acf5a5`.

### 7. Model lineage, and one record the store already had

AlphaFold-Multimer and IntelliFold stay unlinked, and no record for either exists in the store under
any other ID: the only `multimer` occurrences in `data/entities` are inside other records' prose,
and there is no IntelliFold record at all. This source asserts no lineage for either, and the
contract wants `variant_of` and `family` edges backed by a source-checked association claim, so
asserting that AlphaFold-Multimer is an AlphaFold 2 extension on this source would be unsupported.

The extractor's search missed one record. The store does already hold OpenFold3, as
`esmfold2-2026-runs-n-poses-method-openfold3-msa`, a `configuration` named "OpenFold3 (MSA)" whose
`links` array is empty, so it has no parent model. That does not make the new record wrong: a
configuration is not a model record, and a family-level OpenFold3 is what the six-engine comparison
needs. It does mean the store has an orphan that should point at it. The new record's
`provenance_note` now names that configuration and says the family edge belongs on it;
`metadata-correction-68a8df51bb4063382203` keeps the previous text. The edge itself is not made here
because it changes a stored record outside this batch, so it is left for the coordinator. The
extractor's other identity calls hold: `discovery-model-openfold` is OpenFold, a PyTorch
implementation of AlphaFold 2 and AlphaFold-Multimer, and not OpenFold3;
`lit-structure-20261011-afesmfold-model-alphafold2` is AlphaFold 2 and not AlphaFold-Multimer; and
Protenix-v1 and Protenix-v2 are correctly two configurations of the existing
`ucc-research-model-protenix`, with Boltz-2 on `catalog-model-boltz-2`.

### 8. The unpinned ipSAE implementation: no evidence concern

The paper scores with "a modified implementation of the ipSAE tool
(https://github.com/DunbrackLab/IPSAE)" and names no commit, tag or released code, so the published
values cannot be recomputed and no snapshot would describe what ran. That is real, and it is already
recorded: in `claim-ipsae-definition`, and as a limitation on every affected evaluation.

It does not meet the bar for an evidence concern, which `docs/collection.md` sets at a problem that
should block comparison. `metric_implementation` is one of the nine `comparison` fields, it is
populated on all 66 evaluations, and its text names the modification, so another study's ipSAE
figure, whose implementation string differs, cannot be automatically compared with these. Within the
batch all six engines were scored by the same implementation, and that six-way comparison is what
these results support. Adding a concern would withhold the results from the comparison they do
support in order to block one that is already blocked.

### 9. Table 8's per-construct cells and Table 6: both stay out

Accepted. Table 8's 162 per-construct cells would need 27 antigen-construct subsets and 162 further
evaluations, because each construct is a different candidate pool, and Table 6's 132 cells measure
the benchmark's own label noise rather than model performance on it. Both are recorded in
`scope-audit.jsonl` with reasons and both are retrievable from the same pinned artifact. Table 6's
headline means are kept in `claim-label-noise-sensitivity`, which checks out against Appendix D.2.

## A finding the extractor did not raise: three wrong `metric_implementation` fields

Three protocols carried the full-Fv ipSAE implementation text for an endpoint that does not use
ipSAE at all.

`protocol-epitope-recovery` is a geometric contact criterion. Section 3.3.2: "We counted a record as
recovered when at least one annotated epitope residue had a heavy atom within 4Å of an antibody
heavy atom (Table 2)." No confidence score enters it. Its `metric_qualifier` says this correctly,
which is how the field was caught.

`protocol-consensus-pairwise-accuracy` and `protocol-consensus-pair-coverage` are cross-model pose
agreement. Section 3.3.1: "we calculated DockQ scores [Basu and Wallner, 2016] for the Fv-antigen
interface and defined consensus as two or more models predicting the same pose above a given
threshold. For any given pair, a prediction was considered successful if the binder demonstrated
structural agreement amongst more models than its decoy." The pair is decided by how many models
agree, not by an interface confidence score.

This matters beyond tidiness, because `metric_implementation` is copied onto each evaluation's
`comparison` block and is one of the fields automatic comparison keys on. Left as it was, an
epitope-recovery figure would have advertised an ipSAE implementation it does not use, and so would
have matched or failed to match other studies on a false basis. All three protocols and the ten
evaluations that assess them were corrected: the six `eval-*-epitope-recovery` records and the four
`eval-consensus-k3-*` records. Previous values in `metadata-correction-4343ac8b04c064f6e425`,
`metadata-correction-9fe144bc6b0f7cbb1519` and `metadata-correction-fb8648245db333adbca9`, each
naming the evaluations changed with it. No printed value, metric concept, direction, unit or
denominator changed, so no result is superseded and nothing is marked disputed.

## What else was checked and found correct

- The three counts the issue warned about stay apart: 89,074 extracted records on
  `dataset-poisk-v1-0-1`, 22,494 complexes on the benchmark subset and every pairwise denominator,
  93,193 rows on `dataset-poisk-v1-1`, 540 trials on the screening protocol and 1,232 annotated
  records on the epitope denominator. No result rests on 93,193.
- The putative-negative status is carried on the benchmark, the benchmark subset, all 66 evaluations
  and four claims, in the paper's own words. The 87% figure is correctly the 50%-coverage protocol
  and `claim-coverage-definition` carries Appendix F's statement that it "is not the accuracy over
  the benchmark as a whole", with 0.72 over all pairs, which matches Table 7's `100%` column value of
  0.719 for Protenix-v2 as rounded in the abstract.
- The uncertainty objects are twice-a-sample-standard-deviation across 25 seeds, not confidence
  intervals, matching the Figure 5 legend and NeurIPS checklist item 7. Every result whose table
  prints no spread carries `missing_metadata.uncertainty` with a reason; no missing value is zero.
- Unit concepts were checked against `unit.ttl`'s own scope notes, which draw the line between a
  proportion of items and a dimensionless score: `fraction` is "only for proportions of items
  (correct predictions, targets, complexes)", and a unitless statistic printed times 100 "stays
  unitless with unit_detail 'printed x100'; percent would imply a proportion". Nothing in this batch
  falls on the wrong side. Every `percent` and `fraction` value is a proportion of pairs, records or
  trials: `paired-accuracy` on 36 Table 1 and Table 7 cells as `fraction` on the printed 0 to 1
  scale and on the 2 consensus prose values as `percent` where the paper prints 73.1% and 96.8%,
  `proportion` on the 8 tie and consensus-coverage values as `percent`, `success-rate` on the 6
  epitope cells as `percent` under a column headed "Records (%)", and `top-1-accuracy` and
  `top-10-accuracy` as `fraction` or `percent` according to which table printed them. The only
  dimensionless statistic in the batch is Cohen's d, and all 12 of its results are `unitless`,
  printed on its natural scale, so no `unit_detail` is owed. The store runs the same way for these
  metrics: `cohens-d` is `unitless` on all 59 results elsewhere, `paired-accuracy` `fraction` on all
  13, and `proportion`, `success-rate`, `top-1-accuracy` and `top-10-accuracy` are `percent` or
  `fraction` throughout. The pattern to avoid, a normalised score stored as `percent`, would show on
  a metric like AUROC, which the store holds 4,475 `unitless` to 9 `percent`; this batch reports no
  such metric. `paired-accuracy` carrying two units here is not a comparison hazard, because the two
  groups differ in `metric_qualifier`, in denominator and in how the pair is decided, ipSAE ranking
  against cross-model pose agreement.
- All six configurations carry the printed engine label as `version` with `missing_metadata` for
  checkpoint, checkpoint revision, hyperparameters and code. No value is attached to a concrete
  checkpoint anywhere in the batch.
- Both Zenodo records declare `cc-by-4.0`, and both dataset records and both licence claims record
  that neither source states any position on the patent rights in the underlying filings. The
  re-retrieved records confirm the licence field and the advice to verify critical sequences against
  the original patents.
- The v1.1 claims check out verbatim against the re-retrieved record 21775623: the three audit tiers
  with "REVIEW is not an assertion that a row is false", the 93,193 rows and 47 columns, the three
  target-sequence provenance columns, the chain-pair label warning and the folding-version statement.
  Record 19883049 states no row count, which is why the 1.0.1 dataset record carries
  `missing_metadata.denominator`; that is right.
