## How the values were compared

`npm run -s batch -- check-values` returns `locator_not_parsed` for all 101 results, as the
extractor reported. That is not a failure of the batch: the parser reads `Table N` locators, and
this report contains no tables at all. `value-check.tsv` is kept as the record of that.

All 101 were therefore re-read by `review/check_figure_labels.py`, written for this pass. It uses
neither of the extractor's scripts and differs from them in the way that matters here: the
extractor read the `-layout` text layer line by line and assigned Figure 2's cells to panels by
character offset, while this script reads the word-level geometry of a `pdftotext -bbox-layout`
run and places every value by its own x and y coordinates. Each value is tied to the row whose
label it sits beside, and in a multi-column figure to the column or panel whose printed header it
sits under, so a value cannot be taken from a neighbouring column by a spacing accident. Rows are
matched in printed order, which is what stops a section heading standing in for a data row: both
Figure 2 and Figure 6 print "Ladon" twice, once as a heading with no bar and once as a row.

113 checks, no disagreement: 101 printed values and the 12 printed 95% intervals of Figures 11 and
18 against the results' `uncertainty` bounds.

Three arithmetic constraints were re-derived rather than taken from the extractor:

- Figure 17's five per-lineage Pearson r values average to the bottom chart's overall r for both
  methods: 0.872 against a printed 0.87 for Ladon and 0.594 against 0.600 for CIBERSORT. This
  independently fixes which of the two numbers in each lineage label (`r 0.90 0.78`) is Ladon's,
  which is the one place in that figure where a swap would be invisible.
- Figure 2's pan-cancer bar differs from the mean of its two transfer bars (0.75 against 0.655 for
  Ladon), as the caption requires: "The third plot averages the two pan-cancer C-indices", not the
  two six-cancer means.
- Figure 14's in-figure label, `top 5% 70% vs 59%`, agrees with the page 27 prose the two
  COVID-19-contrast results are taken from.

Manual check, concentrated where column or panel assignment is the only thing that can go wrong.
All 24 Figure 2 cells, all 14 Figure 3 OS and PFS values and all 10 Figure 18 AUCs with their
intervals were read by eye from the `-layout` text layer, along with the Figure 5, 11, 12a, 13 and
17 readouts. All matched.

Figure 2's 24 cells, Figure 3's 14 and Figure 18's 10 AUCs and intervals were then confirmed a
third time against the authors' own figure pages, which carry the numbers as data with explicit
keys: Figure 2's `facet` values `tcga>cptac`, `cptac>tcga` and `mean2` reproduce the batch's panel
assignment for every row, and Figure 3's bars are the exact geometric means of their trial values,
with five trials rather than six for the clinical-only OS summary, as page 13 states. Those pages
are cross-checks only. No value is stored from them.

## Rulings on the extractor's questions

### 1. Hover-only web values: no, and the distinction the extractor drew is the right one

Do not store them. The decisive rule is `collection.md` section 4: "Figures without printed
numbers are not read by eye, by colour or from pixels; record them as a gap, unless the source
publishes the figure's underlying rows", and the route it opens is a `derivation`, which requires
"the aggregation [stated] in its own words" and a deterministic script over pinned inputs. A
per-trial AUROC lifted out of a chart's rendering JSON has no aggregation to apply, so it does not
qualify as a derived value, and storing it as `printed_value` would be false, because nothing
prints it.

The extractor's own practice is consistent with this and worth recording. It stored four claims
from the Figure 6 page's embedded notes, and that page, alone among the nineteen figure pages, is
pinned as a source record with its own hash. Prose statements read out of a hashed artifact at a
stated locator are evidence; chart coordinates in the same file are not printed values. The
boundary is the same one `collection.md` draws, and the batch is on the right side of it.

### 2. Figure 8's unnamed comparator selection: leave it unassigned

Leave it. The PDF prints the panel header `PD-L1 mRNA  AP/T  Plasma-cell  TLS3` and the caption
says only "Ladon is shown above the selectable RNA biomarker reconstructions"; nothing in the PDF
marks which one the printed -2.0 and -2.6 pp gaps belong to, and "never guess" applies.

One correction to the reasoning, and a cheap follow-up. The extractor's guess, PD-L1 mRNA, is
right, but not because it is the first toggle: in the Figure 8 markers page the buttons carry
`data-index` 3, 0, 1 and 2, and PD-L1 mRNA is the one with `aria-pressed="true"`. That is static
markup in a hashed artifact, the same basis as the Figure 6 notes. Assigning the gaps would
therefore be sound evidence, but it needs
`https://www.blank.bio/assets/ladon-white-paper/fig08-km-markers.html` pinned as a source record
(SHA-256 `bfe23719d4da2c5e12565f884e0caf3fda6c3ab39367b879cd02a6ca5a8ff545` on 2026-11-15), which
is an extraction decision rather than a review edit. Left for whoever wants it; the claim is
accurate as it stands, because it says the PDF does not mark the selection.

### 3. Benefit numbers as claims: agree, and do not add a risk-difference concept

Agree. The numbers are differences between two Kaplan-Meier proportions at one time point, read
off Figure 8's panel headers, in one arm of one retrospective trial, with no interval and no
interaction test. The page 19 caption is explicit that the comparison across arms is what carries
the argument: "Comparing the high-low separation across arms distinguishes an observed
treatment-benefit pattern from survival stratification alone." A metric concept would have to
cover a quantity the report never defines as a benchmark measure, on an endpoint with one
observation, and `collection.md` warns against adding a concept that only respells existing ones.
Claims keep the numbers findable without implying they are comparable.

### 4. `:hazard-ratio`: accepted, with the direction note made explicit

Accepted and added to `data/vocab/metric.ttl` as part of this review (see below). Both external
matches check out on the EBI OLS API: `STATO:0000677` is labelled "hazard ratio" (exact) and
`NCIT:C93150` "Hazard Ratio" (close). No existing concept fits: `odds-ratio` is a ratio of odds
from a two-by-two table, which the stored values are not.

`defaultDirection unknown` is right, and for the same reason `odds-ratio` and `magnitude-ratio`
use it: the direction depends on what the groups are. For risk-group separation, which is what
Figure 3 measures, larger is better, and all 14 results correctly set `metric_direction: higher`
themselves; for a treatment effect, smaller is better. I added that sentence to the concept's
`scopeNote`, because a scheme default of `unknown` without it reads as an unresolved gap rather
than a deliberate choice.

### 5. Figure 4 assigned to the Figure 3 combined configuration: accepted

Accepted. The caption says "We used Ladon combined with clinical covariates to score 699 OAK
patients, fitting the risk predictor on other cohorts", and page 13's Methods for that
configuration say the predictor is fitted "using TCGA and other clinical trials" with "the
evaluated trial ... excluded". The description matches on both the system and the fitting pool,
which makes this the only configuration in the batch it can be. The caption still does not say it
is the same fitted predictor, and the result keeps that caveat. The separate protocol is right:
Figure 4 reports Uno's C, not a hazard ratio.

### 6. A new CIBERSORT method record: agree

Agree, and the source text settles it. Page 39: "We reimplemented CIBERSORT and DWLS from their
published algorithms. Both used a signature of the references' mean lineage gene CPM over 1,000
marker genes (200 per lineage). For CIBERSORT, we fit nu-support vector regression on z-scored
gene TPM." The existing record is `ctdnameth-20261009-method-cibersort`, "CIBERSORT (EpiDISH
implementation)", which is nu-SVR run through the EpiDISH R package. Different implementation and
a different signature, built from this study's own single-cell references rather than a published
one, so a different method. The same reasoning covers `method-dwls-reimpl`.

I also checked the wider reuse question the brief raises, since a missed reuse is the kind of
thing that only shows up later: `bulkrnabert`, `bulkformer`, `bmretriever`, `bayesprism`, `dwls`,
`epic`, `abis`, `mammaprint`, `oncotype` and `impres` have no records anywhere in
`data/entities`, and the only TCGA and CPTAC datasets in the store are narrow and unrelated
(uveal melanoma SF3B1, the DREAM synthetic WGS sets). Nothing was duplicated.

### 7. Figure 2's unexplained double dagger: claim is the right home

Agree. The symbol appears on the "Differential gene expression" and "Hallmark GSEA" rows and
nothing in the PDF defines it; the Figure 2 page carries it inside the row labels themselves
(`label:"Differential gene expression ‡"`) with no footnote either, so it is unexplained in both
artifacts rather than lost in the print. A claim recording that it is unexplained is the honest
record. It does not affect the four cells it marks, which match everywhere they are printed.

### 8. Figure 7's scope disagreement: one claim, no evidence concern

No concern, and most of the apparent disagreement is scope rather than contradiction. The prose
counts associations across trials and arms: "Of 304 associations significant before adjustment, 24
remained significant", with page 20 naming four trials (IMmotion150, IMmotion151, OAK, POPLAR) and
models fitted "separately within each trial and treatment arm". The figure's 106 and 0 are one
selection: the caption says each line is drawn "within the selected trial's
immunotherapy-containing arm". A subset of 304 being 106 is what the caption predicts. The
extractor's claim already says "for its default trial selection", so it does not overstate this.

Two things stay genuinely unexplained and the claim names both: the caption's "(550 total)",
which the report never reconciles with either count, and the figure's trial toggle listing only
POPLAR, OAK and IMmotion151 while the Methods analyse four trials. Neither bears on a stored
value, because Figure 7 produced claims and no results, and the only record a concern could
withhold is the claim that documents the problem. Withholding it would hide the finding.

### 9. The response protocol: no concern for the context asymmetry, one concern for the in-sample POPLAR value

This is two questions wearing one coat, and they come apart on the source text.

**The asymmetry does not need a concern.** Page 16 states it plainly, and states it for exactly
one comparator: "The gene-expression baseline uses log2(TPM + 1) for 20,250 protein-coding genes,
followed by PCA and TabICL. This baseline uses academic cohorts alone as context", against page
15's "The context contains 1,419 patients from 33 academic cohorts, together with patients from
the other trials in the response benchmark." That is a disclosed difference between two arms of
one protocol, not a defect in how the source reports them, and the contract already has a field
for it: `comparison.adaptation`, which is one of the nine fields two evaluations must agree on
before they can be compared. The two strings differ, and in any case `budget` and
`dataset_version` are null on all ten Figure 6 evaluations, so no pair on this protocol can enter
an automatic comparison as the batch stands. A concern scoped to the protocol would do something
different and worse: `shared/omics/concerns.ts` withholds every result whose evidence path names
the target, so it would take out all ten Figure 6 results, including the comparators that have no
problem, and withhold the protocol from use-case judgements as well. That deletes evidence rather
than qualifying it. The asymmetry is already recorded four times over, on the protocol's
`limitations`, on both evaluations' `limitations`, in the gene-TPM method's own description, and
in `claim-response-context-asymmetry`, which is the right weight for a stated design choice.

**The in-sample POPLAR value does need one, and it is a different defect.** The Figure 6 page
states: "The T-effector signature was derived on POPLAR, so its POPLAR result is in-sample." The
stored 0.628 is an equal mean over six trials, so one sixth of it is a training-set score. That is
a property of the number, not of the setup: no comparison field records it, the report's own
Figure 6 text does not mention it, and a later study's out-of-sample T-effector AUROC would look
directly comparable. So I added one entry to `source-web-fig06`'s `attributes.evidence_concerns`,
with that artifact's hash and the locator of the note, scoped to
`applies_to: ["ladon-20261115-result-fig6-t-effector-ifn-gamma-response"]`.

The scope is the result, not the evaluation, deliberately. The contamination is specific to the
six-trial mean: a per-trial OAK value from the same evaluation would be clean and a per-trial
POPLAR value would be worse, so pinning the concern to the aggregate says what is actually wrong.
The printed value is kept as published, and the other nine Figure 6 results, the protocol, the
task and the method record are untouched.

Two related points the source leaves open, recorded and not inferred. Page 16 says of
BulkRNABert, BulkFormer and BMRetriever-7B only that "Their embeddings use the same PCA and TabICL
procedure as Ladon", which plainly implies the same context but does not say so, and it says
nothing about which context the DGE signature is fitted in beyond "within the context data". The
extractor left both unresolved, which is correct under "never guess", and the claim says so in
those terms. IMPRES and TIDE carry the developer's own notes that both were developed on melanoma
cohorts outside these trials, which is a limitation in their favour being absent rather than an
in-sample problem, so no concern there.

## Findings of my own, beyond the nine questions

**Ladon was not named as a member of its own combined configurations.** `records -- add` refused
the batch on the first attempt, with two SHACL violations of `rbs:CombinedConfiguration`
(`data/ontology/shapes.ttl`): `config-ladon-clinical-transfer-cox` and
`config-ladon-clinical-trial-survival` both carry a `combination_rule` but named only
`model-bmretriever-7b` with `uses_model`, so each had one member where the shape requires two.
This is worth more than a validation fix. The shape's comment says why it exists: "The discrepancy
rules compare it as its own system and never against one of its members", and
`scripts/kg/contradictions.py` builds that member set from `uses_model` alone
(`_excluded`-adjacent code at line 423). As extracted, the rules would have been free to compare
"Ladon + clinical covariates" against "Ladon" as two independent systems, which is exactly the
comparison the paper's own argument rests on and exactly the one that must not count as a
disagreement between studies. I added `uses_model` to `model-ladon` on both, so both members are
named. `configuration_of` still points at `model-ladon`, which is right here and differs from the
vote-ensemble precedents in the store (`somatic-20261009-config-guille2025-vote-*`, where it
points at a separate combining method): the report presents these as Ladon's own combined score,
not as a neutral ensemble, so the result belongs to Ladon.

**Figure 18's DFCI IBC AUC: 0.73 in the figure, 0.74 in the prose.** The page 34 prose reads "it
exceeds HER2 expression (0.82 and 0.74, against 0.71 and 0.53)" while Figure 18 prints
`0.73 [0.50, 0.93]`. One value, rounded two ways, and the mechanism is visible in the figure
page's own code: it holds `auc:.735` and renders it with `toFixed(2)`, which yields 0.73 because
0.735 sits just below 0.735 in binary floating point, while the prose rounds half up. The batch
stores the figure's 0.73, its locator names the figure, and `attributes.source_discrepancy` and
`rounding_note` already record the conflict. No evidence concern: the difference is 0.005 against
a printed interval of [0.50, 0.93], and `source_discrepancy` is the contract's own non-blocking
note for exactly this. Recorded in that result's `review.note` as confirmed rather than asserted.

**Figure 5's legend is the one assignment the PDF cannot settle.** The three legend values sit on a
single line below the panel, so no geometry ties them to a training stage; my script says so
rather than pretending otherwise. Checked against the authors' Figure 5 page: its third stage,
"TCGA + non-trial cohorts + other clinical trials", holds 0.6355716, 0.5954244 and 0.5784649 for
Ladon, DGE and Hallmark GSEA, which round to the three stored values. The same stage's
`equal_trial_mean` values are 0.630, 0.583 and 0.567, which are different numbers, so the stored
values are the size-weighted means the caption and the protocol describe and not the equal-trial
ones. Recorded in those three results' `review.note`.

**Figure 17's default comparator.** The page 34 caption says the comparator is "chosen above the
plots (CIBERSORT by default)", which confirms from the PDF itself what the legend order and the
per-lineage arithmetic also show. The batch's assignment of each second value to CIBERSORT is
right.

**The uncertainty metadata is supported, which is not automatic for figure-only sources.** Page 28
states "95% confidence intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals with predictions held fixed.
The INSR analysis resamples tumors 2,000 times", and page 39 states "We computed 95% intervals as
percentiles of 2,000 bootstrap resamples ... For response prediction, we resampled each cohort's
patients and discarded resamples with a single class." Both match the `method`, `resamples` and
`note` fields on the Figure 11 and Figure 18 results. Figure 17's intervals are drawn and not printed, and are correctly left as
`missing_metadata.uncertainty`.

### Two corrections made after the batch had been added

Both were caught by a second look at my own review objects, after `records -- add` had run, and
both were fixed by editing the canonical files and then
`npm run records -- change docs/reviews/2026-11-15-ladon.md data/omics/ladon-20261115 <209 ids>`.
The ids are in `review/changed-record-ids.txt`.

   its review, and the same for `source-web-report`, `source-web-fig06` and `claim-access-blog`,
   whose only source is the announcement page. A review object has to name the artifact that was

   first source it does cite.
2. **An overclaiming method note.** The shared note on the descriptive records said "Every field

   fifteen or so pages closely, not every cited page. The honest fix was to do the missing work

Sequencing is the lesson, and `collection.md` section 5 says it plainly: make every ruling and
edit before adding the batch. Had I re-read my own review objects before `records -- add`, these
would have been two lines in `apply_review.py` instead of a 209-record `records -- change`.

## Remaining gaps

- Everything here is developer-reported. Blank Bio built Ladon and ran every comparator, no
  weights, code or licence are published, and three days after release no independent evaluation
  exists. The batch does not overstate this, but it is the dominant limitation on all 101 values.
- Per-trial values, exact p-values and most intervals exist only in the web figures' embedded data
  and are not stored. Figures 20 and 21 (scaling and ClinBench) are images, so ClinBench has no
  score and stays `discovered`.
- The trial RNA-seq and outcome data have no stated provenance or access route anywhere in the
  report, which no record can repair.
- Figure 8's comparator selection is assignable from the markers page but that page is not pinned;
  hash and reasoning are in ruling 2 for whoever picks it up.
- The 19 new protocols carry no use-case relevance judgements. This batch is a model
  registration and the brief did not ask for them, but several protocols (trial prognosis,
  immunotherapy response, deconvolution) are the kind that use cases read, so they are worth a
  pass of their own.
- No discrepancy involves this batch. There is no current `discrepancies.json` in the tree, the
  knowledge-graph build produces it, and these are the first results in the store for every system
  in the batch, so there is nothing yet for a discrepancy to contradict.
