47,081 of 104,243 evidence citations rest on a source whose bytes are not stored anywhere in this repository, and no build step has ever read an artifact byte. This record decides how source bytes are captured, identified, published and checked. It is proposed, not accepted: the licence tier that about 97 sources land in depends on a determination only the owner can make.

Problem

Of 1,453 pinned hashes, 300 match bytes in the worktree. A re-retrieval of 859 pins on 2026-09-19 found 175 no longer returning the pinned bytes. Licence is recorded on 408 of 1,483 sources, and the written rule about the rest is backwards.

Decision

One capture tool. Two identities per source: the bytes as received, and the normalised artifact the values were read from. An append-only capture ledger as the join. Six licence tiers that govern publication, never possession. Rescue in waves by citation weight.

Expected effect

95.8 percent of the at-risk citation mass covered for about 23 hours of hand work and 765 MB. 45.2 percent becomes a ratcheted number in CI. Zero records and zero provenance hashes change in the first pull request.

Superseded in part, 2026-11-17

The owner has decided to recollect the store from the primary sources instead of auditing it against the hashes it already pins, and has dropped the drift and re-pinning apparatus this record designs. The policy in force is docs/source-preservation.md, which carries the seven determinations of 2026-11-17 from issue 276. Nothing below is deleted, so what was designed and what replaced it can both be read.

No longer applies. By this page's own numbering:

  • Decision 2, two identities per source. There is one digest per set of bytes. A stored object carries the SHA-256 of its own bytes; a text layer is a separate object with its own manifest row and its own digest, not a second identity of the original. No record field holds an evidence identity.
  • Decision 3's added receipt fields. The receipt that exists is fetchReceiptSchema in shared/archive/schema.ts. It has no content_match, capture_id, derived[], witness[], attempt, etag or swhid. content_match, attempt, etag and witness[] exist to compare a retrieval with an earlier pin; the other three are the ledger and the second identity, dropped with them.
  • Decision 4's capture ledger and record-side pointers. data/archive/captures/<0-f>.jsonl is not written. primary_capture_id, capture_ids, evidence_identity, pin_status and hash_scope_class are not declared on the source kind.
  • Decision 6's rescue waves and the Wayback CDX probe. Fetch order comes from the worklist, not from citation-weight bands, and no web archive is queried: the fetcher's four routes all go to the publisher, Unpaywall, Europe PMC or the preprint server.
  • Decision 7's derived preservation_status and data/archive/preservation.jsonl. Neither is generated. The rule underneath it survives, below.
  • Decision 8 in full. No scheduled re-capture, no drift file, no drift classifier, and no tool_version_changed guard. A poppler bump mints nothing, because nothing re-derives an old layer to compare.
  • In "How it is verified", the capture-ledger assertions (items 2, 3, 5, 6, 8 and 10, and the hash_scope_class half of item 9) and the citations_without_capture ratchet in item 11. The commands that exist are worklist, fetch, verify, status and upload. The ratchet's shape is still open in issue 272.
  • Implementation sequence steps 1, 6, 8 and 9, and the wave part of step 10: the CDX measurement, the citation-weight waves and the drift sweep. Step 7, the SWHID pass over the 453 pinned-commit GitHub sources, is not built either, and no determination names it. Step 10's habit change stands, and docs/collection.md carries it.

Still governs.

  • Context, as measurement. 47,081 of 104,243 evidence citations rest on unstored bytes, licence is recorded on 408 of 1,483 sources, and the storage rule in docs/collection.md was backwards. Those numbers are why the recollection is funded, and the collection document has since been corrected.
  • Decision 1. The object store, the manifest at data/archive/objects/<0-f>.jsonl, the layer field and the archiveAssetUrl trap, all owned by ADR 0004. Each stored object keeps a plain SHA-256, which is what lets a claim cite a span in one file and what deduplicates the store.
  • Decision 3's principle. One append-only receipt per source per attempt, written for a failure as well as a success, stating only what one transfer returned on one date, and never edited after merge.
  • Decision 4's rule that no existing record and no provenance hash changes, and that no release id moves.
  • Decision 5, the licence policy, in full. Six tiers computed from the licence string by a hand-reviewed map, a licence that never blocks a fetch, publication restricted rather than possession, and a new licence string failing the build until it has a row. This is the part the determinations settle rather than drop: question 1 is determination 1, question 2 is determination 2, question 3 is determination 3.
  • Decision 7's refusal to mark an unstored source with evidence_concerns. A source whose bytes are not held is not a source whose values are in doubt.
  • The text layer and its sanity gate, which belong to ADR 0002 and are built in shared/archive/layers/. Determination 2 settles that a text layer follows its original's tier.
  • Considered and rejected, and the two closing sections on issue #228 and docs/collection.md.

Why. The store is being recollected, not audited. Every claim is extracted again from the primary sources with new tooling, so a comparison against a hash pinned under the previous tooling answers a question nobody is asking: whether a value extracted the old way still matches its source. The new extraction answers that directly, by reading the source again. One consequence is stated plainly in the policy document. We do not know, and will not learn from this work, which of the 1,453 pinned hashes still reproduce; the 175 that returned different bytes on 2026-09-19 are left exactly as they are, and nothing treats a pin that no longer reproduces as an error.

Status and figures. The status line stays at Proposed. Context

Of 1,483 source records, 1,453 pin an artifact_sha256. A SHA-256 index of every file in the worktree (3,126 files, 3,331 digests, each file hashed raw and, where gzipped, also expanded) matches 300 of those hashes to stored bytes. 297 of the 300 are original bytes; 3 are curator factual receipts whose artifact_sha256 hashes the receipt JSON rather than a source. 1,153 pinned hashes have no copy anywhere, and a further 30 sources carry no hash at all. 47,081 of 104,243 evidence citations, 45.2 percent, rest on a source whose bytes are not stored, across 574 cited sources.

The 300 is a lower bound by at most 26, the number of unmatched sources whose hash pins a member inside a committed zip or tar that the index did not expand. Wave 0 below settles it by expanding them.

The loss is already happening

A re-retrieval of 859 pinned artifacts on 2026-09-19 (data/omics/audits/2026-09-19-source-access.receipts.jsonl) recorded 650 supported, 203 insufficient_evidence and 6 inaccessible, with retrieved_sha256 differing from expected_sha256 on 175 lines, 20.4 percent. Twenty-seven findings across twenty-two dated reviews record a pin that does not reproduce. The causes are understood and none of them is a withdrawn paper: bioRxiv restamps the PDF on every download (bacformer version 3 gave two distinct hashes minutes apart at an identical 21,613,661 bytes, with Producer iLovePDF and CreationDate equal to the download moment), Europe PMC regenerates full text XML, and a Nature URL moved from the accelerated article preview to the version of record, so 97f61ec0 became d7dfe801.

Four structural gaps

  1. No join. No record field points at a stored copy. artifact_path is used by 0 records. The source-to-file mapping exists only as command-line pairs to npm run batch -- hash (scripts/omics/batch.py:426-445), after which review.json keeps two dicts that cannot be joined: files, keyed by path with the gzip container hash, and source_artifacts, keyed by source id with the uncompressed original hash.
  2. No retrieval metadata. No fetcher in the repository records HTTP status, final URL after redirect, ETag, Last-Modified or response Content-Type. media_type is hand-entered on 461 records. hash_scope is free text with 106 distinct strings over 587 records, and 868 records have a pinned hash with no scope at all.
  3. No check. See the figure above. Nothing reads a byte.
  4. No licence. Licence is recorded on 408 of 1,483 sources. Of the 1,153 pinned hashes with no stored copy, 913 record no licence, and all 30 sources with no hash at all also record none, so 943 of the 1,183 unstored sources are licence-blank. docs/collection.md says to archive a copy "if the licence allows it" and then treats CC BY-NC-ND sources as hashed, not stored. That reading is backwards, and it is a direct cause of the gap.

Numbers that did not reproduce, and the ones that settled them

  • 280 stored sources. This figure appears in an earlier audit brief. It does not reproduce. Re-running the worktree hash index gives 300 matches on artifact_sha256, and 307 if archive_sha256, original_artifact_sha256 and compressed_artifact_sha256 are also counted. A third design reported 302. No decision here moves on the difference; the number is 300 and it is stated once.
  • 994 licence-blank unstored sources. Against the current index the figure is 913 of the 1,153 pinned-but-unstored sources, or 943 of the 1,183 counting the 30 that carry no hash at all. The 994 came from an earlier count over a denominator of 1,173, taken from the index that also produced the 280. The conclusion is unchanged: licence metadata is missing on about four fifths of the gap.
  • 547 versus 574 cited unstored sources. Both are right about different sets. 547 counts "pinned hash, no copy", which drops the 30 sources carrying no artifact_sha256 at all, 27 of which are cited and carry 43 citations between them. 574 is the operational number, because those 27 also have no stored bytes.
  • 69 licence strings. The implementation plan sizes the licence map at 69 rows. The store has 67 distinct licence values today. The table is generated from the measured set, and a new string fails the build until a row is added, so the row count is whatever the store says at merge.
  • The licence tally. An earlier classification summed to 1,474 of 1,483 records. The tally in the tier figure below sums to 1,483; the nine-record difference is prose licence strings such as http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ (stated on the retrieved article page) falling through a regular expression. That is the argument for a hand-reviewed map rather than a pattern match.
  • 12 minutes per hand rescue. Unmeasured. The whole 23-hour effort figure rests on it. The wave 1 pilot logs time per source and resizes wave 2 if the estimate is wrong by more than a factor of two.

Options

Three designs were measured against this repository. Each owns one layer and gets another layer wrong, so the decision takes the policy from C, the mechanism from B and the verification discipline from A. The rejections inside each option are listed at the end of this record.

AOne OCFL 1.1 object per source in private object storage

An OCFL storage root with sha256 as the digest algorithm, so every inventory manifest key is literally the artifact_sha256 the records already pin. Each retrieval is a new OCFL version. Originals raw, a derived layer beside them, receipts and a sharded manifest public in git, plus a spec/ snapshot and a thirty-line shell verifier.

For

  • The digest choice makes the record-to-archive join an equality test rather than a lookup table.
  • A versioned object expresses "one source, three container hashes, one stable derived identity" natively.
  • The self-describing archive plus a verifier using only sha256sum, jq, cut and printf turns the ten-year claim into an acceptance test.
  • Its measurements are the most precise of the three. The 526,310-byte mean over 853 sized receipts is exactly right.

Against

  • OCFL is built for institutional repositories with mutable objects and dedicated staff. Here it is 1,483 immutable observations and two people. The design itself concedes a flat store gives most of the value at about 30 percent of the code.
  • A hand edit to an object leaves the inventory and the content disagreeing silently until the next fixity run.
  • It asserts CC BY-NC-ND material can never be republished. That is wrong, and the error drives its private-by-default architecture.
  • It refuses a capture without a licence determination. That is the exact pressure that produced the 47,081 gap.

BOne WARC-writing capture tool and an append-only ledger

Every retrieval goes through one fetcher that writes a WARC and appends a capture receipt to a JSONL ledger in git. The receipt is the join. Each capture carries a payload digest and a normalised evidence identity from a named, versioned normaliser. Bytes are tiered by redistribution right. Rescue starts in the Wayback CDX index.

For

  • The only design that searched this repository for working code and found it. readPinnedPdfText at scripts/omics/extract/pdf.ts:22-71 already verifies bytes, binds the conversion to them through stdin, captures the pdftotext version string and returns an output_sha256. data/omics/vep-use-cases-20261014/extract/pmc_html.py already blanks the two per-request PMC tokens with exactly-one-match assertions.
  • Its tool_version_changed classifier row prevents the first poppler bump reporting false content drift on every PDF. No other design saw it.
  • The coverage ratchet turns 47,081 into a committed number that cannot grow silently.
  • It classifies Hugging Face model cards at a revision as git-blob-identified files, and notes that a doi.org URL is never the artifact URL. 152 of the unstored are doi.org resolvers and 235 records carry no artifact_url.

Against

  • The WARC, Memento and SWHID machinery serves about 15 percent of the backlog, and the design says so.
  • The Wayback-first headline rests on one anecdote. Wayback's copy of a bioRxiv PDF is itself a restamped variant, so recovery will come from static files that moved, not from the 47 Europe PMC fullTextXML sources that dominate the tranche.
  • An 1,800-line first pull request bundles the contract with the pilot.
  • It mints a second retrieval-outcome vocabulary beside the frozen one.

CLicence tiering, rescue triage, no new substrate

Six licence tiers computed from the licence identifier alone, written once in SKOS and applied by code. Bytes as release assets of a private GitHub repository, verified by the existing expand(). The join is a committed manifest keyed on the artifact_sha256 that 1,453 records already carry, so no record and no provenance hash changes. Rescue in four bands by citation weight.

For

  • The only design that read the licence text and found the repository's written rule backwards. Licence is the measured blocker on 913 of the 1,153 unstored, so this is the highest-value finding in the set.
  • Separating "may the original be redistributed" from "may a derivative be redistributed" is the right abstraction, and neither other design draws it.
  • Its refusal to mark unstored sources with evidence_concerns is the best governance call in the set, and the reasoning checks out at shared/omics/concerns.ts:5-11.
  • Keeping the frozen outcome strings and adding content_match beside them, on the ground that a second vocabulary for the same concept in the same directory is how 106 hash_scope strings happened, is the sharpest argument anywhere in the three designs.

Against

  • The join on artifact_sha256 alone fails for exactly the sources that matter. The 175 drifted pins match no retrievable bytes, and 3 pinned hashes hash a curator receipt rather than a source.
  • No SWHID, which leaves the 453 pinned-commit GitHub sources, 39 percent of the missing set, without the cheapest durability available.
  • No text-layer sanity gate, so it does not catch the class of defect where a claim is drawn from an unreadable rendering.
  • One GitHub account holds the only copy, and the design admits nothing puts a second copy anywhere.
  • It asserts a pdftotext text layer "plainly is" adapted material. CC 4.0 authorises technical modifications across media and formats and states they never produce adapted material, so that is arguable either way and goes to the owner.

Decision

Build one capture tool, two identities per source, two publication tiers by licence, and rescue in bands by citation weight. Take the policy layer and the triage from option C, the capture mechanism and the ledger from option B, and the identity and verification discipline from option A. Reject OCFL as the layout and keep its two best ideas.

1. Substrate, which this record does not decide

The object store belongs to ADR 0004, and captures here write their bytes into it rather than into a second store. That record keys an object on the sha256 of the expanded bytes, publishes objects as GitHub release assets on sixteen shard tags archive/objects-<first hex digit>, verifies on download with the existing expand(), and commits the manifest at data/archive/objects/<0-f>.jsonl. Publishing is append-only, following scripts/serving/publish.ts:48-70, which never replaces a published asset. What this record would otherwise have expressed as four key prefixes is that manifest's layer field:

layer: exact-bytes      original bytes as received, stored gzip -n
layer: text-layer       pdftotext -layout output
layer: canonical-json   canonical JSON or XML text content
layer: warc             WARC 1.1 request and response pair

Keys never carry .gz, because assetName() appends it, and assetUrl() cannot be reused: RELEASE_ID = /^\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}-[a-f0-9]{12}$/ at scripts/release/files.ts:31 rejects a tag named archive/objects-0, which is why ADR 0004's scripts/archive/objects.ts carries a sibling archiveAssetUrl(shard, name). That is the one trap a reader of either record will walk into.

Where restricted bytes sit is ADR 0004's open question 1, and nothing here turns on the answer. An earlier draft of this record named release assets of a private rewire-bio/rewire-benchmark-archive repository as decided. That was this record ruling on a storage question that belongs next door, and it is withdrawn. Tier governs publication, not possession, so the policy below holds under all three answers: a t0 or t1 object is published in the public store; a t2 to t4 object goes to whichever private backend that question picks, and under its answer (c), where no restricted bytes are stored at all, the capture receipt is still written, both identities are still recorded, and the source's preservation_status is hashed_only instead of bytes_archived. The coverage ratchet counts captures, not bytes, so it falls either way.

From option A, the archive carries its own contract: maintenance/verify-archive.sh, thirty lines using only sha256sum, gunzip, jq and cut, shipped to the archive root as README.txt, so any blob can be verified with no Node and no project code.

The single-copy risk is this record's to mitigate whatever the backend: the tripwire is the weekly fixity job failing loudly, plus one annual operator-held offline copy, which at the measured 765 MB projection fits on anything. For the open tiers only, one Zenodo deposit per archive generation gives an off-GitHub copy with a concept DOI and a version DOI.

2. Two identities per source, always

Every capture records a payload identity, the SHA-256 of the bytes as received that day, and, where one applies, a normalised evidence identity, the SHA-256 of the artifact the stored values were actually read from, with the normaliser id, the pinned tool version and the exact command. That is what the GPN-MSA measurement demands and what no record holds today.

The evidence identity and ADR 0002's layer_sha256 are one hash over one set of bytes, not two. The bytes are one object in ADR 0004's store at layer: text-layer; the capture's derived[] entry here is where that hash is recorded with its command; and ADR 0002's per-batch extract/layers.jsonl is a generated projection of those entries for the sources a batch cites, checked byte-equal in CI rather than written by hand. A span resolves against that one object.

Do not invent canonicalisation. Promote the two normalisers already working here, pdf-text-layer@1 from scripts/omics/extract/pdf.ts:22-71 and pmc-article-page@1 from data/omics/vep-use-cases-20261014/extract/pmc_html.py, keeping its exactly-one-match assertion on ncbi_phid and csrfmiddlewaretoken. Add two more: europepmc-fulltext-xml@1 (JATS text content in document order, whitespace runs collapsed), which is load-bearing because 47 of the top 150 are Europe PMC fullTextXML, and json-jcs@1 (RFC 8785) for Europe PMC search, arXiv Atom and Crossref responses. An earlier draft had the JATS normaliser apply NFKC. It does not: ADR 0002 addresses byte spans into this layer and forbids Unicode normalisation, because an ICU version change would then move every stored offset. Normalisation belongs in the versioned comparison function that record names on every check result. Each is a pure bytes-to-bytes function, re-runnable offline by a reviewer with no language model.

Text-layer sanity gate. This is ADR 0002's layer_sanity function, one implementation for both records: unusable here is that function's fail verdict, and the thresholds are its criteria. A derived layer fails when its printable-ASCII ratio is below 80 percent, its digit frequency below 0.5 percent of characters, its U+FFFD ratio above 0.1 percent, or it contains a run of more than 40 characters outside Unicode categories L, N, P and Zs. The layer is still stored; CI refuses any evidence identity that points at it. This is the one mechanism in any option that structurally prevents the bacformer defect, where version 2's text layer rendered "0.81 plus or minus 0.07" as +.)! +.+( and a claim was drawn from it anyway.

WARC only where the headers are the evidence: HTML pages, API responses, and any source whose recorded hash_scope already admits the response is not byte-stable. Re-wrapping an already-hashed PDF in a WARC adds a container whose own hash is unstable, which is the confusion this decision removes. A WARC is not byte-reproducible, because it embeds a record UUID and a capture timestamp, so a reviewer re-derives the text layer from the payload inside it and never re-derives the WARC. No screenshots: no deterministic re-check can be built over a PNG, and docs/collection.md already forbids reading a value off pixels.

3. The fetch receipt

data/omics/audits/<date>-archive-fetch.receipts.jsonl, append-only, one JSON object per line with sorted keys, extending the twelve-field shape that scripts/omics/audit/check-sources.py already writes and the 859-line 2026-09-19 file already carries. The twelve existing fields and the three existing outcome strings are kept unchanged. Added fields:

content_match compares a retrieval against a recorded identity. exact means the payload matches expected_sha256. derived means a derived identity matches one the store already records. none means both were available and both differ. unbaselined means no derived identity was recorded before the capture, which is the case for all 175 drifted pins, because the only measured text-layer hash in this repository lives in a review and not in a record. An unbaselined capture populates derived[], becomes the baseline and raises nothing. Without that rule, wave 1 opens 175 findings on day one, most of them benign restamps.

Receipts are machine-written observation logs, like the existing data/omics/audits/*.receipts.jsonl and data/omics/scope-audit.jsonl. They assert only what a transfer returned on a date, state no value and name no entity, so they do not breach the two-pass rule. What a reviewer gates is the promotion of a capture into a record's pointers. Receipts are never edited after merge: a second attempt is a second line, and a source's drift history is its receipt sequence. The 2026-09-19 file is not rewritten; readers treat the new fields as absent on its 859 lines and derive content_match from expected_sha256 == retrieved_sha256.

4. No record changes in the first pull request

One committed file is this record's own: data/archive/captures/<0-f>.jsonl, the receipt ledger in sixteen shards by the first hex digit of sha256(capture_id), matching shardOf at scripts/omics/records.ts:48. The manifest of stored bytes is ADR 0004's data/archive/objects/<0-f>.jsonl, not a second file here; this record adds three columns to its rows, tier, observed_licence and exposure, and ADR 0004 derives visibility from the tier. Projected size is about 1,500 manifest rows at about 450 bytes plus about 1,500 captures at about 1.6 KB over sixteen shards, so about 150 KB a shard against the 50 MB CI cap. Neither file enters storeDigests() at scripts/omics/records.ts:134-146, so the archive does not perturb a release id and every past release id stays valid.

Record-side pointers are declared in shared/omics/attribute-registry.ts on the source kind and populated lazily, never in bulk: primary_capture_id, capture_ids, evidence_identity, hash_scope_class, pin_status, archive_tier, redistribution, swhid. New batches set them at collection step 3 from day one; an existing record gains them the next time a reviewer touches it for any reason. A bulk pass would rewrite 1,483 records and 1,483 provenance lines under one dated review, and the 2026-11-16 pass is the warning: 2,574 records carrying a non-canonical review object reached the store through thirteen independent reviews unnoticed.

artifact_sha256, hash_scope, archive_sha256 and original_artifact_sha256 are untouched on every existing record. The 106 free-text hash_scope strings are not rewritten: 101 of the 587 carry a reviewer's hand-written caveat about instability that an enum cannot hold, and rewriting a reviewer's prose to fit a schema is the same move as correcting a pin. hash_scope_class goes beside it. The overloaded archive_sha256, 55 records with two incompatible meanings, is not migrated either; new captures do not write it.

5. Licence policy

A licence answers two separate questions and this repository treats them as one. May the verbatim original bytes be redistributed? May a derivative, a text layer, a canonical JSON or a WARC payload, be redistributed? CC BY-NC-ND answers yes to the first, non-commercially and with attribution, and restricts the second. docs/collection.md:79,145 records the opposite assumption and must be corrected.

Tiers are computed from the licence identifier alone by a hand-reviewed lookup table data/archive/licence-map.tsv, one row per observed licence string, with the tier concepts in data/vocab/archive-tier.ttl. Four rules are enforced in code.

  • Licence never blocks capture. The default for an unresolved licence is t4-private. Unknown never means public and never means unstored. Refusing a capture without a licence determination is the same pressure that produced the 47,081 gap: it adds two to five minutes per new source, and the predictable response is to mark everything unreported and private, which satisfies the gate and defeats it.
  • The policy restricts publication, not possession. A private copy of a work the team lawfully accessed, made for computational analysis, is ordinary research practice. t5-no-store is the only tier producing no bytes.
  • Licence resolution writes candidates, never records. scripts/archive/licence.ts queries Crossref license[], the arXiv API, Europe PMC permissions, the repository LICENSE at the pinned commit and Hugging Face card front matter, stopping at the first answer. Each resolution is itself a capture, so the licence claim is pinned to bytes. The result lands in the manifest as observed_licence with its resolver and date, and a reviewer promotes it into attributes.licence only when next touching that source record.
  • What stays public for a private-tier source: the whole capture receipt, both digests, byte counts, media type, URL and final URL, status, retrieval time, the normaliser id and command so a reader with lawful access reproduces the evidence identity themselves, the Memento URL, the SWHID, and a quotation-scale span inside the claim record. Those are facts about the work, not the work.

Existing exposure is enumerated, not inherited. Six CC BY-NC-ND verbatim originals and one CC BY-NC are already committed to this public repository: gpn-paper-20261116-source-biorxiv-v3, -source-pnas-2023, -source-pnas-2023-si-appendix, phylogpn-2025, phylogpn-20261116-source-supplement, protein-stability-20261009-source-dieckhaus2024 and protein-stability-20261009-source-pancotti2022. Bands are recomputed from the current store on every archive -- triage run, so a never-cited source is promoted automatically when it acquires its first citation. Licence resolution runs inside each wave, in the same pass: 943 unresolved licences will never be cleared as a standalone chore, and the resolver needs a capture anyway.

The Wayback CDX probe runs first, on its own, as a measurement. For a source whose origin has moved, a contemporaneous third-party capture is the only copy of the pinned bytes that still exists, and refetching can only produce new bytes. The probe takes the 175 sources whose retrieved_sha256 differed on 2026-09-19, queries the CDX index near each record's retrieved_at, fetches the nearest two through the id_ endpoint, which is not optional because the default endpoint injects a toolbar and rewrites links, hashes each and compares with artifact_sha256. Twenty or more byte matches makes CDX the first step of waves 1 and 2 for every host. Five to nineteen makes it the first step only for static-file hosts where a byte match is plausible: Nature, Springer static content and figshare. Below five it is dropped and waves 1 and 2 are origin-first. Matches will come from static files that moved, like the Nature preview-to-version-of-record case, not from the 47 Europe PMC fullTextXML sources, whose bytes are regenerated per request.

Two failed attempts, at least seven days apart, each with a receipt, before a source is a lost candidate. One attempt is not evidence of loss: the 2026-09-19 run found 6 inaccessible of 859, including one HTTP 500 and four artifacts over the 25 MB budget. Politeness is per-host serialisation with a minimum two-second gap, robots.txt and Crawl-delay respected, one retry on 429 or 503 honouring Retry-After, and never a spoofed user agent. A bioRxiv 1015 response is a rate limit and the fetcher backs off. The sweep reports by host, so a block on one host does not silently poison the drift statistics.

What cannot be rescued. The four purged amp originals (dragen.html.gz and three siblings) were never in git history, so only a refetch can recover them. They go through the same two-attempt rule as everything else and then preservation_status: lost, a dated review, and their curator receipts standing in with hash_scope_class: curator-receipt. There is no carve-out: a vendor documentation page that 404s today can return next week. Their original_artifact_sha256 values are never touched.

7. How a lost source is marked

Not with evidence_concerns. An entry without applies_to on a source withholds every record citing it from comparisons and use-case judgements (shared/omics/concerns.ts:5-11), and _excluded in scripts/kg/contradictions.py applies the same exclusion to discrepancy inference. Marking the 574 would remove 45 percent of the evidence base from comparison. It is also wrong on the merits: a source whose bytes are not archived is not a source whose value is in doubt. Two agents read it and a reviewer approved it. The loss is in future re-verifiability, not in present support.

Instead, a derived, deterministic preservation_status per source, computed by the build from the manifest and the latest receipt, written to data/archive/preservation.jsonl and exported as rb:preservationStatus: bytes_archived, derived_archived, hashed_only, withheld_by_licence, lost. It is not a record attribute, so no provenance hash moves. It is visible on the website source panel and queryable in the graph, and it withholds nothing.

A lost source becomes an evidence concern only through the normal reviewed path, with a dated review, when all three hold: preservation_status is lost; no stored bytes and no stored derived layer exist anywhere; and a reviewer has recorded that the value cannot be re-derived from any other source the store holds. The third condition is the one that matters and the one a machine cannot settle. Most lost sources will fail it, because a published AUROC is usually printed in more than one place. The concern, when written, uses applies_to with specific result and claim ids, never the unscoped form.

8. Drift is classified, never written to a record

A scheduled sweep re-captures every source with a primary_capture_id and writes data/archive/drift/<date>.jsonl, one line per source with both digests and both identities before and after, and the normaliser tool version before and after.

Consequences

Good

  • Every cited source either has stored bytes with two identities, or a dated reason it does not, and CI enforces the difference.
  • The 20.4 percent drift becomes a classified, tracked series, and a bioRxiv restamp stops looking like a withdrawn paper.
  • 45.2 percent becomes a ratcheted number that cannot grow without a deliberate commit.
  • The source-to-file join is persisted, so the two unjoinable review.json dicts stop mattering.
  • Licence becomes a dated, resolvable, attributable fact instead of an absence on 1,075 records, and the backwards rule in docs/collection.md is corrected.
  • The unreadable-text-layer defect becomes structurally impossible to repeat, and the pin-identity and citation-metadata defects become cheap offline checks.
  • Zero records and zero provenance hashes change in the first pull request, and no release id shifts ever.

Costs

  • Money: nothing in the public tiers. GitHub release assets are 0 USD at the projected 765 MB. What the restricted tiers cost is ADR 0004's open question 1, where the priced alternative is about 0.05 USD a month. Crossref and arXiv need no key. CI adds about 13 MB per release run at tier 3 and a weekly rotation at tier 4.
  • Human: about 23 hours to reach 95.8 percent citation coverage: about 2.5 hours building the licence map, about 9 hours of hand rescue on the projected 45 failures in waves 1 and 2, about 5.5 hours of licence calls the resolvers cannot settle, 4 hours of policy page and review, and 2 hours supervising automated runs. The per-rescue basis is an estimate and wave 1 measures it.
  • Forever, for two people: one triage sweep per quarter, about 300 drift lines of which most should classify as container_restamp with no action; one archive token to rotate; one licence map row whenever a new licence string appears, which fails the build until added; four SKOS vocabularies to keep; two pinned tools, poppler and warcio, whose versions are now part of the data contract, so a toolchain bump is a dated decision rather than a silent upgrade; and about 2.5 MB of receipt JSONL per full sweep accumulating in git.
  • The habit change that decides whether any of this holds: collection step 3 goes through scripts/archive/capture.py instead of the ad hoc curl that five of twenty-two notes.md files show today. docs/collection.md and the collection skill files must be updated, or the ledger quietly stops growing while the store does not.

Accepted losses, stated plainly

  • 1,030 sources (606 never cited, 424 lightly cited carrying 1,974 citations, 4.2 percent of the at-risk mass) get machine effort only, forever. Hand rescue for them is about 66 hours against 4.2 percent, and pretending otherwise is how the 2026-09-19 audit's findings sat for two months.
  • The archive has one home plus an annual offline copy, unless ADR 0004's question 1 buys a second vendor. A lost GitHub account loses the private tier. The open tiers survive at Zenodo.
  • An outside reader cannot obtain private-tier bytes. They can check every hash against the manifest and re-derive the evidence identity from their own lawful copy with the recorded command. For the unknown-licence set this is a legal constraint, not an engineering one.
  • Raster-only values stay out of reach. The published GPN-MSA ablation values are an image, there is no text layer, and docs/collection.md forbids reading a value off pixels. This decision does not close that gap.
  • Nine of the nineteen recorded defect classes are touched; ten are untouched, including the 728-record comparability-field normalisation group that is the largest corrected set in this repository's own reviews. Those are normalisation decisions keyed on store convention and vocabulary scope, not readings of a source. This decision stops the evidence base dissolving. It does not fix extraction, and if only one piece of work is funded this is not the one that corrects the most records.
  • Two written rules are corrected rather than followed: docs/collection.md on no-derivatives storage, and the distribution implication of the 2026-11-16 GPN-MSA ruling. Reviews are not edited after merge, so the correction arrives as a later review naming the earlier one, and anyone reading the older review alone gets the wrong rule.

Open questions for the owner

These three are why the status is proposed and not accepted. All three are licence determinations, which is the part only the owner can settle. Nothing in the first two pull requests is blocked by them, because the default for an unresolved licence is private storage. The substrate question, which backend holds restricted bytes, is not asked here: it is ADR 0004's open question 1, and section 1 above states why this policy holds under any of its answers.

1. Is this work NonCommercial use under CC 4.0?

A benchmark knowledge graph published by rewire-bio on a public repository and a public website. This gates 39 of the top 150 sources and is the single answer the whole public tier hangs on.

  1. Yes. Record a dated, named determination in docs/source-preservation.md. About 97 sources carrying about 16,800 citations move from the private tier to publishable verbatim, with a manifest flag change and no refetch.
  2. No. Nothing above t0 is ever published, the archive covers the same bytes privately, and the seven restrictively licensed originals already committed become a remediation task.
  3. Seek counsel first. The archive proceeds private by default meanwhile and nothing is blocked.

2. May a derived layer of a no-derivatives work be published?

A pdftotext -layout text layer, a canonical JATS text extraction or a canonical JSON of a CC BY-ND or CC BY-NC-ND work, published alongside the original.

  1. Yes, treating it as a technical modification across media and formats, which CC 4.0 authorises and states never produces adapted material. The derived layer then follows the original's tier.
  2. No, treating it as adapted material, which section 3 forbids sharing. Derived layers of both no-derivatives tiers stay private forever, even where the original is published.
  3. Undecided, hold private pending counsel.

Answer (b) also corrects the distribution implication of the 2026-11-16 GPN-MSA ruling, which archives exactly this layer as the thing to store.

3. What happens to the seven restrictively licensed originals already committed here?

The six CC BY-NC-ND and one CC BY-NC originals listed under the licence policy above.

  1. Leave them, recording each in the manifest with exposure: pre-policy and a dated rationale, on the ground that verbatim non-commercial redistribution with attribution is what those licences permit.
  2. Move the bytes to the private tier, drop them from the worktree, keep their hashes in the manifest, and let the already-planned history rewrite of data/omics/releases/ take them from history at no extra cost.
  3. Remove them from the worktree now in a separate pull request and defer the history question.

Proposed default: (a) if question 1 is yes, (b) if question 1 is no. Quietly continuing and quietly cleaning up are both excluded.

What this record changes in issue #228 and in docs/collection.md

Issue #228 is answered by ADR 0002, not by this record. That record owns the staged pipeline, the structured span beside the printed locator, the extraction receipt and the coverage figure on the batch receipt. This record does not decide any of that. It touches #228 in one place, and in that place #228 is wrong.

#228 carries as a decision driver that sources under CC BY-NC-ND are hashed, not stored. That is the same reading as docs/collection.md:79 and :145, and it is backwards. CC BY-NC-ND permits reproducing and sharing the unmodified work for non-commercial purposes with attribution, and restricts sharing adapted material. So an ND original may be stored and, subject to question 1 above, published verbatim. CC BY-ND permits even commercial verbatim redistribution. The thing genuinely in doubt is the derived layer, which is question 2, and the question of whether this repository's use is non-commercial, which is question 1. Neither of those is a reason to hold a source at hash-only. 80 of the 1,483 sources are CC BY-NC-ND and 2 are CC BY-ND, against 1,075 with no licence recorded at all, so this correction moves 82 sources out of a wrongly imposed limit and leaves the real blocker, missing metadata, where it belongs.

One more point of contact, stated so the records do not collide. The pipeline in ADR 0002 re-reads archived source bytes to re-derive claims, so the archive is a build input, not a side artifact, and it has to be fetchable deterministically by hash in CI. That is what tiers 2 to 4 above provide, over ADR 0004's object store. The sanity gate is the other handover: a span is only checkable against a text layer that a machine has agreed is readable, and without it the bacformer defect, a claim drawn from +.)! +.+(, repeats under a pipeline that validates spans.
