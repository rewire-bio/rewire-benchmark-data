Architecture decision record 0001 · rewire-benchmark-data

Comparable evidence for contested results

Accepted Date 2026-10-23 Decider Tim Richardson Accepted 2026-10-23, option B Bundle measured kg/2026-10-23-9f59283abc5a
Umbrella for #201, #204, #205, #206, #207, #208 Related #174, #209 to #211, PR #212

The discrepancy rules count result rows as if each were independent evidence about one well-defined system, from one independent study, on a known task. The store guarantees none of those. This record compares three ways to fix that. Option B was accepted; the design implements it.

Problem

Of 14 reviewed medium and high findings, 1 was a real error and 3 were leads. 220 of 221 open findings are labelled cross-task, mostly because task links are missing.

Decision

Option B: define system, study, evidence and task scope explicitly, in the data and in the rules. Until precision is measured, show medium and high publicly only for same-task findings.

Expected effect

The three leads sit at medium. Every explained finding drops to low. Both false positives stop firing. The real error stays visible at low with its review.

Context

The feature exists because of the Alavi miss: a wrong AlphaGenome splicing result went live although other studies in the store ranked the same models the other way (#174). The rules are meant to surface that kind of disagreement as a lead. Today most of what they surface is a task difference or an artefact of how the store is modelled.

Problem: four assumptions the rules make

How each assumption fails

Decision drivers

  1. Keep the cases that matter. Alavi and the three leads must still fire. Of these four, only #211 has a consensus of more than one study.
  2. Precision of what is public. A reader who sees "medium" should usually find a real disagreement on the same task.
  3. Stable IDs. The orphan check fails the release if an unsettled review claim loses its finding, so any re-keying needs previous_ids.
  4. Curation cost. 425 unlinked protocols and about 5,000 configurations without size or checkpoint metadata.
  5. Honest scope. The site should claim no more than cross-paper evidence can support (see On a meta score).

Options

APatch each issue

Close each issue as written:

  • no strong-consensus step for cross-task findings (#201);
  • require two or more consensus studies (#205);
  • re-link the ensembles (#204);
  • group the ProteinGym paper and repository (#207);
  • add the three task links (#208);
  • use one configuration per study (#206).

Pros

  • Small diffs that can merge separately.
  • Each issue closes against its own acceptance list.
  • Little curation work.

Cons

  • Drops the real error and two of three leads.
  • "Cross-task" still mostly means "unknown".
  • Rows are still counted as evidence, so one dense study still dominates.
  • New false-positive classes will need new patches.

BComparable-evidence modelRecommended

  • System. A configuration that combines others (union, intersection, k of n) is compared as itself. Its members are recorded as uses_model links. It is never compared with one of its own members. Each study uses one configuration per system for every metric: the one with the most results, then the larger recorded size, then the ID.
  • Study. A new companion_of relation from source to source covers a repository, leaderboard or supplement released with a paper. Index._studies unions on it.
  • Evidence. Consensus and the high threshold count studies. Row counts stay on the page for reading only.
  • Scope. Unknown is not treated as different. Each unknown finding names the protocols that need a task link, which turns the detector into a curation queue for #208.

Pros

  • Fixes the cause, so the same classes of false positive do not come back.
  • Leads rank above every explained finding.
  • Keeps Alavi and all three leads.
  • Task links and study grouping also improve use-case pages and search.

Cons

  • Rules version 4. Two-study conflicts get new IDs, so unsettled claims need a previous_ids revision.
  • Depends on curating 425 protocols. Unknown findings stay at medium until that is done.
  • Alavi drops to low. That is honest, but less visible.
  • Most work of the three.

CNarrow the claim to comparison blocks

Only compare inside curated blocks: one task, a shared comparator, and three or more independent studies. Everything else becomes an internal curation queue. The same blocks are the basis for per-task rankings later.

Pros

  • Highest precision of the three.
  • The public claim matches what the evidence supports.
  • Gives the natural base for a defensible per-task score.

Cons

  • Hides cross-task signals like the one that would have flagged Alavi.
  • Small public coverage at first. Probably only #211 of the 14 would show.
  • The contested page shrinks to a few findings.

Decision

Adopt option B for the data model and the rules.

From option C, adopt the presentation rule. Until fixture precision has been measured on a release, the public contested page shows medium and high only for same-task findings. Everything else is listed as "needs review" in a curation view. That removes the main risk of B (unknown-task findings at medium) while the 425-protocol backlog is worked down.

Reject option A. Its two-study filter removes the real error and two of the three leads, and it leaves the counting problem in place.

On a meta score

Separate benchmark papers can be consolidated into one database, and that database is useful as a checked, citable index of what each paper reported. They cannot be consolidated into one global score. The papers differ in task, dataset, split, metric, configuration and training overlap, and for most pairs of papers no system was measured by both.

A ranking is defensible inside a comparison block (the figure under option C), for the same reason network meta-analysis works in clinical trials: a shared comparator links trials that never tested the same pair directly. Outside a block, a single number would hide the very differences this review found.

The test that settles coverage. After rollout steps 1 and 2, count the tasks that have three or more independent studies and a shared comparator. Fit per-task rankings on study-level verdicts for those tasks only, with uncertainty. The count is the honest answer to how much of the field can have a score. Expect a handful of tasks, such as ProteinGym-style variant effects and splicing, not most of the site.
