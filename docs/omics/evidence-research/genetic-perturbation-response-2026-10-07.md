### 1. Miller, Mejia, Leblanc, Swain, Wang & de Lima Camillo, "Deep learning perturbation models can outperform baselines on calibrated metrics," *Nature Biotechnology*, published online **1 October 2026**. DOI [10.1038/s41587-026-03307-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-026-03307-w).

**Access status: genuinely open access, confirmed, not paywalled.** `curl -L` with a standard browser user-agent returned HTTP 200 and the full Main+Methods text; the page carries the explicit sentence "This article is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License." (The earlier WebFetch tool call had followed a cookie-consent redirect and been misread as an authentication wall in the prior revision of this dossier — that was a tooling artifact, now corrected and independently re-verified by direct fetch.) Cached: `workbench/genetic-perturbation-20261007/miller_test.html` (fetched 2026-10-07T11:20Z), text-extracted to `miller_text.txt`.

**Article type:** Brief Communication (shorter format than a full research article — noted because this bounds how much Methods detail is in the main text versus Supplementary Notes/Tables, which were not separately fetched this pass).

**What it actually argues (not a blanket "opposes Systema" — it is explicitly a metric-calibration argument, not a claim that Systema or Ahlmann-Eltze et al. were simply wrong):**
- The paper explicitly agrees with Ahlmann-Eltze et al. (2025) and Viñas Torné et al. (Systema) that the mean baseline matches or exceeds published models under **uncalibrated** metrics such as MSE and Pearson(Δctrl), and explicitly names "control bias" (Systema's own mechanism) as one of two causes, alongside a second mechanism it calls "signal dilution" (error metrics diluted by the small fraction of genes with a true perturbation effect).
- Its contribution is a **metric calibration framework**, not a probability-calibration method — the paper's own vocabulary throughout is "calibrated"/"miscalibrated" in the sense of a metric's dynamic range/sensitivity, never predictive-probability calibration. This dossier uses "metric calibration" throughout for the same reason, per instruction not to conflate the two senses.
- **Positive control construction — the "held-out actual cells" distinction the task asked to preserve:** the paper's **technical-duplicate baseline** is built by randomly splitting the *measured* cells for each perturbation into two halves, using one half's mean profile to predict the other half's mean profile. This is **not** a model generalising to an unseen perturbation; it is a split-half reliability ceiling built entirely from already-observed cells of the *same* perturbation. The paper's own **interpolated duplicate** (its actual positive control for DRF calculation) is a per-gene blend of this technical-duplicate value and the mean baseline, weighted by DEG significance (α = 1 − P, estimated from an independent DEG test on the held-out duplicate half only, explicitly to avoid leakage into evaluation gene selection). Both controls are evaluated only on already-measured cells and must not be read as, or conflated with, any model's unseen-perturbation inference performance reported later in the paper (Fig. 2).
- **Dynamic Range Fraction (DRF):** DRF = [m(ŷ_p) − m(ŷ_n)] / [m(y) − m(ŷ_n) + ε], where ŷ_n is the negative control (mean baseline), ŷ_p the positive control (interpolated duplicate), y the ground truth, m(·) a metric, and ε a numerical-stability constant. This is the calibration meta-metric, applied per-perturbation across 14 datasets.
- **Datasets:** "14 genetic perturbation datasets coming from nine studies spanning ten different cell lines under activation and repression perturbations," obtained via the scPerturb standardized resource where available, else Zenodo/Figshare/GEO. Exact per-dataset cell/perturbation counts are in Supplementary Table 1, **not fetched in this pass** (Main text only gives spot figures: e.g. Norman19 "102.09" mean DEGs/perturbation vs Replogle22 K562 GWPS "3.25"; Norman19 "100 single and 124 combo (two-gene) perturbations," 31 of 4,950 possible pairs used in the train split).
- **Models evaluated (unseen single-perturbation and unseen-combination tasks only — explicitly, not unseen-cell-context):** scGPT, GEARS, PRESAGE, scLambda, CellFlow, plus four foundation-model-embedding MLP probes (Geneformer, ESM2, scGPT, GenePT embeddings). Baselines: mean, control (average of unperturbed cells), linear/PCA-ridge (from Ahlmann-Eltze et al.), additive (for combinations).
- **18 metrics**, confirmed named count, "spanning complementary objective families — direct reconstruction error, reference-based delta metrics and retrieval-based metrics." Named individually in text: MSE, MAE, Pearson(Δctrl), Pearson(Δpert), WMSE (weighted MSE), weighted R²_Δ, NIR (normalized inverse rank). The remaining ~11 of 18 are not individually named in the Main text (Methods/Supplementary Table likely needed; not fetched this pass).
- **Headline result, exact wording:** "on every metric, at least one deep learning model outperforms all baselines" (Replogle22 K562, per Supplementary Table 2, not independently fetched). For the unseen-combination task on Norman19, the additive baseline "captured ~88% of the ideal performance gap in the best-calibrated metrics," and deep-learning models "outperformed it on six of 18 metrics"; on Wessels23 (a separate, higher-combinatorial-coverage dataset — 157 unique pairs vs Norman19's 124, 10.3% vs 0.63% of combination space trained on), PRESAGE exceeded the additive baseline "on 15 of 18 metrics."
- **Scope limit, stated by the paper itself, directly relevant to this use case's cross-cell-context exclusion:** "we focused on the unseen genetic perturbation and unseen combination prediction tasks, leaving the unseen context task (predicting responses in unobserved cell types, donors or conditions) unaddressed." This paper therefore does **not** bear on cross-cell-type/context transfer at all — it must not be cited for that question.
- **Example named-model numeric spot-values given in Main text (not a full table — quoted exactly, not rounded further):** scGPT WMSE in Replogle22 K562 = "0.027 ± 0.001"; scGPT Pearson(Δctrl) on DEGs = "0.367 ± 0.009"; scGPT Pearson(Δpert) across all genes = "0.081 ± 0.003, barely above the mean baseline" — illustrating the paper's own caution against treating any single metric as definitive.

This is recorded as a found textual artifact, not resolved or corrected by this pass.

### 3. PertEval-scFM — Wenteler et al., *Proceedings of the 42nd ICML*, PMLR vol. 267, pp. 66633–66677 (2025).

**Access status: genuinely open, full PDF retrieved and read in full via local extraction** (`curl` + `pdftotext -layout`; PMLR/GitHub-hosted PDF, no authentication). Cached: `perteval_full.pdf` (2,670,617 bytes), `perteval_full.txt` (extracted text), fetched/extracted 2026-10-07T11:22Z. Official code: `github.com/aaronwtr/PertEval`, confirmed directly in the paper's own text ("Source code and documentation can be found at: https://github.com/aaronwtr/PertEval").

- **Datasets, exact counts from the paper's own Appendix tables:**
  - Norman et al. (2019), K562, CRISPRa: 196 total perturbations (105 single-gene, 91 double-gene), ~12,000 control cells, ~110,000 total cells, 10x Genomics Chromium, 20,000+ genes measured.
  - Replogle et al. (2022), K562 and RPE1, CRISPRi: 4,452 total perturbations (2,058 K562, 2,394 RPE1), ~310,000 cells (K562), ~250,000 cells (RPE1), NovaSeq 6000/Ultima Genomics.
- **Preprocessing:** top 2,000 highly variable genes selected per dataset; top 20 DEGs per perturbation also used for a separate evaluation (Table 3). Pseudobulk control features built from 500 randomly sampled control cells, averaged, repeated `n_p` times.
- **Models compared:** GEARS (official implementation, **only the train-test split modified** to align with SPECTRA, other parameters at default values, **trained from scratch without using pretrained weights** — a genuine NPPM comparator, not a foundation-model embedding); an MLP baseline whose input ZGE = Xc ⊕ Gc (Eq. 3) concatenates log-normalized raw control expression with a gene co-expression matrix for the perturbed gene(s) — not raw expression alone; a Mean baseline, defined by the paper's own Section 2.2 text as the predicted effect = "the mean gene expression of all cells in the same context" minus the cell's own expression (δ̂ = X̄c − Xc) — the text does not state whether this population is restricted to control cells or to training cells, and this ambiguity is preserved, not resolved; and five zero-shot foundation-model embedding probes: Geneformer, scBERT, scFoundation, scGPT, UCE.
- **Scored target, exact definition (Eq. 5):** all compared models are scored on the perturbation-effect delta := P − Xc (P = perturbed expression matrix, Xc = control expression), which the paper's own text calls the "log fold change perturbation effect" — **not raw post-perturbation expression**, and a different metric construction from GEARS Supplementary Table 6's own Pearson-ΔExpression metric; the two must not be conflated. Input budgets are not identical across the three intaken configurations (GEARS's graph-based architecture vs. the MLP baseline's Xc⊕Gc features vs. the Mean baseline's non-learned context mean), and this protocol does not establish forecasting for unseen cells, donors, or cell-line/context transfer — only generalisation to unseen perturbations under SPECTRA's distribution-shift splits.
- **Independently pinned code observation (Mean baseline, dated separately from the paper's own text):** the GitHub repository's `src/models/components/predictors.py`, class `MeanExpression.forward`, at main commit `ce48c8b998901c8f8b6275114caac3a4d8543c0b` (LICENSE blob `8f7d6532772dcd06951192fe441e00d02e0848c2`), computes `torch.mean(x[:, :x.shape[1]//2], dim=0)` over whatever batch of inputs is passed at call time, then returns that batch mean minus `x[:, :x.shape[1]//2]` — a batch-mean of the first half of the input feature dimension, not confirmably "all cells in the same context" as the paper's text states. This code observation and the paper's stated definition are kept as two separate, dated facts, not merged; the code commit's correspondence to the exact revision that produced the published Table 1/2 run is unverified. Cached: `workbench/genetic-perturbation-20261007/codex-perteval-src-models-components-predictors.py` (1,341 bytes, SHA-256 `fc1f39877c143beae50dcc51e0fc0933b460d61f1c18594db7b2424465355df9`, fetched 2026-10-07T11:35:14.743899Z).
- **Splits/distribution-shift mechanism:** SPECTRA (a spectral-graph-theory method, Ektefaie et al. 2024) generates train/test splits at controlled "sparsification probabilities" S0.1 through S0.7 (increasing difficulty / decreasing train-test similarity), explicitly designed to control cross-split overlap and model generalisation under distribution shift — a different, independently-sourced splitting mechanism from both GEARS's own Table 6 split and Systema's/Miller et al.'s held-out-perturbation splits. This must be kept separate, not merged, with either.
- **Metrics:** MSE at each sparsification level S0.1–S0.7 (scoring the delta=P−Xc target above); **AUSPC** (Area Under the SPECTRA Performance Curve, the trapezoidal-rule integral of MSE over sparsification level s, lower is better); **ΔAUSPC = AUSPC(baseline) − AUSPC(evaluated model)** — the negative-control baseline's AUSPC minus the model's own AUSPC; since lower AUSPC is better, a positive ΔAUSPC means the model's AUSPC is lower (better) than the baseline's, so positive favors the model. The trapezoidal integral itself is implemented as `np.trapz(ϕ, s)` over exactly the seven printed sparsification points s ∈ {0.1, 0.2, ..., 0.7} (Algorithm 1) — not a ROC-AUC, accuracy, or rank-based score; the source's own formal integral (Eq. 7) is written with a lower bound of 0, while the numerical approximation (Eq. F2/Algorithm 1) sums only over the seven printed points starting at s=0.1 — this is preserved exactly, not silently reconciled.
- **Uncertainty, author-reported and internally compatible (not a conflict):** the source describes AUSPC's error bars as a **standard error** (main-text Figure 2 caption: "Average AUSPC... with standard error bars") and separately gives its own **propagation formula** (Appendix F.2, Eqs. F3–F5: σ² = Σᵢ(d/2)²σ²_ϕᵢ, d=0.1 the fixed step size) deriving that standard error from each split's own MSE uncertainty. These two author statements describe the same quantity and are not contradictory — a propagated quantity can correctly be called a standard error. This is recorded as the authors' own reported, propagated uncertainty; the F3–F5 derivation is the authors' own formula and its mathematical correctness is not independently validated here, and it is not read as an independently resampled model-seed standard deviation or a confidence interval. The underlying per-split uncertainty is attributed by the source to **triplicate experiments per model**, stated in **Appendix I's Figure I1 caption** ("Experiments were carried out in triplicate for each model") — not in the main-text Figure 2 region. Figure I1's own caption separately states **"8 train-test splits of increasing difficulty"** for this same Norman single-gene evaluation, while Table 1 prints **seven** S-columns (S0.1–S0.7) and Appendix F.2 describes the grid as spanning 0.1 to 0.7. ### 4. AmbiModBench — Huang, Yang, Yu, Chen & Li, arXiv:2609.32527v1, submitted 2026-09-26.

**Access status: full HTML Methods/Experiments/Appendix read in full** via `arxiv.org/html/2609.32527v1` (no access limit). Cached: `ambimodbench-arxiv-abs-20261007T111719Z.html` (abstract page) and `ambimodbench-fullhtml-20261007T112717Z.html` (full HTML body, fetched and byte-verified at 2026-10-07T11:27:17Z, spot-checked to contain genuine article content).

- **Correction (independently identified by a concurrent Codex review, verified against the full HTML in this pass): this paper evaluates SIX predictors, not "no deep-learning model" as an earlier pass of this dossier incorrectly stated.** Exact list, quoted: "a constant response, nearest embedding, ridge, radial-basis-function kernel ridge and a spectrally normalized response network. The training mean supplies the evaluation reference." I.e.: (1) training mean (the evaluation reference/negative control), (2) zero/constant-response predictor, (3) nearest-embedding predictor, (4) ridge, (5) RBF kernel ridge, (6) a **spectrally-normalized neural response network** — "the only neural predictor in the suite" (four spectrally-normalized linear layers, hidden width 256, ReLU activations, AdamW). It does not evaluate GEARS, CPA, or PerturBench-class dedicated perturbation models, but it is not correct to say it evaluates no deep-learning model at all.
- **The neural response network's own result, quoted exactly:** "does not separate from the reference in any of the three datasets, with a paired interval at the primary selection narrower than the change produced by a single additional correct gene in one held-out record" — i.e. this paper's one neural predictor also fails to beat the training-mean reference, by the same direction-agreement metric used for ridge/kernel ridge below. It is trained under a single fixed configuration (not a searched hyperparameter grid, unlike ridge/kernel ridge), with a separate ablation (Table 9, three-arm, three seeds per arm) of its auxiliary response-to-embedding penalty term.

- **Task definition:** target-disjoint — the held-out target (gene) is the unit of generalisation; cellular context is fixed within each dataset (this paper does not test cross-cell-context transfer either).
- **Primary metric, exact definition (Eq. 2 in the paper):** direction-agreement score D_k, counting the fraction of the k largest-|effect| genes (by true response) whose predicted sign matches the true sign, with a zero prediction scored as half credit. Paired effect (Eq. 1): Δ̂_S = mean over (target, split) pairs of [S(ŷ, y) − S(training-mean, y)] — i.e., the metric this dossier should call "paired gain over training-mean," not "accuracy" alone.
- **"Pooled target-split records" vs. "unique targets," exact distinction confirmed from the paper's own caption:** a "record" is one (target, split) evaluation instance; a target evaluated across multiple splits contributes multiple records but counts once as a unique target. Table 3 caption, quoted: "The all-six analysis contains 1,308 records from 792 targets. The five-additional analysis contains 1,090 records from 724 targets" — these two denominators (records vs. targets) must be kept separate and neither substituted for the other if this source is ever cited numerically.

Percentile intervals use the 2.5th/97.5th bootstrap percentiles; pooled/cross-dataset summaries use 10,000 draws (seed 20260915), individual-split/coverage summaries use 2,000 draws (seed 1). The paper's own text states reported intervals "are per-estimate and are not treated as family-wise significance tests."
