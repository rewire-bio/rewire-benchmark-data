## Decisions a reviewer should check

1. **How a threshold is stored.** Each cell of the calibration tables is one result with the printed interval in `printed_value`. `numeric_value` is the score a variant must reach for that strength: the printed bound of a one-sided cell, or, for a two-sided interval, the end further from the next-stronger interval (the script finds the end nearest the stronger cell's bounds and takes the other). This is a reading of the printed interval, stated in `numeric_representation` and the metric definition. '–' cells are results with `numeric_value` null and an `undefined_reason`. Indeterminate intervals (Bergquist) have `numeric_value` null.
2. **New metric concept.** No existing concept holds a calibrated score interval. `likelihood-ratio` and `proportion` are reused for Table 3.
3. **Origin.** Evaluations of BayesDel, MPC, MutPred2, REVEL and VEST4 in Pejaver et al. 2022 are `author_reported`: B.-J. Feng (BayesDel, via the PERCH reference), A. O'Donnell-Luria (MPC co-author), V. Pejaver, K.A. Pagel, S.D. Mooney and P. Radivojac (MutPred2), V. Pejaver (REVEL co-author) and R. Karchin (VEST4) are authors. Overlap was checked against the reference lists only, not the tools' full author lists beyond what the references print. In Bergquist et al. 2025 only MutPred2 and REVEL are `author_reported` (Pejaver and Radivojac are authors); BayesDel and VEST4 are `independent_paper` there, although their Supporting to Strong thresholds are carried over from Pejaver et al. 2022 (stated in each evaluation's `interpretation`). The reviewer may prefer `author_reported` for those carried-over rows.
4. **Configurations.** Bergquist reuses the Pejaver configurations for BayesDel, MutPred2, REVEL and VEST4, since the scores and thresholds are the same calibration. MutPred2 configurations link to the existing MutPred family method, as the Lee 2026 MutPred2 configuration does. ESM1b links to the model `uc20260930-model-esm-1b`.
5. **PMC page hashes.** PMC article pages carry two per-request tokens, so the raw bytes change on every download. The recorded `artifact_sha256` is the hash after blanking them (`extract/pmc_html.py`); the raw hash of this retrieval is in `hash_scope`. Bergquist et al. 2025 was re-downloaded and matched after normalisation.
6. **DAMpred's source.** The AVE list cites Gao, Zhou and Skolnick 2015 (Structure), which does not describe DAMpred. The record cites Quan et al. 2019 (J Mol Biol), which does.
7. **Repository READMEs as primary sources.** PromoterAI and LOL-EVE rest on official README files pinned to a commit, because the Science paper is not open access and the LOL-EVE paper was not found. ESGMM rests on its CC BY preprint.
8. **Model or method.** GPN-Star and LOL-EVE (genomic language models) and PromoterAI, ExPecto and CpGenie (deep networks trained on genomic profiles) are `model` records; the others are `method` records, as in the first pass.
9. **Relevance.** For #151, the two calibration tables and the developer-threshold likelihood ratios are `direct`; everything else is `proxy`, including the gnomAD over-calling fraction, the Livesey and Marsh and ProteinGym rankings (discrimination, not evidence strength), the BRCA1 MAVE study and the two ENIGMA generic-BayesDel protocols (single-gene checks). For #152, the ProteinGym zero-shot DMS track and the five Livesey and Marsh DMS strata are `direct`.
10. **Old or unavailable tools.** timini commented on #153: "please do not add anything too old or outdated or bad". All retrievable tools were recorded; `coverage.json` gives each one's publication year and a single access probe so the reviewer can exclude old or unavailable ones (for example SAAPpred 2013, EA and GWAVA 2014, PaPI 2015, ENTPRISE, VIPUR and UMD-Predictor 2016, CpGenie 2017). EA is the exception that carries results.
