## What this search did not cover

- **No systematic coverage.** Three Europe PMC queries were run; each returned 46 to 169 hits and only the first 30 by relevance were screened, on title. No arXiv, bioRxiv, PubMed or Crossref search was run directly, and no citation chase from any benchmark paper was done.
- **The single-target literature.** Query s3 surfaced a large population of one-off papers (Francisella virulence factor, deltacoronavirus, Nipah/Langya/Measles RBDs, GDF15-GFRAL, RH5 malaria, canine and human TNF-alpha, HSP90, CCL25, BRD3) that each report a hit rate for one target with its own assay. None were retrieved. Taken together they are the largest untapped pool of measured outcomes, and also the place where publication bias will be worst.
- **Antibody and nanobody design** was reached only through Bennett et al. 2025. No query used antibody or nanobody design terms on their own.
- **No non-ML baseline search.** No search was run for directed evolution, library display or grafting arms on the same targets. Not one of the nine candidates runs such an arm, so the comparator #176 asks for does not exist in the evidence found; establishing it would need a separate pass over the display and directed-evolution literature.
- **Named methods not found with measured outcomes in this pass:** Chroma, ProteinGenerator, EvoBind, RFdiffusion2. RFdiffusion3, BindEnergyCraft and BoltzGen appear in the excluded Junker and Schoeder and Nanda comparisons with in-silico endpoints only.
- **Vendor and leaderboard channels.** Only the Adaptyv round 2 README was read. Model cards, Hugging Face design releases (BoltzGen points to `boltzgen/adaptyv_data1`) and any live leaderboard were not checked.
- **Licences and access terms** were not read for any source, and no source bytes were hashed or archived.
- **Excluded as in-silico only**, and recorded as gaps rather than absence: Junker and Schoeder 2026 (PLOS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0355549), which is the most careful cross-method comparison found and has no wet-lab arm, and Nanda 2026 (doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-11106209/v1) on amyloid fibrils. Amyloid fibrils and GPCR peptide targets are target classes for which no measured binder-design outcome was found at all.

## Can the decision be answered from published evidence?

Partly, and not in the form the decision asks for.

What exists is nine sources with real measured outcomes, locatable tables, and in several cases published per-design data. What does not exist, anywhere in what was found, is a controlled comparison: no paper runs two design methods and a conventional comparator on the same targets, with the same expression and assay pipeline and the same hit definition. The three partial exceptions each fail a different control. AlphaProteo runs RFdiffusion in its own assay, but the authors built AlphaProteo and the hit threshold is a display signal, not an affinity. The Adaptyv competition holds the target and assay fixed across many methods, but the methods are self-declared by entrants, entrants chose how much effort to spend, and the per-method numbers sit in figures that were not read. PXDesign overlaps AlphaProteo's targets deliberately, but its baseline column is copied from publications and partly adjusted by its own authors.

Three structural problems limit what any amount of further extraction can deliver. Hit definitions are not commensurable: a yeast-display signal above 0.2, a BLI KD below 1000 nM, an SPR response at half the positive control, and "nM with an HSA counter-screen" are four different endpoints, and the hit rate moves by tens of percentage points depending on which is used. Denominators are small and chosen: BindCraft's 46.3% average rests on 7 to 53 designs per target, and the designs tested were the top-ranked ones by the authors' own filter, so the design and selection steps are coupled exactly as #176 warns. And the spread within a single method across targets (AlphaProteo 0% to 88%, BoltzGen 3/9 novel targets with nothing, Bennett 0% to 2% for antibodies, BindCraft 10% to 100%) is wider than any plausible difference between methods, so a method ranking built from these numbers would mostly be measuring which targets each group chose.

Judgement: the use case can be defined and populated with honest direct evidence for individual method-target pairs, and that is worth doing, because the per-target spread and the antibody-versus-miniprotein gap are themselves the finding a user needs. It cannot currently answer "which workflow should I use", and the evidence limits should say so in those words. The highest-value next steps are the Adaptyv data packages and figures, which are the only same-target same-assay cross-method measurements found, and the ProtDBench retrieval, which is the only candidate standing benchmark. Neither has been read yet, so the conclusion above is provisional on both.
