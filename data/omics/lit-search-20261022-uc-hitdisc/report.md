# Literature search report: hit discovery by virtual screening, 2026-10-22

Lane `interactions`. Proposed use case `hit-discovery-virtual-screening` (rewire-benchmark-data #177). Window to 2026-10-22. Budget 25 queries; 21 used, comprising one connectivity probe, eight searches (one of which failed) and twelve retrieval calls. Logged as `search-hitdisc-20261022-s0` to `-s13` in `data/omics/search-ledger.jsonl` and 24 decisions in `data/omics/scope-audit.jsonl`.

Bounded pass, not systematic exhaustion. No record was created and no vocabulary was added. Every retrieved artifact is a Europe PMC `fullTextXML` rendering, an arXiv API record, a GitHub API response or a mutable project page; the hashes in the ledger describe one retrieval on 2026-10-22, not immutable artifacts. The extract phase must re-pin each source against the publisher PDF or the DOI version.

**Endpoint discipline.** Only library ranking counts: enrichment of true actives at a stated fraction of the library, or a measured hit rate with its denominator. The pose and affinity arms of several sources below are excluded from the use case even where the same paper supports an included enrichment arm. In particular the Mac1 pose arm of Kim et al., the structure arms of Shen et al. and the CASF-2016 scoring and ranking protocols already in the store do not answer this decision.

## What the search did not cover

- **Coverage within the searches run.** Four of the six Europe PMC searches returned more hits than were screened: 94, 685, 355 and 166 hits with only the first 30 of each read. Between them that leaves well over 1,000 unscreened records, and the ranking Europe PMC applies is not relevance to this endpoint.
- **Only one arXiv query and no native preprint search.** bioRxiv and ChemRxiv were searched only through what Europe PMC indexes. The single arXiv query returned 14 records, which is implausibly low for the field. SPRINT (arXiv:2411.15418), HelixVS, AANet and Inference for Hit Enrichment Curves were seen but not retrieved.
- **No official leaderboard was retrieved.** The store already holds PLINDER, PoseBusters and the TDC molecular tasks as `discovered` records with no results, and the 2026-10-11 interactions pass recorded the same gap. This pass did not close it either.
- **No pre-2020 docking-benchmark literature.** The classical comparative docking assessments were not searched; only DUD-E, DEKOIS and the hidden-bias paper were reached, and through citations rather than by search.
- **No ligand-similarity or pharmacophore baseline arm was found.** Issue #177 asks for ligand-similarity or pharmacophore search as a pre-ML comparator. Every candidate above baselines against docking or physics instead. Lam et al. include IFP and GRIM rows on LIT-PCBA, but those values are quoted from third parties, not computed by the authors.
- **Ultra-large library campaigns are under-covered.** The one retrieved (Luttens et al.) uses machine learning as a docking accelerator, not as the ranking model. The campaigns that would answer "how many compounds must be tested" directly, and the paper on library size against hit rate, were not read.
- **No Chai-1, Chai-2 or NeuralPLexer enrichment evaluation was found.** Chai-1 appears only in the Mac1 pose arm of Kim et al., not in any screening-enrichment table. Whether such evaluations exist is unresolved, not answered.
- **Compute per compound is thinly covered.** Only Shen et al. Table 5, Agha et al. and Wang and Kireev report it, and the Boltzina speed-up is known only from its abstract.

## Judgement for the curation phase

A model-versus-docking comparison on the same screening data does exist, in more than one form, so the question the use case asks can be answered rather than only recorded as a gap. Three independent sources carry it: Shen et al. (retrospective, co-folding models against Glide and Gnina on DEKOIS 2.0, a GPCR set and a LIT-PCBA subset, with a leakage control), Kim et al. (co-folding scores against DOCK3.7 on three sets of compounds that were actually synthesised and tested, with denominators), and CACHE #3 (blinded prospective, machine-learning workflows against physics-only workflows, with a merged-library arm on a common library).

What the answer looks like matters as much as its existence, and it is not flattering to the models. Shen et al. find co-folding confidence scores beating docking on synthetic-decoy benchmarks by a wide margin, then find the advantage shrinking on the GPCR set and nearly vanishing on the LIT-PCBA subset with measured inactives (Boltz-2 min-iPAE average EF1% 9.30 against Glide SP 6.79, with EF1% = 0.00 on TP53 for both Boltz-2 variants). Kim et al. find that on tested docking hit lists the co-folding methods do no better than docking and often worse, with AF3 L-pLDDT giving no enrichment signal at all for sigma-2 and D4. CACHE #3 found the purely physics-based workflow retrieved the most hits. The one spectacular prospective hit rate, 70% from Kawano et al., comes from 40 compounds chosen after score and diversity filtering from a pre-curated 50,240-compound library with no docking arm, and the authors say plainly it is not an unbiased estimate of the 867-compound pool.

So the shape of the recordable finding is: on property-matched synthetic decoys, co-folding and machine-learning scores enrich strongly and beat docking; as the negatives get more realistic, and especially when they are compounds a docking screen already ranked highly, the advantage disappears. Two things must be recorded as limits on every number, not as footnotes. First, the LIT-PCBA leakage audit, which concludes that nearly all published results on that benchmark are undermined and that a parameter-free memorisation baseline matches state-of-the-art models. Second, the actives-to-decoys ratio and the decoy construction, which differ by more than an order of magnitude across the candidates (1:30 for DEKOIS 2.0, roughly 1:60 for DUD-E, up to 1:18,000 for LIT-PCBA ADRB2, and 1:1.3 in Wang and Kireev) and make raw enrichment factors incomparable between them.

Three things the curation phase should not do: present the Mac1 pose results or the CASF-2016 protocols already in the store as bearing on this decision; record `relative-enrichment-factor` and `normalized-enrichment-factor` as one concept before DockM8's formula has been read; or treat the Luttens hit rate as evidence about a machine-learning ranker, since its model is scored on agreement with docking.
