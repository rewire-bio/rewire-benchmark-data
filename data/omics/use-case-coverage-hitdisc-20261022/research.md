# Research notes

Extraction pass for the `hit-discovery-virtual-screening` use case (rewire-benchmark-data #177),
2026-10-22. Built by a Claude extraction agent; no human review claimed. Nothing in this batch is
reviewed: every record has status `needs_review` and every `review` block says the value was
extracted and not checked.

Scope was set by the literature-search report in
`data/omics/lit-search-20261022-uc-hitdisc/report.md`, which named the candidates, their tables,
their decoy construction and their leakage controls. This pass extracted the first four candidates
on that list in priority order and registered the benchmark primaries. It did not reach the rest.

## The finding the numbers support

Stated only as far as the extracted results carry it. The draft summary claim
(`hitdisc-20261022-use-case-summary`) is the same account with every figure traceable to a result.

On property-matched synthetic decoys the co-folding confidence scores enrich strongly and beat the
docking arms. On DEKOIS 2.0 (79 targets, 40 actives and 1,200 ZINC decoys each, 1:30) AF3 min-iPAE
reaches a mean EF1% of 23.15 against 12.01 for the quoted Glide SP row and 5.16 for Gnina Vina.

As the negatives become more realistic the margin shrinks. On GPCRrecent (16 targets with post-2022
structures, decoys generated by TocoDecoy at 1:50) Boltz-2 min-iPAE gives a mean EF1% of 13.96
against 5.02 for Glide SP, and every value is far below its DEKOIS 2.0 counterpart. On the five
LIT-PCBA targets whose inactives were measured, the averaged EF1% is 9.30 for Boltz-2 min-iPAE
against 6.79 for Glide SP, with NEF1% 0.100 against 0.079, and on TP53 both Boltz-2 scores give
EF1% 0.00 while Protenix ipTM gives 5.00 and Glide SP 3.75.

Where the negatives are compounds a docking screen already ranked highly and every compound was
synthesised and assayed, the ordering reverses on two of three targets: AmpC AUC 76.5 for DOCK3.7
against 68.1 for Boltz-2 pIC50 and 60.5 for AF3 L-pLDDT; dopamine D4 indistinguishable for docking
and Boltz-2 (both about 71) with AF3 L-pLDDT worse than random at 46.4; sigma-2 the one case where a
co-folding score leads, Boltz-2 pIC50 83.8 against DOCK3.7's 78.8.

In the one blinded prospective challenge, WF1696, which the article describes as a purely
physics-based workflow, captured 2 of the 4 Tier 1 and 9 of the 25 Tier 2 confirmed hits from 99
predicted actives on the merged library, which the authors report as significantly more hits than
any other workflow.

Every LIT-PCBA number above carries the leakage audit's finding that a parameter-free memorisation
baseline matches or exceeds published deep-learning results on that benchmark.

## Decisions a reviewer should check

1. **The actives-to-decoys ratio is recorded on every protocol and dataset, and named in every
   judgement's limitations.** DEKOIS 2.0 is 1:30, GPCRrecent 1:50, the five LIT-PCBA targets range
   from about 1:43 to about 1:200 (and LIT-PCBA ADRB2, which was not screened, is about 1:18,000),
   DUD-E is 50 decoys per clustered ligand. Raw enrichment factors are therefore not comparable
   across protocols, and `COMMON_CONSTRAINTS` in the extractor says so on all twelve judgements.
2. **Retrospective and prospective are recorded as different things.** Each evaluation carries
   `retrospective`, each protocol says so in `scope_note`, and the judgements separate the
   retrospective enrichment groups from the prospective ones.
3. **Pose and affinity arms are excluded on endpoint grounds.** Shen et al. Table 6, the Kim et al.
   Mac1 pose benchmark and the CASF-2016 protocols already in the store are not presented as bearing
   on this decision; every judgement's constraints say not to read them together with these results.
4. **`normalized-enrichment-factor` and `relative-enrichment-factor` are not merged.** The NEF
   concept quotes Shen et al. Table 4 footnote a verbatim and its scope note says DockM8's REF
   formula has not been read and must not be merged with it. REF was not added, because no cell here
   uses it.
5. **`hit-rate` was not added to the vocabulary.** No source extracted in this pass prints a hit
   rate. CACHE #3 prints compounds tested and confirmed hits as separate counts, and defines its own
   hit rate as confirmed actives divided by compounds predicted active, which is the merged-library
   denominator, not the own-selection one. This batch records the numerator and the denominator as
   printed counts and does not divide them. Kawano et al.'s 70% was not extracted.
6. **The literature-search report's CACHE #3 figure reference is wrong for the published version.**
   The report describes "Figure 5b (per-workflow hit rate, defined in the legend as confirmed
   actives divided by compounds predicted active)". In the published JCIM article the relevant
   figure is **Figure 6**: panel a is the score of the best Round 1 hit, panel b is the aggregated
   score of all Round 1 hits, and panel c is the normalised Tier 1 and Tier 2 counts on the merged
   library, with the "confirmed actives divided by compounds predicted active" wording belonging to
   panel c. So CACHE #3 publishes no per-workflow hit rate, and Figure 6b is an activity score, not
   a rate. The values behind all three panels are in sheet S8, which was extracted.
7. **Luttens et al. and Kawano et al. were not extracted**, so neither caveat the brief asked for
   had to be written into a result. If a later pass extracts them: Luttens et al.'s model is scored
   on agreement with docking, so it is not evidence about a machine-learning ranker, and Kawano et
   al.'s 70% comes from 40 compounds chosen after score and diversity filtering from a pre-curated
   library, which the authors say is not an unbiased estimate.
8. **One evaluation per configuration and protocol, with per-target cells distinguished by
   `metric_qualifier`.** Shen et al. Tables 4 and 5 print per-target columns. Rather than create an
   evaluation per target, this batch keeps one evaluation per arm on the multi-target dataset and
   distinguishes the per-target and averaged cells by `metric_qualifier` and `source_locator`; the
   evaluation's `comparison.aggregation` says "per target, and the average over the five targets".
   The five LIT-PCBA targets also exist as `dataset_subset` records for their counts. A reviewer may
   prefer per-target evaluations linked to those subsets.
9. **Table 2 reprints three values from Table 1.** The Protenix ipTM row and the AF3 ipTM and
   Min-iPAE rows of Shen et al. Table 2 are the same numbers as in Table 1. They are kept as results
   on the rescoring protocol because they are printed cells of a table extracted in full, and each
   carries a `source_warnings` entry saying it is the co-folding confidence score, not a rescoring
   arm, and that the same value is printed in Table 1.
10. **Eight of Table 1's 24 arms are quoted from other papers.** Footnotes b to f attribute Glide SP
    and Surflex-Dock to ref. 38, GOLD CHEMPLP and LeDock to ref. 39, TankBind and KarmaDock to ref.
    34, CarsiDock to ref. 35 and SurfDock to ref. 36. Those evaluations have origin
    `paper_compilation` and a `reference` note. None of those references was retrieved. The Gnina
    rows are **not** footnoted and the Methods list refs 34 to 39 (not Gnina's ref. 32) as the
    quoted baselines, so the Gnina rows are the authors' own runs and have origin `author_reported`.
11. **SurfDock's row was computed on 81 targets, not 79.** Footnote f says the indicators were
    calculated on all 81 targets because the per-target labels were unavailable. That evaluation's
    `comparison.population` and each of its results' `scope_note` say so.
12. **CACHE #3 workflows are configuration records with no parent entity.** Their computational
    steps appear only in Figures 2, 7 and 8, which this batch does not read, so there is no source
    from which to describe each workflow as an entity. Eleven of the Shen et al. comparators
    (RTMScore, PLANET, PIGNet2, IGModel, GOLD CHEMPLP, LeDock, Surflex-Dock, TankBind, KarmaDock,
    CarsiDock, SurfDock) are configurations without a parent entity for the same reason: their
    primary papers were not retrieved. Glide, Gnina, DOCK3.7 and TocoDecoy do have `method` records,
    because the sources describe what each does.
13. **Four CACHE workflows have no merged-library values.** Workflows 1694, 1695, 1716 and 1717
    have blank cells in columns L to T of sheet S8. No result is written for them and no
    merged-library evaluation exists; the absence is recorded in the protocol's limitations and in
    `coverage.json`, never as zero. The same applies to the carboxylic-acid-penalty column, which
    carries a value for one workflow and is blank for the rest; the blanks are not recorded as a
    value because neither the sheet nor the article says whether a blank means no penalty.
14. **Sheet S8's first column has no header.** Its 23 values are a permutation of 1 to 23, so it is
    almost certainly a rank, but nothing says what it ranks. It is recorded as a single
    `tables_not_extracted` claim listing the whole column rather than as 23 results with an invented
    metric. The sheet's final column also has no header; its values equal the sum of the two
    preceding normalised columns, and each of those results says so.
15. **The LIT-PCBA totals conflict between sources.** The official page gives 7,761 actives and
    382,674 unique inactives; Shen et al. give 10,030 actives and 2,798,737 inactives. Neither
    reconciles the other. This is recorded as an `evidence_concerns` entry on the project page's
    source record and as a limitation on the LIT-PCBA benchmark record. It is not resolved.
16. **Relevance assignments.** The three Kim et al. hit lists and both CACHE #3 arms are `direct`:
    the compounds were synthesised and assayed and the labels are measurements. Everything
    retrospective is `proxy`, including the LIT-PCBA arm despite its measured inactives, because the
    compounds come from past campaigns and the benchmark's splits are compromised. The compute
    protocol is `proxy`: it measures cost, not ranking, but issue #177 asks for compute per compound
    on every result. The CACHE own-selection arm is the weakest of the `direct` five, because each
    workflow chose its own compounds; that confound is its first limitation.
17. **The use case names no baseline claim.** Issue #177 asks for docking **and** ligand-similarity
    or pharmacophore search as the pre-machine-learning comparator. Docking baselines are scored on
    every Shen et al. protocol and on all three Kim et al. lists, but no ligand-similarity or
    pharmacophore arm is scored anywhere in this batch, so a `baseline` claim naming both would
    overstate what is here. The gap is recorded in the use case's `evidence_gaps` and in every
    judgement's limitations, and a baseline claim is left to a later pass.

## What this pass did not reach

Listed in full in `coverage.json` under `gaps`. The ones that matter most for the decision:

- No ligand-similarity or pharmacophore baseline anywhere.
- No per-workflow hit rate from any source, and no hit-rate metric concept.
- The Kim et al. hit-rate curves and early-enrichment values, which are figures.
- DockM8 (relative enrichment on DEKOIS 2.0, DUD-E and LIT-PCBA with an AVE-split check) and Lam et
  al. 2024 (sequence-only screening with a homology-removed zero-shot arm), both of which carry
  docking baselines on the same data and were the next two candidates in priority order.
- Kawano et al.'s prospective 70% hit rate, Boltzina and the Boltz-2 affinity-head fine-tuning work
  on MF-PCBA, Agha et al.'s MolDockLab, Luttens et al. and Wang and Kireev.
- CACHE #1 and #2, so nothing shows whether the CACHE #3 ordering holds on another target.
- The paper relating library size and number tested to hit rate, which is the second half of the use
  case's question.
- No compute figure for any docking tool, so the cost comparison is one-sided.
